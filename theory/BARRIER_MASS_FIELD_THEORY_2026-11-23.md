## 0. Bottom line

**Question (T64 §8).** Can a local, readable record sit near vacuum energy behind a barrier much larger than its own mass-energy? T64 needed the answer "no" in the form A1, \(B\le\beta E_{\rm rec}\), to turn the retention barrier into a gravitating-mass floor.

**Plain answer.** Half yes, half no. A barrier much larger than the record's own mass-energy: **yes**, so A1 is not a law of field theory. Near vacuum energy: **no**, for a record that is confined to a region and readable there. That second fact gives a cap on records that does not need A1 at all. It is the known Cohen–Kaplan–Nelson scaling, below area.

1. **A1 fails in the destruction channel (PROVED in-model, with KNOWN Standard Model inputs).** Baryon number in the electroweak theory changes only by crossing the sphaleron, \(E_{\rm sph}=(4\pi v/g)\,B(\lambda/g^2)\), between 7.2 and 12.9 TeV for any Higgs coupling and about 9.0 TeV at the measured one. The lightest record that sphaleron processes can erase (\(\Delta B=\Delta L=3\)) weighs about 2.809 GeV (a helium-3 atom plus a neutrino). So \(\beta=B/E_{\rm rec}\) is between \(2.6\times10^{3}\) and \(4.6\times10^{3}\) (\(3.2\times10^{3}\) at the measured Higgs mass), and in a Yang–Mills–Higgs toy it grows without bound as the Yukawa coupling goes to zero. Exactly conserved charges (electric charge, a monopole's topological charge) have no local destruction channel at all, so their \(\beta\) is infinite there.
2. **Readability removes the vacuum-energy cases (PROVED in-model).** Pure gauge vacua with different Chern–Simons number sit at vacuum energy, but they are related by a large gauge transformation, so no gauge-invariant observable can tell them apart (Lemma G). They are not records. A region sitting in one of two degenerate vacua is readable at zero energy, but it holds one bit. Many such bits need walls, and a closed wall in the continuum shrinks with no barrier (Lemma W). The only readable trace of a Chern–Simons change is the fermion charge that the anomaly creates, and that charge has rest mass.
3. **A local record also has a transport channel (PROVED, Lemma C).** A record in a region is erased just as well by its carrier leaving as by its carrier being destroyed. The effective barrier is the smallest over channels, so \(\beta_{\rm eff}=\min(\beta_{\rm destroy},\beta_{\rm transport})\). The sphaleron and conservation laws raise only \(\beta_{\rm destroy}\). Whether \(\beta_{\rm transport}\) can be \(\gg1\) is where A1 now lives (**OPEN**). A field-energy-only sketch gives 18.1 for a 1 eV, 1 nm electrostatic well, and that rises to at most \(1.96\times10^{-6}\) once a single electron of source mass is counted (SKETCH, not a counterexample).
4. **The cap does not need A1 (PROVED in-model, given the hoop input and A2).** \(N\) carriers confined to a ball of radius \(R\), in orthonormal one-particle states (fermions, or one bosonic mode per record), have total energy at least \(C_B N^{4/3}\hbar c/R\) with \(C_B=\tfrac34(9\pi/2)^{1/3}=1.8135\) (Lemma L, the Berezin bathtub argument). With the hoop limit and an input A2 that interactions do not cancel this localization energy, \(N\le\bigl(R^2/(2C_B\ell_p^2)\bigr)^{3/4}=\bigl(A/(45.58\,\ell_p^2)\bigr)^{3/4}\): \(5.86\times10^{51}\) at \(R=1\) m, 18.3 orders of magnitude below \(A/(4\ell_p^2)\). This is the 't Hooft / Cohen–Kaplan–Nelson \(A^{3/4}\) count (KNOWN), here made exact for one-carrier records. It closes T63's self-correction loophole for every \(\beta\), and by a known route.
5. **Where T64's barrier floor still matters (ELEMENTARY).** The two caps are \(\min\bigl(\beta Rc^4/(2GkT\ln),\ N_{\rm loc}\bigr)\). The barrier one binds only for \(\beta<\beta_c=kT\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)\,N_{\rm loc}/E_{\max}\), which is \(2.63\times10^{-11}\) at 1 m and 300 K and grows as \(T R^{1/2}\). T64's magnetic grain (\(\beta=3.37\times10^{-16}\)) is in the barrier regime; its carbon example (\(8.94\times10^{-11}\)) is already in the localization regime.
6. **Secondary: Jacobson (ELEMENTARY).** An equality form of the record-cost floor, \(\delta Q=T\,\delta S_{\rm rec}\), is the Clausius relation with record entropy in place of horizon entropy. It says nothing about how \(\delta S\) scales with area, so it cannot replace Jacobson's area input. If one feeds in the record count this note does prove, \(S\propto A^{3/4}\), then \(\eta=dS/dA\propto A^{-1/4}\) depends on the patch size and has no finite local limit. So readable records are not the degrees of freedom Jacobson's argument counts.

**What the cost adds.** The retention requirement \(kT\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)\) per record, which binds only in low-\(\beta\) media. It does not add the \(A^{3/4}\) count (that comes from \(\hbar\), the hoop input with \(G\), and the localization of carriers), nor an area, nor \(1/4\). This is the model's implication, stated plainly. Nothing here derives general relativity, quantum mechanics, or holography.

## 3. The destruction channel: A1 fails

**Setup.** A1 compares the barrier \(B\) that protects a record with \(E_{\rm rec}\), the record's share of the region's gravitating mass-energy. In the electroweak theory a configuration of net baryon number \(\Delta B=3\) (with \(\Delta L=3\), one unit per generation) can be destroyed locally only through a change of Chern–Simons number by one, whose minimal-energy path crosses the sphaleron (KNOWN, Manton 1983; Klinkhamer–Manton 1984).

**Proposition 1 (PROVED in-model, given the KNOWN inputs).** For the record "this region holds \(B=3,L=3\)" against "it holds \(B=L=0\)", the destruction-channel barrier is \(E_{\rm sph}\), and
\[
\beta_{\rm destroy}=\frac{E_{\rm sph}}{E_{\rm rec}}\in[2.6\times10^{3},\ 4.6\times10^{3}],\qquad \approx 3.2\times10^{3}\ \text{at } m_H=125\ \text{GeV}.
\]
*Inputs.* \(v=246.22\) GeV, \(m_W=80.37\) GeV, so \(g=2m_W/v=0.6528\) and \(4\pi v/g=4739.5\) GeV; \(B\in[1.52,2.72]\), giving \(E_{\rm sph}\in[7.2, 12.9]\) TeV, and \(B\approx1.9\) gives 9.0 TeV. The lightest neutral state with \(B=L=3\) is a helium-3 atom (2.809 GeV) plus a neutrino. *Proof.* Divide. The barrier is the minimax energy over paths between the two charge sectors, which is the sphaleron by its definition. ∎ The zero-temperature tunneling route is suppressed by about \(e^{-4\pi/\alpha_W}\approx10^{-161}\) (KNOWN form, 't Hooft 1976; number computed), and the thermal rate carries \(e^{-E_{\rm sph}/kT}\) (Kuzmin et al. 1985). At 300 K a one-year, \(10^{-9}\) record needs \(B^*=1.70\) eV (T64's numbers, \(\ln=65.62\)), so this channel overshoots the need by a factor \(5.3\times10^{12}\).

**Parametric form (PROVED in-model, a toy).** In a Yang–Mills–Higgs theory whose chiral fermions get mass only from a Yukawa coupling \(y\), \(m_f=yv/\sqrt2\), a record of \(n\) fermions has \(\beta_{\rm destroy}=4\sqrt2\,\pi B/(g\,y\,n)\), which is unbounded as \(y\to0\). So no universal \(\beta\) exists in the destruction channel.

**Exactly conserved charges.** For electric charge or a monopole's topological charge ('t Hooft 1974) the charge in a region changes only by flux through its boundary. There is no local destruction channel, and \(\beta_{\rm destroy}=\infty\) trivially. This is why the destruction channel alone cannot decide A1 (§5).

## 4. Readability: what sits at vacuum energy is not a record, or not many

**Lemma G (PROVED).** If \(|n{+}1\rangle=U|n\rangle\) for a gauge transformation \(U\) and \(O\) is gauge invariant (\([O,U]=0\)), then \(\langle n{+}1|O|n{+}1\rangle=\langle n|U^\dagger OU|n\rangle=\langle n|O|n\rangle\). *Proof.* One line. ∎ So the pure vacua \(|n\rangle\) of Jackiw–Rebbi and Callan–Dashen–Gross, which sit at vacuum energy behind the sphaleron, cannot be told apart by any gauge-invariant measurement, local or not. The physical trace of \(\Delta N_{\rm CS}\) is the fermion charge \(\Delta B=\Delta L=n_g\Delta N_{\rm CS}\), and that charge carries rest mass, which is Proposition 1.

**Lemma W (PROVED in-model, thin-wall continuum).** Let two vacua be exactly degenerate with wall tension \(\sigma\). A record "this ball sits in the other vacuum" has energy \(E(r)=4\pi\sigma r^2\), increasing in \(r\). The path \(r\to0\) is downhill all the way, so its barrier is zero and the record erases itself. Flat parallel walls on a torus are also barrier-free to bring together (translation zero mode; SKETCH for the wall–wall force). A single region uniformly in one vacuum is readable (the order parameter is gauge invariant) and sits at zero energy behind a barrier of \(2\sigma L^2\) on a periodic box of side \(L\), so \(\beta=\infty\) for it. But it is one bit, and many bits need walls, which Lemma W erases unless something pins them. Pinning is the transport channel of the pinning matter. T64's sketch (\(\beta\sim1\) for a random domain pattern) is replaced by this: with no pinning, zero barrier. The repulsive active mass of planar walls (Vilenkin 1983; Ipser–Sikivie 1984) does not enter, because walls give no many-record example to begin with.

**Solitons.** Stable three-dimensional solitons need gauge fields or higher-derivative terms (Derrick 1964). Their charge is topological and locally conserved, so they fall under the exactly-conserved case: \(\beta_{\rm destroy}=\infty\), and erasure in a region happens only by transport.

## 5. The transport channel: where A1 now lives

**Lemma C (PROVED in-model).** If a record can be erased along several routes, the minimax barrier over the union of paths is the minimum of the routes' barriers. For two parallel routes with Arrhenius rates the effective barrier is within \(\ln2\) of the smaller one (script §4). So \(\beta_{\rm eff}=\min(\beta_{\rm destroy},\beta_{\rm transport})\) for a record held in a region, and every carrier held in a region has a transport route: leaving it. Proposition 1 and conserved charges therefore show that A1 fails in the destruction channel, and they leave the record's A1 to the transport channel.

**Transport (OPEN; SKETCH only).** The question becomes: can a carrier be held in a region by a barrier \(U\) much larger than the gravitating energy of the carrier plus whatever holds it? Two estimates, neither a theorem. Counting only field energy, an electrostatic well of depth \(U\) and size \(\ell\) stores about \(\varepsilon_0 (U/e)^2\ell\), so \(\beta\approx4\pi\alpha\hbar c/(U\ell)=18.1\) at \(U=1\) eV, \(\ell=1\) nm. Counting a single electron as the source, \(\beta\le1\ \text{eV}/511\ \text{keV}=1.96\times10^{-6}\). Ordinary matter sits at the second figure or below (T64 §4). No theorem found bounds \(\beta_{\rm transport}\) in general, and no counterexample with \(\beta_{\rm transport}\gg1\) is exhibited here.

## 6. A cap that does not need A1

**Lemma L (PROVED; Berezin's argument applied to \(|p|\)).** Let \(\phi_1,\dots,\phi_N\) be orthonormal one-particle states supported in a ball \(\Omega\) of radius \(R\), each carrying \(g\) internal states at most. Then
\[
\sum_{j=1}^N\langle\phi_j,\ c|p|\,\phi_j\rangle\ \ge\ C_B\,g^{-1/3}\,N^{4/3}\,\frac{\hbar c}{R},\qquad C_B=\tfrac34\bigl(\tfrac{9\pi}{2}\bigr)^{1/3}=1.8135 .
\]
*Proof.* Put \(\rho(k)=\sum_j|\hat\phi_j(k)|^2/(2\pi)^3\). Bessel's inequality against the plane wave restricted to \(\Omega\) gives \(0\le\rho(k)\le g|\Omega|/(2\pi)^3\), and \(\int\rho=N\). The sum equals \(\hbar c\int|k|\rho(k)\,dk\), which, with \(|k|\) increasing, is smallest when \(\rho\) fills the ball \(|k|\le k_F\) at its ceiling (bathtub principle). That gives \(\tfrac34N\hbar ck_F\) with \(k_F=(6\pi^2N/(g|\Omega|))^{1/3}\), and \(|\Omega|=\tfrac43\pi R^3\). ∎ It applies to any \(N\)-fermion state, through its one-particle density matrix \(0\le\gamma\le1\), and to bosons in distinct modes. Massive carriers have \(E\ge c|p|\), so it holds for them too. Script §5 checks the exact discrete form on a \(14^3\) lattice (208-site ball) for every \(N\) and for random orthonormal sets.

**Inputs.** The hoop limit \(E\le Rc^4/(2G)\) (ASSUMED INPUT, as in T64). **A2 (ASSUMED INPUT):** interactions among the carriers cancel at most a fraction \(\kappa_{\rm int}<1\) of their localization energy. A2 holds with \(\kappa_{\rm int}=0\) for free carriers. For Coulomb systems it is the kind of statement stability-of-matter proofs establish for small couplings (Lieb–Yau 1988, context only). Gravity's own binding is exactly what the hoop input caps. **Readable records:** one carrier per record, in orthonormal states (automatic for fermions). Records encoded collectively, including presence/absence of a carrier in a cell (whose empty state sits at vacuum energy trivially), are covered by the next paragraph instead; A1 in T64 is read per record averaged over its states, and the empty state is why the general-encoding bound is the one that matters for such codes.

**Proposition 2 (PROVED in-model, given the hoop input, A2 and finite \(g\)).**
\[
N\ \le\ \Bigl(\frac{g^{1/3}}{1-\kappa_{\rm int}}\cdot\frac{R^2}{2C_B\ell_p^2}\Bigr)^{3/4}=\Bigl(\frac{g^{1/3}}{1-\kappa_{\rm int}}\cdot\frac{A}{45.58\,\ell_p^2}\Bigr)^{3/4}.
\]
At \(R=1\) m, \(g=1\), \(\kappa_{\rm int}=0\): \(N\le5.86\times10^{51}\), 18.3 orders below \(A/(4\ell_p^2)=1.20\times10^{70}\). The two coincide only at \(R=0.0147\,\ell_p\), far outside any field-theory scope. **General encodings** (KNOWN in form, Cohen–Kaplan–Nelson): the information retrievable from a state of mean energy \(E\) is at most its entropy, which is at most the thermal entropy at \(E\). For one free bosonic species at \(E_{\max}(1\ \text{m})\) this is \(1.32\times10^{52}\) nats (NUMERICAL, black-body formula, valid since \(kT\gg\hbar c/R\)), with the same \((R/\ell_p)^{3/2}\) scaling.

**Consequences (ELEMENTARY).**
- T63's loophole (self-correcting media holding records at zero ongoing heat, with volume scaling) closes for every \(\beta\), given the hoop input, A2, and a finite number of species. The species count is the standing weak point: the cap grows as \(g^{1/4}\), and an unbounded number of species is the known species problem of entropy bounds (Bousso 2002).
- Combined with T64, \(N\le\min\bigl(\beta Rc^4/(2GkT\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)),\ N_{\rm loc}\bigr)\). The barrier route binds only for \(\beta<\beta_c=kT\ln\,N_{\rm loc}/E_{\max}=2.63\times10^{-11}\) at 1 m, 300 K, with \(\beta_c\propto TR^{1/2}\).
- The answer to "near vacuum energy" is no for any confined, readable record: per carrier, the energy is at least the localization energy, and collectively at least \(C_BN^{4/3}\hbar c/R\).

## 7. Writing and reading these records

- **Write through the destruction channel (PROVED in-model, minimax).** Any path from \(B=0\) to \(B=3\) passes through a configuration of energy at least \(E_{\rm sph}\), so a driven write must concentrate about 9 TeV per record, in principle recoverable. Thermally, detailed balance makes the write rate as slow as the erase rate, so a record that lasts cannot be written thermally at the same temperature. Whether collisions can deliver the energy efficiently is contested (Ringwald 1990; Espinosa 1990; Tye–Wong 2015). Writing by moving existing carriers uses the transport channel instead, and the record's lifetime is then set by that channel.
- **Read.** Counting carriers in a region is an ordinary local measurement. Reading can be made reversible with no fundamental heat floor (Bennett 1982); resetting the reader costs \(kT\ln2\) per bit (Landauer 1961), as in T57/T63.

## 8. The GTAO method, run explicitly

1. **The cost, with named terms.**
   \(J=\sum_i\bigl[c_{\rm err}\,\varepsilon_i+\lambda_G\,E_{{\rm rec},i}\bigr]+J_{\rm write}+J_{\rm read}\), with the **retention-loss term** \(\varepsilon_i\approx\nu\tau e^{-B_{{\rm eff},i}/kT}\), \(B_{\rm eff}=\min(B_{\rm destroy},B_{\rm transport})\) (Lemma C); the **mass-energy term** \(E_{{\rm rec},i}\ge\max(B_{{\rm eff},i}/\beta_{\rm eff},\ m_ic^2)\) plus the **localization term** \(\sum_iE_{{\rm rec},i}\ge(1-\kappa_{\rm int})C_Bg^{-1/3}N^{4/3}\hbar c/R\) (Lemma L, A2); the **gravitational budget** \(\sum_iE_{{\rm rec},i}\le Rc^4/(2G)\) with multiplier \(\lambda_G\) (hoop input); the **write term** (at least the barrier, transiently, through the destruction channel; T63/T57 otherwise) and the **read term** (zero reversible, \(kT\ln2\) per reset).
2. **Its minimum.** Each record at \(B_{\rm eff}=B^*=kT\ln(\nu\tau/\tilde\varepsilon)\); count \(N^*=\min(\beta_{\rm eff}Rc^4/(2GkT\ln),\ \text{rest-mass cap},\ N_{\rm loc})\) (PROVED, given the inputs).
3. **Read-off design.** Barrier beyond \(B^*\) buys nothing (the sphaleron overshoots by \(5.3\times10^{12}\) at 300 K), so a strongly selected record keeper would not pay for excess barrier if it cost mass. Raise \(\beta_{\rm transport}\), not \(\beta_{\rm destroy}\), since the transport channel is the weaker one. Above \(\beta_c\), more barrier per mass stops helping; capacity is then set by carrier count and localization.
4. **Scope.** Records held in a region by thermally activated barriers, carriers with \(E\ge c|p|\), finite species, A2, quasi-static, at most marginally self-gravitating regions; the hoop limit is an input. Excluded: the tunneling regime (T64 C3), cosmological and collapsing settings, black holes (which exceed both caps; T64 §5), and any claim about the transport channel's \(\beta\).

## 9. Secondary: could an equality record-cost floor replace Clausius in Jacobson 1995?

Jacobson's inputs are \(\delta Q=T\,dS\) at the Unruh temperature and \(dS=\eta\,\delta A\) with a constant \(\eta\). An equality form of the record-cost floor (Landauer saturated) gives \(\delta Q=T\,\delta S_{\rm rec}\). That is the Clausius relation itself with \(S_{\rm rec}\) as the entropy, so it can stand in for the first input, but it says nothing about the second. The area proportionality must still come from somewhere (ELEMENTARY). Using the count this note proves for readable records, \(S_{\rm rec}\propto A^{3/4}\), gives \(\eta(A)=dS/dA\propto A^{-1/4}\): ten times larger when the patch area is \(10^{4}\) times smaller, with no finite limit as the local patches shrink, which Jacobson's construction needs (ELEMENTARY). So **no**, not without assuming area scaling. Model implication, stated plainly: the area-scaling entropy in Jacobson's argument is not a count of readable records; Jacobson motivates it by vacuum entanglement, which is a different object.

These are modest. None of them is an area law, and none is a new inequality.
