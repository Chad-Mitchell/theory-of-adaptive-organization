## Answer, in plain words

**Free particle: RESTATES-KNOWN.** If the cost is taken over a time window with a stake on where the coordinate ends up, the cheapest way to move it is at constant speed. That is the classical free-particle path, and it holds at any noise level. As the end stake grows, the path converges to the classical path between the two fixed points (`DERIVATION.md` §2). The heat spent equals the free action times \(2\gamma/M\). Every step is a published result on minimum-dissipation protocols and optimal transport, and the term that is specific to GTAO, the information heat \(kT\) per nat (`CORE.md:70`), plays no part in choosing the path (§4).

**Harmonic oscillator: NO.** A positive stake on distance from a target gives an inverted oscillator, which is imaginary-time (Euclidean) mechanics, not the real one (§3.1). The real-time oscillator needs a negative stake. Nonnegative prices are assumption A8 (`CORE.md:52`), so that is outside the cost. Even with the sign flipped, past half a period (\(\tau>\pi/\omega\)) the classical path is a saddle of the action and the cost has no minimum at all (§3.3). A cost is minimized, and real-time action is only stationary, so the two part ways exactly where the action stops being least.

**Overall: NO for an exact bridge from \(J\) to real-time least action in class PATH.** What does hold is one worked case, the free particle, and it restates known results. This is not a derivation of mechanics, and nothing here bears on quantum theory, gravity or any force.

## Does anything specific to GTAO survive?

Read against each known starting point the brief named:

- **Onsager–Machlup and Freidlin–Wentzell** (Onsager and Machlup 1953, doi `10.1103/PhysRev.91.1505`; Dürr and Bach 1978, doi `10.1007/BF01609446`; Freidlin and Wentzell, doi `10.1007/978-3-642-25847-3`; Schilder 1966, doi `10.1090/S0002-9947-1966-0201892-6`). For an overdamped diffusion with drift \(F\), the small-noise path functional is \(\frac{1}{4D}\int(\dot x-F)^2dt\), whose minimizers obey Newton's equation with potential \(-\frac12|F|^2\): Euclidean mechanics, as in §3. For \(F=0\) it is the straight line of §2. Same content, from the path probability rather than from a cost.
- **Stochastic thermodynamics.** The heat \(\dot Q\) of class PATH is Sekimoto's (doi `10.1143/PTPS.130.17`; review Seifert 2012, doi `10.1088/0034-4885/75/12/126001`). Minimum mean dissipation of a finite-time protocol is the kinetic, or Benamou–Brenier, action, with constant-velocity optimal paths (Schmiedl and Seifert 2007, doi `10.1103/PhysRevLett.98.108301`; Aurell et al. 2011, doi `10.1103/PhysRevLett.106.250601`; Benamou and Brenier 2000, doi `10.1007/s002110050002`). This is the whole of LA-FP.
- **Schrödinger bridges** (Léonard 2014, doi `10.3934/dcds.2014.34.1533`). The most likely noisy evolution between two given laws; its small-noise limit is optimal transport, whose paths are the straight lines of §2. A bridge is the information-free rival to any GTAO path claim: it selects the same mean path with no stake and no memory.
- **Nelson's stochastic mechanics and Guerra–Morato** (Nelson 1966, doi `10.1103/PhysRev.150.1079`; Guerra and Morato 1983, doi `10.1103/PhysRevD.27.1774`; Yasue 1981, doi `10.1016/0022-1236(81)90079-3`). These reach real-time quantum dynamics from a diffusion, but through a stochastic action in which the osmotic (noise) kinetic term enters with the opposite sign to the current term, which makes the action stationary rather than minimal. In \(J\)'s terms that is a negative price, the same obstacle as §3.2. This is a reading of their construction, not a result of this note.
- **Jaynes's maximum caliber** (Pressé et al. 2013, doi `10.1103/RevModPhys.85.1115`). Maximizing path entropy under constraints gives exponential path weights; with a quadratic constraint these are the Onsager–Machlup weights. It selects the same paths without a stake.
- **Entropic dynamics** (Caticha 2011, doi `10.1088/1751-8113/44/22/225303`). Dynamics from inference, with quantum theory reached by added structure (a phase, or a drift potential). This route was earlier set aside in this repo (`NOTES/rnd-2026-11-26/stepback/TRACKING_FIRST.md:99`), and nothing here revives it.
- **The free energy principle, read critically** (Friston 2010, doi `10.1038/nrn2787`). It also writes behavior as least action on a path functional. The critique that its key steps hold only under restrictive conditions, even for linear systems (Aguilera et al. 2022, doi `10.1016/j.plrev.2021.11.001`) is the same warning `theory/MASTER_EQUATION.md:11` gives for \(J\): "minimize some cost" fits any behavior unless the cost has no free prices. Repeating that move with \(J\) would add a stake \(w\) as a free price, which is the problem, not a step forward.

**What survives.** No GTAO-specific content survives as a bridge to mechanics. Two sentences survive as statements about \(J\), both close to known results: (1) because CORE's prices are nonnegative (`CORE.md:52`), any path selected by minimizing \(J\) obeys Euclidean, not real-time, mechanics; (2) in class PATH the information term changes how tightly a coordinate holds its path, never which path it takes (§4). Neither is a new theorem. Sentence 1 is the stochastic-control–Euclidean link (Fleming and Mitter 1982, doi `10.1080/17442508208833228`; Kappen 2005, doi `10.1103/PhysRevLett.95.200201`), and sentence 2 is certainty equivalence.

## Path integrals: one scope-limited paragraph (no claims)

Selection's stationary law over designs is \(\pi\propto e^{-\beta J}\) (`CORE.md:124`). A weight \(e^{-J/kT}\) over paths of class PATH is a real, positive Onsager–Machlup weight, that is, a Euclidean path integral (Feynman–Kac). Feynman's real-time sum over paths weights each path by \(e^{iS/\hbar}\) (Feynman 1948, doi `10.1103/RevModPhys.20.367`), a phase, and its classical limit picks out stationary paths, saddles included. So the analog \(J\) has is the imaginary-time one. Getting from it to the real-time sum needs a Wick rotation, which is not in \(J\), or a negative price, which `CORE.md:52` excludes. No statement about quantum theory follows. The quantum-record route stays as the README's `Q-RECORD` row states (`README.md:85`).

## Scope limits

One coordinate, linear drift, additive noise, one temperature, quadratic loss. A window cost with a terminal stake replaces the stationary rate of `CORE.md:27` (assumption S4). CORE's floor \(kT\,\dot I\) is stated for steady states or long-run averages (`CORE.md:70`); §4 uses it only to say where information enters, not as a bound on the transient. The underdamped residual route of `DERIVATION.md` §6 reproduces real-time paths for \(\tau\ne n\pi/\omega\) only because Newton's equation is assumed in the model. Lean covers the finite statements of §2, not the continuum calculus of variations or §3–§4. Self-check, not an independent review.

## Next measurement

The match is decided by the sign and by certainty equivalence, so another linear-Gaussian case cannot miss. A test that can miss needs a class where the information term reaches the path: a nonlinear world where certainty equivalence breaks, for example a double-well bridge observed through a noisy, priced channel (dual control). The preregistered question: does the \(J\)-minimizing mean path depart from the Schrödinger-bridge path (the information-free rival) by more than the resolution, with the sign of the departure fixed before the run? If it never departs, the GTAO-specific term has no hold on paths, and the least-action route should be closed in favor of other work. On the evidence here, the route to real-time mechanics through \(J\) should not be pursued further without a reason to expect a negative price or a stationary rather than minimal selection.
