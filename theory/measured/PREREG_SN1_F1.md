# SN1-F1 preregistration: a counting-noise-limited relative as the discriminating follow-up

Written Friday 20 November 2026 (commit time is the record), after the SN1 premise checks
(`SN1_RESULTS.md`) and **before looking up any swimming-statistics or input-correlation data for Vibrio ordalii**.

**What I have already seen (disclosed).** Brumley et al. 2019 (PNAS; arXiv 1905.07774) abstract: V. ordalii starts
chemotaxis when the gradient rises above sensory noise "generated by stochastic encounters with chemoattractant
molecules", i.e. counting noise, the exogenous case. A search summary reported its adaptation (methylation) time as
about 1.3 s and a gradient-estimation window of about 0.1 s. I have not seen its velocity correlation time or run
statistics.

## Why this discriminates
The E. coli miss is diagnosed as partly a store-noise effect (s = 0.31 < ½). In a relative whose binding noise is
counting noise (exogenous), Model S applies unextended and predicts λτ\* = 1/√(1+SNR) ≤ 1. If V. ordalii's adaptation
time is also several times its input correlation time (as in E. coli, λτ = 8.5), the cause is shared by both species and
is not store noise (pointing to objective, world or selection). If λτ ≤ 2, the store-noise diagnosis gains support.

## Rule
- τ: V. ordalii adaptation time as reported by Brumley et al. 2019 (with their uncertainty if given; otherwise ±30%).
- λ: 1/(correlation time of the swimming velocity along a fixed axis) for V. ordalii, from Brumley et al. 2019 or
  another open source on V. ordalii (or, failing that, on V. alginolyticus run-reverse-flick statistics, flagged as a
  proxy species); computed as given or from run durations and turning angles via the standard run-and-tumble formula.
- **PASS** iff the whole CI of λτ ≤ 2 (memory at or faster than its mode, within ×2).
- **FAIL** iff the whole CI of λτ > 3.
- Otherwise **NEITHER**. **UNTESTED** if λ is not available from an open source. **INVALID** if the source shows that
  counting noise is not the dominant noise (premise).
