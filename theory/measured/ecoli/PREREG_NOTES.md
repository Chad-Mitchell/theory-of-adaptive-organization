# Dated notes on the locked ECOLI-MEM preregistration

`PREREG.md` is locked (`LOCK.txt`, commit 6903de9) and is not edited. Notes on it are added here, dated.

**20 Nov 2026 (v2.61, after independent review 27, item M2). Missed prior art, credited.** Tjalma & ten Wolde 2024 (Phys. Rev. Research 6, 033049, doi 10.1103/PhysRevResearch.6.033049, CC BY; DOI verified on Crossref; read in full as arXiv:2402.05825v1, because the journal PDF returned 403 to curl, so the published text may differ in detail) study the same quantity with the same input statistics (Mattingly et al. 2021: τv = 1/0.86 s, σ²vx = 157 µm² s⁻²). Their objective differs from ours. The network predicts the future concentration change v(t0 + τ) (forecast interval τ = τv in their figures), and the error has two parts: a sampling error (readout counts, plus methylation noise in the receptor clusters, whose variance does not average out with τm, their Eq. 21) and a dynamical error. The optimal adaptation time comes from that trade-off. It shortens with more resources and scales approximately as 1/g with gradient steepness (their Eq. 29). With their MWC resource estimates (XT = 10⁴, RT = 8, Nr = 12), the measured τm ≈ 10 s (τm/τv ≈ 8) is "close to optimal" **in performance** for g ≲ 4 mm⁻¹ (their Fig. 3c). Their optimum scales as 1/g, so it sits near 10 s only around g ≈ 4 mm⁻¹. At the lab gradients of our locked box (0.1–0.4 mm⁻¹), their optimum is **longer** than 10 s. They call 10 s close to optimal because performance there is already near the bound ("the improvement in shallow gradients is only very minor"). The result rests on RT = 8, which the authors themselves flag as uncertain: other data suggest RT ∼ 10²–10³, and they say the small value is "conceivable" as a critical-point domain count. **Consequence here:** the ×8.5 F1/P1 FAIL stands as preregistered, but it reflects our choice of objective (estimate the current u at lead 0, with the measured noise treated as exogenous sensing noise). A published account, with a prediction objective and internal noise that does not average out, makes the measured 10 s close to optimal in performance for g ≲ 4 mm⁻¹ under RT = 8 (flagged as uncertain by its authors). Its optimum is longer than 10 s at shallower gradients. With that qualifier, it may explain the remaining factor. Note the direction: at lab gradients their optimum lies beyond the measured 10 s, not on it (corrected v2.62, review 27 R2-1). I have not reproduced their calculation. Their 1/g scaling is an analytic statement of F1's predicted direction, by a mechanism different from Vladimirov et al. 2008. It is still not a measurement.
