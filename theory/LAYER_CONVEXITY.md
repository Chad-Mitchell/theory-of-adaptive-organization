## 0. Bottom line

**The question.** N60 (CLAIM_MAP §N) says that in Model S the best number of layers is \(K^\*=1+\#\{k\ge 2: R(\varepsilon_{k-1}-\varepsilon_k)>1\}\), *if* the excess error \(\varepsilon_K=E_K/E_\infty-1\) is convex in \(K\). That from-1 formula has a \(K^\*=0\) hole; the corrected from-0 count is \(K^\star_0(R)=\#\{K\ge 1: R(\varepsilon_{K-1}-\varepsilon_K)>1\}\). Convexity was numerical only (S5: 120/120 worlds). The rhythm counterexample (`core/KEYSTONE_NOTE.md` §2) shows it fails once the world has oscillations.

**What this note proves.**

1. **Exactness (elementary).** On a finite domain, the from-0 count \(K^\star_0(R)=\#\{K\ge 1: R(\varepsilon_{K-1}-\varepsilon_K)>1\}\) equals the leftmost \(\arg\min_K J\) for all \(R\ge 0\) if the decrements \(d_K=\varepsilon_{K-1}-\varepsilon_K\) are non-increasing; the converse holds under positive, pairwise distinct decrements (more generally, distinctness with the threshold decrement positive; without that, \(d=(-2,-1)\) is non-convex yet matches for all \(R\ge 0\)). N60 as written in `core/KEYSTONE_NOTE.md` (\(1+\#\{k\ge 2: R\,d_k>1\}\)) has the \(K^\*=0\) hole: it equals \(K^\star_0(R)\) when \(K^\star_0(R)\ge 1\), and returns \(1\) when \(K^\star_0(R)=0\).
2. **Band model 𝓑, any monotone world: PROVED (in-model 𝓑).** If layers are ideal low-pass bands combined with free weights (the zero-phase, sharp-cutoff picture of parallel stores), then the layer curve is convex for *every* world whose signal-to-noise ratio \(S(\omega)/N(\omega)\) does not rise with frequency. That covers any number of real time scales, scale-free (power-law) spectra and colored noise with falling SNR. Mechanism: the problem becomes 1-D weighted clustering of the Wiener gain, whose interval costs satisfy the quadrangle (Monge) inequality.
3. **Model S with at most two modes: PROVED (in-model, lead 0).** For every Model S world with \(J\le 2\) OU modes (any rates, variances, noise), \(\varepsilon_K\) is convex on \(K=0,1,2,\dots\), so N60 counted from \(K=0\) is exact. The proof uses positive residues of the optimal causal filter.

**What fails, and why.** Convexity can fail when the SNR has a peak, which is what a rhythm is. A low-pass layer can add only a band that starts at zero frequency, so isolating a peak takes two layers (a difference of two low-pass stores), and the second one is worth more than the first. The minimal example is three frequency cells with Wiener gain \(W=(0,1,0)\) and weights \((2,1,1)\), which give decrements \(1/3\) then \(2/3\). This is the same complementarity as "a rhythm is two layers by degree" (CT21) and the known two-oscillator counterexample. Submodularity alone is not enough (§5).

**What stays open.** Convexity in Model S for \(J\ge 3\) is still unproved. 𝓑 is a different model (zero-phase, non-causal), and its curve tracks Model S in magnitude (Spearman 0.79 on log ε) but not in shape (decrement-ratio Spearman −0.18). It gives a mechanism, not a proof for Model S. The halving conjecture \(\varepsilon_{K+1}\le\varepsilon_K/2\), which would imply convexity, holds in every Model S world computed under the \(\varepsilon_K>10^{-4}\) screen (144/144) but is unproved (and fails without the screen in low-SNR equal-\(L^2\)-norm regimes while convexity still holds).

A convex \(\varepsilon_K\) makes \(J(K)=hK+w E_K\) a convex sequence, so any local minimum in \(K\) is global and the minimizers form an interval. Every "convex" result here therefore also gives "local equals global".

## 1. Two elementary lemmas

On a finite domain \(K\in\{0,1,\dots,K_{\max}\}\) (\(K_{\max}\ge 1\)), write \(d_K=\varepsilon_{K-1}-\varepsilon_K\) for \(K\ge 1\), \(J(K)\propto K+R(1+\varepsilon_K)\) for \(R\ge 0\), and define the from-0 count \(K^\star_0(R)=\#\{K\ge 1: R\,d_K>1\}\).

**L1 (exactness).** On a finite domain, if \(d_K\) is non-increasing, then \(K^\star_0(R)\) equals the leftmost \(\arg\min_{0\le K\le K_{\max}} J(K)\) for all \(R\ge 0\). Conversely, if the decrements \(d_K\) are positive and pairwise distinct (in fact, distinctness with the threshold decrement \(d_{(m)}>0\) suffices) and some \(d_m<d_{m+1}\), there is an open interval of \(R>0\) on which \(K^\star_0(R)=m\) is not a minimizer of \(J\).
*Proof.* (⇒) At \(R=0\), \(J(K)=K\) has unique minimizer \(0\) and \(K^\star_0(0)=0\). For \(R>0\), this is marginal analysis (Fox 1966): \(J(K)-J(K-1)=1-R\,d_K\) is non-decreasing, so \(J\) strictly falls while \(R\,d_K>1\) and never falls afterwards, making \(K^\star_0(R)\) the leftmost minimizer. (⇐) \(d_m<d_{m+1}\) puts \((m,\varepsilon_m)\) strictly above the chord from \(m-1\) to \(m+1\), so \(J(m)>\min(J(m-1),J(m+1))\) for every \(R>0\) (and \(J(m)>J(0)\) at \(R=0\)), and \(m\) is never a minimizer. Ordering the positive, pairwise distinct decrements as \(d_{(1)}>\dots>d_{(K_{\max})}>0\) (and noting \(1\le m<K_{\max}\)), any \(R\in(1/d_{(m)},1/d_{(m+1)})\) gives \(K^\star_0(R)=m\), which is not a minimizer. (If only distinctness and \(d_{(m)}>0\) hold, take \(R>1/d_{(m)}\) below \(1/d_{(m+1)}\) when \(d_{(m+1)}>0\), or any \(R>1/d_{(m)}\) when \(d_{(m+1)}\le 0\).) ∎

Without positivity and distinctness, the bare "iff" is false: \(d=(-2,-1)\) (\(\varepsilon=(-3,-1,0)\)) is not convex, yet \(R\,d_K>1\) never holds, so \(K^\star_0(R)=0\) equals the unique minimizer for every \(R\ge 0\).

**Hole in N60 as written.** `core/KEYSTONE_NOTE.md` (and `CLAIM_MAP_v2.md` N60) writes \(1+\#\{k\ge 2: R\,d_k>1\}\), which starts at \(k=2\) and cannot return \(0\) (`core/KEYSTONE_NOTE.md:33`, §5). On the convex sequence \(\varepsilon=(1,\,0.4,\,0)\) (\(d=(0.6,\,0.4)\)) at \(R=1\), \(J=(2,\,2.4,\,3)\) has leftmost minimizer \(0\), while N60 as quoted—and a count started at \(k_0=1\)—returns \(1\). Under non-increasing \(d_K\), \(1+\#\{k\ge 2: R\,d_k>1\}\) equals \(K^\star_0(R)\) whenever \(K^\star_0(R)\ge 1\), and equals \(1\) when \(K^\star_0(R)=0\). The corrected count is \(K^\star_0(R)=\#\{K\ge 1: R\,d_K>1\}\). The general rule for any sequence is the lower-convex-hull test (`core/KEYSTONE_NOTE.md` §4), and L1 says when it reduces to the adjacent count.

**L2 (sufficient conditions).** (a) *Halving:* if \(\varepsilon_{K+1}\le\varepsilon_K/2\) for all \(K\), then \(\varepsilon\) is convex, since \(d_{K+2}\le\varepsilon_{K+1}\le\varepsilon_K-\varepsilon_{K+1}=d_{K+1}\). (b) *Log-convexity:* if the ratios \(\varepsilon_{K+1}/\varepsilon_K\) are non-decreasing, then \(\varepsilon_{K+1}^2\le\varepsilon_K\varepsilon_{K+2}\), and AM–GM gives \(2\varepsilon_{K+1}\le\varepsilon_K+\varepsilon_{K+2}\). The constant \(1/2\) is sharp for a tail ending at zero, because \((1,r,0)\) is convex iff \(r\le 1/2\). ∎

## 2. The Ky Fan / Eckart–Young class (prior art)

If the value of \(K\) layers is the best \(K\)-dimensional subspace of a fixed quadratic form, \(V(K)=\max_{\dim S=K}\operatorname{tr}(P_S T)=\sum_{i\le K}\lambda_i(T)\) (Ky Fan 1949), then the residual \(\sum_{i>K}\lambda_i\) (Eckart & Young 1936) has decrements \(\lambda_K\), which are sorted, so it is convex. One instance is a memory that does **not** have to update itself: a rank-\(K\) linear summary of a fixed regressor vector, read out for a vector target in squared error (reduced-rank regression, Izenman 1975). Model S is not in this class, because a \(K\)-store memory must be recursively updatable, and that imposes Hankel structure. For the Hankel case the optimal Hankel-norm error of a degree-\(K\) approximation is \(\sigma_{K+1}\) (Adamjan, Arov & Kreĭn 1971; Glover 1984), and balanced truncation gives \(\sigma_{K+1}\le\|G-G_K\|_\infty\le 2\sum_{i>K}\sigma_i\) (Moore 1981; Enns 1984; Glover 1984). Hankel singular values are non-increasing but need not be convex, and \(H_2\)-optimal reduction is a non-convex problem (Gugercin, Antoulas & Beattie 2008). These results bound the curve but do not give convexity. Rate–distortion reverse water-filling (Kolmogorov 1956) makes distortion convex in *rate*, a continuous resource. The layer count is a discrete degree, and the repo's water-filling count rule (T25) failed, so it does not transfer.

## 3. The band model 𝓑 and the Monge argument

**Model 𝓑.** The signal has spectral density \(S(\omega)\ge0\) and the noise has density \(N(\omega)>0\), with \(y=s+n\). A \(K\)-layer design is \(H(\omega)=\sum_{k\le K}g_k\,\mathbf 1[|\omega|<b_k]\): \(K\) ideal low-pass layers with free cutoffs \(b_k\) and free real weights, so \(H\) is a staircase in \(|\omega|\) with at most \(K\) steps and \(H=0\) above the top cutoff. The cost is \(E^B_K=\min\int(|1-H|^2S+H^2N)\,d\omega/2\pi\). This is the sharp, zero-phase picture of \(K\) parallel exponential stores: store \(k\) passes what is slower than its rate. Because it is zero-phase, \(E^B_\infty\) is the non-causal Wiener error, not Model S's causal \(E_\infty\).

**Reduction.** With \(W=S/(S+N)\), the identity \(|1-H|^2S+H^2N=(S+N)(H-W)^2+SN/(S+N)\) gives
\[
E^B_K-E^B_\infty=\min_{\text{K-step }H}\int_0^\infty (H-W)^2\,d\mu,\qquad d\mu=(S+N)\,d\omega/\pi .
\]
Order frequencies from low to high. A design on a finite grid \(\{0,1,\dots,M\}\) (or on \([0,\infty)\)) is a path \(0=x_0\le x_1\le\dots\le x_K\le x_{K+1}=\infty\) made of \(K\) free bands \([x_{i-1},x_i)\) (\(1\le i\le K\)), each with cost \(c(x_{i-1},x_i)=w(x_{i-1},x_i)=\min_G\int(W-G)^2d\mu\) (the weighted within-band variance of \(W\)), plus one terminal band at level 0 with cost \(c(x_K,\infty)=z(x_K)=\int_{x_K}^\infty W^2d\mu\) (on the grid, the sum over cells \(j\ge x_K\)). **\(\infty\)-endpoint convention:** \(\infty\) is a formal sentinel beyond all finite cutoffs \(x_i\) (or beyond \(M\) on a finite grid, where a free band \([x_{i-1},M)\) still has cost \(w(x_{i-1},M)\)), so \(c(x,y)=w(x,y)\) for finite \(y\) and \(c(x,\infty)=z(x)\). Empty bands are allowed, so "at most \(K\)" and "exactly \(K\)" coincide.

**Lemma M (quadrangle).** If \(W\) is non-increasing in frequency, then \(w(i,k)+w(j,l)\le w(i,l)+w(j,k)\) for \(i\le j\le k\le l\).
*Proof.* Let \(A=[i,j)\), \(B=[j,k)\), \(C=[k,l)\), with masses \(\mu\) and \(\mu\)-means \(m\). Merging gives \(w(X\cup Y)=w(X)+w(Y)+\frac{\mu_X\mu_Y}{\mu_X+\mu_Y}(m_X-m_Y)^2\). So \(w(i,l)-w(j,l)=w(A)+\frac{\mu_A\mu_{BC}}{\mu_A+\mu_{BC}}(m_A-m_{BC})^2\) and \(w(i,k)-w(j,k)=w(A)+\frac{\mu_A\mu_B}{\mu_A+\mu_B}(m_A-m_B)^2\). Here \(\mu_{BC}\ge\mu_B\), and monotone \(W\) gives \(m_A\ge m_B\ge m_{BC}\), so the first difference is at least the second. ∎ (This is the 1-D k-means fact behind Wu 1991 and Grønlund et al. 2017.)

**Lemma Z (terminal band).** If \(W\ge 0\) and \(W\) is non-increasing in frequency, then \(w(i,k)-w(j,k)\le z(i)-z(j)\) for \(i\le j\le k\).
*Proof.* Let \(A=[i,j)\) and \(B=[j,k)\). If \(\mu_B=0\), \(w(j,k)=0\) and holding level \(0\) on \(A\cup B\) gives \(w(i,k)\le \int_A W^2d\mu=z(i)-z(j)\). Otherwise, keeping \(B\)'s level \(m_B\) on \(A\) gives \(w(i,k)\le w(j,k)+\int_A(W-m_B)^2d\mu\). On \(A\) (lower frequencies), \(W\ge m_B\ge0\) (using non-increasing \(W\) and \(W\ge 0\)), so \((W-m_B)^2\le W^2\), and \(\int_AW^2d\mu=z(i)-z(j)\). The hypothesis \(W\ge 0\) is necessary: \(W=(0,-1)\) with equal weights gives \(w(AB)-w(B)=1/2\) while \(z(0)-z(1)=0\). It is harmless in 𝓑 since \(W=1/(1+N/S)\ge 0\). ∎

**Lemma X (exchange; the Aggarwal–Schieber–Tokuyama argument with a terminal band).** On a finite grid, if Lemmas M and Z hold, then \(2E_K\le E_{K-1}+E_{K+1}\) for \(K\ge1\).
*Proof.* On a finite grid optimal paths exist; take an optimal path \(P=(p_0,\dots,p_K)\) with \(K-1\) free bands and an optimal path \(Q=(q_0,\dots,q_{K+2})\) with \(K+1\), with \(p_0=q_0=0\) and sentinel endpoints \(p_K=q_{K+2}=\infty\) (all earlier breakpoints finite). Let \(t=\min\{t\in\{1..K\}: q_{t+1}\le p_t\}\). It exists because \(t=K\) qualifies (\(q_{K+1}<\infty=p_K\)). For \(t=1\), \(p_0\le q_1\) comes from \(p_0=0\) (not from minimality); for \(t\ge 2\), minimality of \(t\) gives \(p_{t-1}<q_t\). Hence \(p_{t-1}\le q_t\le q_{t+1}\le p_t\) in every case. Swap tails: \(P'=(p_0..p_{t-1},q_{t+1}..q_{K+2})\) and \(Q'=(q_0..q_t,p_t..p_K)\) each have \(K\) free bands. The cost changes by \([c(p_{t-1},q_{t+1})+c(q_t,p_t)]-[c(p_{t-1},p_t)+c(q_t,q_{t+1})]\). Since \(q_{t+1}\) is finite, this is \(\le0\) by Lemma M if \(t<K\) (all four bands free), and by Lemma Z if \(t=K\) (where \(p_K=\infty\), so \(c(p_{K-1},p_K)=z(p_{K-1})\) and \(c(q_K,p_K)=z(q_K)\) move the terminal band from \(P\) to \(Q'\)). Hence \(2E_K\le E(P')+E(Q')\le E(P)+E(Q)=E_{K-1}+E_{K+1}\). ∎

**Theorem B (PROVED in-model 𝓑).** *If \(S(\omega)/N(\omega)\) is non-increasing in \(|\omega|\), then \(E^B_K\) is convex in \(K\ge0\), so in 𝓑 the from-0 count \(K^\star_0(R)\) equals the leftmost \(\arg\min J\) for every \(R\ge 0\).* The proof needs only that \(W=1/(1+N/S)\ge 0\) is non-increasing. On any finite frequency grid this is Lemmas M, Z and X directly; for a continuous measure on \([0,\infty)\), finite-grid step approximations pass to the infimum. It therefore holds for any finite or continuous measure: every Model S spectrum, any mixture of time scales, any power law \(c/(\omega_0^\alpha+\omega^\alpha)\), and colored noise as long as the SNR falls. It does not need scale separation.

**Numbers** (`tools/layer_convexity_checks.py`; an exact DP over grid breakpoints, which is itself an instance of the theorem): 400/400 monotone worlds (OU sums with \(J=1..6\), power laws, colored noise) gave convex curves on \(K=0..8\). In 20,000 random monotone draws, Lemmas M and Z had 0 violations. Random Monge (convex-of-gap) \(K\)-link costs were convex 600/600, and concave-of-gap costs were non-convex 599/600. Halving is *not* the mechanism in 𝓑: ratios there reach 0.80 while the curve stays convex.

## 4. Model S with two modes

Model S at lead 0: \(s=\sum_js_j\) is a sum of OU modes (rates \(a_j\), variances \(v_j\)) seen in white noise \(N\); \(E_0=\sum v_j\) (act blind); \(E_K\) is the best \(K\)-store MSE, and \(E_J=E_\infty\) (L2, CT21).

**Lemma P (positive residues).** The roots \(-c_k^2\) in \(x=\omega^2\) of \(N+\sum_j 2a_jv_j/(a_j^2+x)\) interlace, \(a_1<c_1<a_2<c_2<\dots<a_J<c_J\), because the sum is decreasing between consecutive poles and runs from \(+\infty\) to \(-\infty\) there. The causal Wiener filter for signal plus white noise is \(H(s)=1-\sqrt N/\Psi(s)=1-\prod_j(s+a_j)/\prod_k(s+c_k)\), the standard spectral-factor form, so it is strictly proper. Its residue at \(-c_k\) is \(-\prod_j(a_j-c_k)/\prod_{i\ne k}(c_i-c_k)\), which has sign \(-(-1)^k/(-1)^{k-1}=+1\). So the optimal estimate is \(\hat s=\sum_k u_k\) with \(u_k=g_kf_{c_k}\), \(g_k>0\), where \(f_b\) is the exponential store at rate \(b\). ∎ This is classical material in a new use. The check confirms interlacing and positivity in 1,000/1,000 random worlds (\(J=2..6\)), and that \(E\) at the Kalman rates matches the Yovits–Jackson \(E_\infty\) in the 40 worlds cross-checked.

**Theorem S2 (PROVED in-model; Model S, \(J\le2\), lead 0).** *\(\varepsilon_K\) is convex on \(K=0,1,2,\dots\).*
*Proof.* \(J=1\) is trivial. For \(J=2\), store outputs are positively correlated (each term of the covariance in `theory/layers_checks/stores.py` is positive), so \(C=\mathrm{Cov}(u_1,u_2)>0\). By orthogonality, \(E_0-E_\infty=\mathrm{Var}\,\hat s=V_1+V_2+2C\), and the best single store at rate \(c_k\) gains \(\mathrm{Cov}(\hat s,u_k)^2/V_k=(V_k+C)^2/V_k\ge V_k+2C\). The two gains sum to at least \(V_1+V_2+4C>\mathrm{Var}\,\hat s\), so the better one gains more than half. Hence \(E_1<(E_0+E_\infty)/2\), that is \(d_1>d_2\), and \(d_K=0\) for \(K\ge3\). ∎
Numerically the worst one-store excess share over 500 two-mode worlds is 0.30, well inside the bound of 0.5. **For general \(J\)** the same Cauchy–Schwarz step gives only that one store at the best Kalman rate captures at least \(1/J\) of the full gain (observed at least 0.67). That is a bound, not convexity.

## 5. Where convexity fails

**Peaks.** In 𝓑, take three frequency cells from low to high with \(W=(0,1,0)\) and weights \((2,1,1)\). Then \(E^B-E^B_\infty=(1,\,2/3,\,0)\) for \(K=0,1,2\), with decrements \(1/3<2/3\). One low-pass band must start at zero frequency and smear the peak, while two bands isolate it. More generally, any \(W\) with a deep enough dip below a peak can be weighted to fail, but shallow peaks need not fail, so monotone SNR is sufficient and not necessary. Over 240 random resonance-plus-background spectra, 51 band curves were non-convex, and every one of them had non-monotone \(W\). The known two-oscillator Model-S-outside counterexample (`core/KEYSTONE_NOTE.md` §2, decrements 0.179, 0.578, 0.226) has non-monotone \(W\), and its band curve is non-convex too (at \(K=3\to4\) rather than at \(2\to3\), so 𝓑 matches the diagnosis, not the location).

**Submodularity is not enough.** Diminishing returns of a *set* value do not give diminishing returns of the *best-K* value. Take the coverage function with \(a=\{1..6\}\), \(b=\{1,2,7,8\}\), \(c=\{3,4,9,10\}\), \(d=\{5,6,11,12\}\): it is submodular, yet its best-\(K\) values \(0,6,8,12\) have increments \(6,2,4\). What convexity needs is an exchange structure (Monge here, sorted eigenvalues in §2). Das & Kempe (2008) work in the subset-regression setting, and their bounds are approximation ratios, not convexity.

## 6. What is open

- **Model S, \(J\ge3\)** (W9). Evidence: S5 120/120, plus 24/24 fresh \(J=5\) worlds (`theory/layer_convexity_checks/models_j5.py`, seeds disjoint from S5, convex on \(K=0..5\), none below \(E_\infty\)). A proof would need either a causal analogue of Lemma M (the causal error has Yovits–Jackson log form, not the squared-gap form used here) or a decomposition of the \(K\)-store problem into ordered segments. Neither is done.
- **Halving conjecture (Model S).** \(\varepsilon_{K+1}\le\varepsilon_K/2\) held in 144/144 worlds screened at \(\varepsilon_K>10^{-4}\). Max ratios: 0.34 (S5), 0.44 (\(J=5\)), and 0.4625 for the adversarial \(J=3\) climb. Unscreened, halving fails at low SNR when modes have equal \(L^2\) norm and widely separated scales (e.g. \(J=4\), \(a=(1,10^{-3},10^{-6},10^{-9})\), \(v_j=10^{-6.5}\sqrt{a_j}\), \(N=1\) gives \(\varepsilon_1=4.75\times 10^{-7}\), \(\varepsilon_2=3.01\times 10^{-7}\), ratio \(0.634\), while staying convex). By L2, failure of halving does not break convexity.
- **𝓑 → Model S bridge.** On the S5 worlds, \(\log\varepsilon\) correlates at Spearman 0.79, but the decrement-ratio shape does not (−0.18; `band_vs_models.py`). 𝓑 explains *why* real-pole worlds behave and rhythms do not. It is not a quantitative stand-in.
- **Lead \(k>0\)** and non-Gaussian worlds are not covered. The predictive rhythm rule (T18) stays OPEN and deprioritized.
