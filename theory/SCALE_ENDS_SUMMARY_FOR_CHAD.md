# The ends of the scale, in plain English (for Chad; 18 Nov 2026)

**The costs are real at every scale.** What changes at the ends is whether a model is worth anything.

**Bottom.** An atom can't hold a model: at room temperature it carries about 10⁻¹⁷¹ nats about its surroundings. It doesn't need one either, because physics keeps it intact for free. A model would still cost at least kT ln 2 per bit, and it would buy nothing. So the theory's own optimum puts atoms in the frozen corner.

**Switch-on.** Models pay only when four things line up: good-enough heredity (Eigen's limit), a choice that depends on the world, a world that drifts at a trackable pace, and a first bit worth its cost. The energy floor never blocks it; heredity and drift do.

**Top.** Stars burn fuel but track nothing outside themselves: held structure, no model. A black hole is the limit of the frozen corner. It holds the most information its size allows, at the lowest energy per bit, pays nothing to persist, and leaks over 10⁶⁶–10⁶⁷ years × mass³. Only 2–3 numbers can be read from outside. It remembers the past and predicts nothing.

**How high.** To the cosmic horizon, which caps memory near 10¹²² nats and sets a floor price per bit. Light delay forces big structures to federate (testable on Earth).

**Labels.**
- Derived: both frozen ends, the black hole's place (given known physics), the light-delay limit.
- Hypothesis: the switch-on conditions; storage tiers sorting by read cost.
- Credited: Smolin (strained by neutron stars of 2+ solar masses), Dyson, Krauss–Starkman.
- Rejected: atoms predict; black holes think; the theory explains why black holes exist.

**Opening:** "An atom never has to guess. Physics holds it together for free, so a model would cost something and buy nothing. The bill for knowing comes due only when a changing world can ruin you."

**Closing:** "At the far end is the black hole: the most memory the universe allows, almost none of it readable. It remembers everything that fell in and predicts nothing. Everything that has to keep up lives in between."
