# The ends of the scale, in plain English (for Chad; 18 Nov 2026)

**Short answer.** The three costs (keeping a memory, being wrong or late, paying for reads) are real at every scale, from atoms to the edge of the universe. What changes at the two ends is not the cost. It is whether a model is worth anything.

**At the bottom (atoms, nuclei, crystals).** An atom can't carry a model. Its state is so locked in that it holds essentially zero information about its surroundings: about 10⁻¹⁷¹ nats at room temperature. It also doesn't need one, because physics keeps it intact without any tracking. A model would still cost something, at least kT ln 2 per bit, and it would buy nothing. So the theory's own optimum puts atoms in the "frozen" corner: free to keep, nothing to update.

**The switch-on.** Models start paying only when four things line up: heredity that copies well enough (the Eigen limit), a choice whose payoff depends on the world, a world that changes at a trackable pace, and a first bit that pays for itself. The energy floor is never what blocks this; a bacterium has about 10¹¹ kT per division to spend. Heredity and drift are what block it.

**At the top.** Stars persist by burning fuel, but they track nothing outside themselves: held structure, no model. In the theory's terms, a black hole is the extreme of the frozen corner. It holds the most information physics allows for its size, at the lowest energy per bit. It pays nothing to persist and leaks slowly (about 10⁶⁶–10⁶⁷ years × mass³). Only 2–3 numbers can be read from outside, and reading anything else costs so much that no tracker would ever use it as memory. It records the past and predicts nothing.

**How high.** All the way to the cosmic horizon, which caps memory at about 10¹²² nats and sets a floor price per bit. Light-speed delay limits what a big structure can track centrally, so big things must federate. That last part is testable on Earth.

**Derived vs not.** Derived: the frozen corner at both ends, the black hole's place in it (given known physics), and the light-delay limit. Hypothesis: the switch-on conditions, and the claim that storage tiers sort by read cost. Prior art credited: Smolin's black-hole selection (under strain from 2+ solar-mass neutron stars), Dyson, Krauss–Starkman. Rejected: "atoms predict," "black holes think," and "the theory explains why black holes exist."

**Video, opening (atoms):** "An atom never has to guess. Physics holds it together for free, so a model would cost something and buy nothing. The bill for knowing only comes due when something can be ruined by a world that changes."

**Video, closing (cosmos):** "At the far end sits the black hole: the most memory the universe allows, and almost none of it readable. It remembers everything that fell in and predicts nothing. Between the atom and the black hole is everything that has to keep up, and the edge of the universe sets the price of every bit."
