## 1. The picture in plain words (the spine)

Everything runs downhill. Hot things cool, fuel burns, and order wears away. That is the second law, and it is the only physical principle this picture leans on. GTAO adds no new physics. It asks one question: **what does it take to last while everything runs downhill?** There are three answers.

**Keep.** Some things settled long ago into states that hardly anything can knock loose. Atoms, crystals and, at the extreme, black holes are like this. They pay nothing to last. They carry traces of where they came from: an atom's state correlates a little with its surroundings, and a crystal records the conditions it formed in. They use none of it, because nothing they do depends on getting the world right. They hold information, but they have no use for a model.

**Burn.** Some things last only by running energy through themselves: a star, a hurricane, a flame. They steady themselves through feedback. A star that swells cools and contracts again. But that feedback is only on themselves. Nothing outside has to be known for a star to keep shining. It burns until its fuel is gone.

**Know.** Some things last only by running energy through themselves *and* staying matched to an outside world that keeps changing and can kill them. A cell whose food moves, an animal, a company, a city. For these, knowing comes in steps:
- **React.** The simplest knowing is feedback on the outside world: notice the change, push back. That is already a model, the simplest one there is: "tomorrow will be like now."
- **Act ahead.** When the world arrives faster than you can respond, reacting is too late. Then it pays to carry a model and act before the world gets there. That is judgment. It is not a new principle, just a way of staying alive under the old one.
- **Pay for it.** Knowing is never free. Keeping a fact costs upkeep, looking costs effort, and being wrong or late costs losses. Everything that knows has to balance **what to keep, what to check, and what to let go**. The theory says the balance depends on how fast your world changes and how much a mistake costs you. That claim is testable, and it has not yet been tested.

**How the three depend on each other.** Knowers are *built* from keepers: atoms, bonds, and the nearly frozen code in DNA. They are *fed* by burners: sunlight from a star, and heat and chemistry from a planet's interior. They *record* themselves in keepers: fossils, rocks, books, the history of this repository. A good knower freezes everything that does not change and spends its effort only on what does. So frozen memory is not the opposite of knowing. It is the cheap foundation knowing stands on.

**Knowing did not start with the cell.** It came on gradually: first chemistry that merely correlates with its surroundings, then chemistry that keeps itself going on a flow, then chemistry whose survival depends on staying matched to a changing supply, then chemistry that copies itself well enough for improvements to accumulate, and finally systems that act ahead. The cell is where all of these were first packaged together in one place.

**When many knowers face the same world, they share a model.** One trail, one price, one rulebook or one brain serves many parts at once. That is where layers come from. Shared knowledge is a commons, though. Parts tend to let it go stale unless the cost of the whole failing reaches them. So wherever shared knowledge is a public good, expect someone to be in charge: a monitor, a rule or an owner. And because signals cannot travel faster than light, nothing large can know everything centrally. Big things have to federate.

**Where the bookends sit.** At the bottom, atoms obey the second law by settling, and they have nothing to know. At the top, black holes are where the second law has gone furthest in any region: the most entropy that region can hold, everything kept, nothing predicted. The universe today is far from its maximum entropy, and that gap is what pays for every star and every living thing. As it closes, knowing stops first, burning stops next, and keeping lasts longest, until even black holes leak away.

**What it would mean if this is right.** The same few relations would hold from the first self-copying chemistry to cities: how often to look, how long to remember, when to share a model, when someone has to be in charge. Each would be set by how fast that level's world changes and what a mistake costs there. You could look at the world an organization faces and say how it should be built. That would be a large claim. It is a bet, not a finding, and §4 lists how to break it at each level.

### 2.2 Is feedback the simplest model? Reconciling H80 with SCALE_ENDS's "held structure, no model"

**Decision [BK, new, unreviewed; resting on PROVED (in-model) pieces].** What decides whether something is a model is **what the feedback is about**, not whether feedback is present.

- **Feedback on an exogenous, ruin-relevant variable is a model: the lowest rung of prediction.** It holds the last reading and acts on it, which is exactly the theory's base sensing class R1 (Axiom 3: "between reads the tracker holds the last value (zero-order hold)"). It carries I(M; X_t) > 0, and by Theorem P it is necessary wherever blind is not viable. In H80's ladder (frozen, timer, threshold/thermostat, small model, large model) this is the thermostat tier, a *predictor* tier ranked by its residual drift q_k (H80; C13(c) in the reviewed core file, which notes that the thermostat rung is event-triggered and must be costed separately). Conant & Ashby 1970 ("every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system") is the classic prior art. GENERAL_THEOREM §2 records its limits: the identity map counts as a model in their sense.
- **Feedback on a system's own state only is not a model of any world.** A star's negative heat capacity restores its own balance. Its ruin (fuel exhaustion) is fixed by its own mass and composition, and no outside drifting variable enters its main-sequence persistence (SCALE_ENDS §4.1, E1/E2). Holding information about yourself is trivial (I(X; X) = H(X)) and predicts nothing outside. This is *self-regulation*. It is held structure without a model, and SCALE_ENDS is right about stars.
- **The repo has been inconsistent.** SCALE_ENDS §2's table labels the no-model class "(H80's thermostat tier)" with R = 0, and §4.1 calls the star's balance "H80's feedback tier". But H80's thermostat tier tracks an *outside* target with R > 0. The independent review already called the word "thermostat" an analogy (`reviews/night2/SCALE_ENDS_INDEP_REVIEW.md`, stars bullet). The H80 ladder itself also starts with "frozen" as a predictor tier, meaning a constant prediction. So the ladder is a ladder of *predictors of an outside target*, and its bottom rung corresponds to SCALE_ENDS's frozen corner (Theorem 8, q ≤ q_f), not to "held structure, no model".
- **Does the class boundary need to change? Yes, in wording, and one class needs adding (§6, P1–P3).**
  - Class 2 becomes "held structure, **self-regulated**: feedback on own state only, no exogenous target".
  - A new class **2b, "held, coupled, unselected"**: feedback or correlation with an exogenous ruin-relevant variable, so semantic information > 0, but no heredity over designs. Candidates: G2 chemistry, Daisyworld-type planetary regulation (§2.5), possibly hurricanes (a hurricane's persistence depends on the sea-surface temperature beneath it; whether its internal state carries semantic information about that is OPEN).
  - Class 3 ("held model") keeps its name, but its condition changes from "selection over designs" to "Theorem P necessity". Selection becomes the condition for **design predictions**, which is exactly FRAMEWORK §1.2 item 4's "design predictions wherever there is selection".
- **Where "feedback" ends and "judgment" begins.** Reactive feedback acts at lead k ≈ 0. Judgment, in the 12 Nov post's sense, is k > 0, and it pays only where lateness is costly and the world is predictable over the lead (P2). The H80 timer tier (an open-loop clock) is the cheapest form of acting ahead. So the ladder in plain words is: keep a constant, then run a clock, then react, then predict a little, then predict a lot. Each rung is bought only when the cheaper one leaves error outside tolerance (H80 formula J_k = C_k + √(2cw·q_k) for periodic-check tiers; HYPOTHESIS, untested).

### 2.3 Why frozen memory exists in the grand scheme

GTAO does not supply purposes. It supplies roles inside its budget and the physical conditions behind them. **Four roles, all DERIVED from existing results:**

1. **Scaffold.** Knowers are built from frozen parts: nuclei, atoms, covalent bonds and crystal lattices, whose keeping cost is 0 and whose lifetime is set by a barrier, a conservation law or a horizon (SCALE_ENDS E4). H9's "paid systems are built out of frozen parts earlier flows left behind" is the plain form. Under T8 this is optimal, because anything drifting slower than q_f = 2c/(W H_l²) should be frozen (F13 PROVED (in-model)). H8's refinement, that frozen things were themselves formed in an earlier non-equilibrium flow and then froze, is consistent with this. It was not checked against the literature here.
2. **Heredity.** A genome is a nearly frozen store. It is copied rather than tracked, and its length is capped by copying fidelity (Eigen 1971). In T8 terms the genome is the freeze tier for features of the world that drift slower than generations, and the lower edge of the hold band (N28's S3; Kussell & Leibler 2005 for constitutive versus switching strategies) is where "write it into the genome" gives way to "sense it". **HYPOTHESIS (= O5/BH-1, F13):** across tiers of a cell's memory (DNA, then chromatin marks, then mRNA, then protein modification), the denser and costlier-to-read the tier, the slower the drift of what it holds. The kill is FRAMEWORK §6.6's O5 kill.
3. **Records.** Frozen stores are memory for *later* knowers: zircons, fossils, ice cores, archives, an append-only git history. Reading one is a T8 "freeze read" (SCALE_ENDS §3.1, "a geologist reading a zircon"). The 12 Nov post's rule 4 (keep prior versions; frozen history is nearly free) is the design rule here (THEORY §3, "near-tautology").
4. **The freeze corner of the band.** Every knower's memory splits into frozen, held and looked-up parts (Theorem 8). An efficient knower freezes as much as it can and pays upkeep only on what drifts. That is the plain-words "what to keep" in "what to keep, what to check, and what to let go".

**Black holes as the frozen extreme (N26 PROVED (in-model), conditional on the Axiom-7 reading and on counting read latency as lateness).** A black hole has maximal memory for its size at the Bekenstein-minimum energy per nat, no upkeep, a readable state of (M, J, Q), and a leak lifetime of about 1.2×10⁶⁷ yr × (M/M☉)³. Everything else returns only after about the Page time. So keeping and knowing come apart completely at this extreme. **This file adds one observation (DERIVED from PRIOR ART numbers already in SCALE_ENDS §4.5):** the present entropy of the observable universe, S_obs ≈ 3×10¹⁰⁴ k, is dominated by supermassive black holes (Egan & Lineweaver 2010). Most of the running-down that has happened so far is therefore stored in the frozen extreme. GTAO did not derive this; it is published physics. What GTAO adds is the label: the place where the second law has gone furthest is the place where models are worth least. **IMPOSTER:** "black holes exist *in order to* store entropy" or "*for* the universe". GTAO has no teleology. The only published proposal in which black holes play a role within a selection process is Smolin's cosmological natural selection (PRIOR ART, under observational pressure; SCALE_ENDS §4.4), and GTAO adds no support to it (SE12).

**The entropy gap as the funding (PRIOR ART statement; DERIVED label).** S_obs ≈ 3×10¹⁰⁴ k against the de Sitter horizon's S_dS ≈ 3.3×10¹²² k (SCALE_ENDS §4.5). Egan & Lineweaver say that the uncapped increase "is the reason dissipative processes are ongoing and that life can exist". By the growth inequality (growth ≤ disequilibrium + information − house take; THEORY §2.3; KNOWN), held structures need D > 0. **Caveat:** an entropy gap is not usable free energy by itself. What structures actually draw on are local gradients (fusion, gravitational contraction, chemical disequilibrium), which the gap makes possible. This file does not quantify the link.

### 2.4 Stars: is complex plasma self-regulation a model?

**No, in the theory's sense. It is held structure with self-regulation (class 2, relabelled per §2.2). Status: DERIVED classification (SE9).** The reasons are non-definitional: a main-sequence star's ruin hazard does not depend on any outside drifting variable it could respond to (E2 relative to externals), and its upkeep h equals its luminosity L, which is measurable. Complexity of internal dynamics (convection, magnetic cycles, oscillations) is not the criterion. What counts is whether persistence requires information about an exogenous future. A turbulent star still "knows" nothing it needs.

**Edges, stated honestly.**
- **Stellar generations pass on composition.** Later stars form from gas enriched by earlier ones. That is a frozen record handed down, but nothing selects among star designs by persistence or reproduction, so there is no design optimum (Axiom 7 fails; MV8-ii fails).
- **Binaries and accretion.** A star can be perturbed from outside, but it has no response whose payoff depends on getting that outside right.
- **Kill (SE9):** a star whose persistence depends on tracking an external drift. Stars also serve as a known group for FRAMEWORK MV7: any instrument that reports a model in a star fails calibration.

### 2.5 Planets and Gaia: tracker, thermostat, host, or shared slow store?

| Part | Class | What GTAO says |

In GTAO terms that is a **persistence filter in one lineage**, not Axiom-7 selection among alternatives. **Earlier chat claim corrected.** Ace said Daisyworld shows regulation "with no model anywhere". In Theorem P's sense, Daisyworld's albedo feedback is a model of the lowest, reactive kind: the daisy cover carries information about the solar forcing, and the planet's habitability depends on it (Watson & Lovelock 1983; Daisyworld as summarized in litmap). What it lacks is selection over designs at planet scale and any lead time. "No model" should read "**no selected model, and no acting ahead, at planet scale**".

**Why would the biosphere survive human extinction? (DERIVED from PROVED (in-model) coarse-graining rules; application is HYPOTHESIS.)** THEORY §4's temperature index is z = B/(σ√τ). Pooling N parts with shock correlation ρ cools the whole: z′ = z·√(N/(1+(N−1)ρ)), up to a ceiling of z/√ρ. The biosphere is a very large, weakly correlated pool of lineages with fast local repair (re-colonization, re-diversification). The ruin of one lineage, even a dominant one, is a small, mostly uncorrelated shock to the pool. **What would ruin it** is a shock correlated across almost all lineages (ρ → 1), because correlation caps the cooling. In plain words: life as a whole is fragile only to what hits nearly everything at once. Redundancy and buffers are the theory's own ruin-avoidance terms (Theorem L: survival-optimal checking is more cautious than cost-optimal). **Caveat:** the diffusive premise of the index fails for single big jumps (GENERAL_THEOREM §0 item 4), and mass-extinction shocks are jump-like. So this is the direction of the effect, not a rate.

**Is there any selection over designs at planetary scale?** Not in the Axiom-7 sense: there is no population of planets with heredity. Sequential selection is a filter over successive states of one planet. **OPEN:** whether a persistence filter acting over geological time can be treated as Axiom-7 selection with a computable ε. Nobody has defined that ε.

**"Is a planet conscious?"** GTAO has no variable for experience, awareness or consciousness. It can say what information a system holds (I_pred), how far ahead it acts (k), which rung of the predictor ladder it uses, and whether its design is selected (ε). By those measures planetary regulation sits low: reactive, no lead, unselected. But nothing in GTAO maps those measures onto consciousness. **Any claim, for or against, that GTAO shows a planet is conscious is an IMPOSTER.** The free-energy principle is a rival framework that grants inference-like dynamics to broad classes of systems (Friston 2013; Friston et al. 2023, credited in SCALE_ENDS §2 as a rival). It too does not settle consciousness by itself, and this file takes no position on it.

### 2.7 One coherent picture, in technical terms: the three axes

**[BK, new, unreviewed synthesis of PROVED (in-model) and PRIOR ART pieces.]** Every persisting structure X gets three coordinates:

- **Axis P (persistence mode):** *frozen* (h = 0; lifetime set by a barrier, a conservation law or a horizon; SCALE_ENDS E4) or *held* (h > 0 paid from a flux; needs D > 0 by the growth inequality).
- **Axis K (knowing):**
  - K0: physical correlation only, with semantic information = 0 (E2 holds);
  - K1: self-regulation (feedback on own state; no exogenous target);
  - K2: reactive coupling (I_pred > 0 about an exogenous ruin-relevant variable at k ≈ 0; class R1, the thermostat tier);
  - K3: anticipation (k > 0: timer, small model, large model; P2 caps k).
- **Axis S (selection):** none; persistence filter only (Pross's dynamic kinetic stability, Lenton's sequential selection, survivorship); cumulative selection with heredity below Eigen's threshold, or learning or design, with resolution ε (Axiom 7; FRAMEWORK §3.10).

**What applies where.**
- **The three-cost accounting** (Axiom 5; J16 OPEN as an empirical claim) applies to every coordinate, physically priced.
- **Theorem P's necessity** (P1: PROVED (in-model)) applies wherever X is held and persists in a world where blind is not viable. That is what forces K ≥ K2.
- **E1 and E2** (N25 PROVED (in-model)) put frozen structures, and held structures with no exogenous ruin variable, at K0 or K1.
- **GTAO's design predictions** (N13, J6, both OPEN) apply only where S is cumulative and the prize exceeds ε.

**How the axes depend on one another.** K ≥ K2 requires P = held, because holding information about drift needs an inflow (I5; E3(b)). Held structures need D > 0, which comes from the cosmic entropy gap through local gradients. S = cumulative requires the frozen-like heredity of G3, so cumulative design needs a frozen store. And knowers keep their slow-drifting content in frozen tiers (T8). In one line: **knowing is held structure that frozen structure lets accumulate and burning structure pays for.**

## 3. Layers and governance, from the same spine (Chad, t1946)

Each step below follows from the one before it. Each keeps its existing label and states where it fails.

1. **Persistence needs a flux, and where lateness is costly a model pays** (Theorem P; GENERAL_THEOREM §1). Models cost upkeep, reads and error (Axiom 5).
2. **When many knowers face a common driver, one shared model can be cheaper than many copies.** T3a (`theory/THEOREMS.md`; **PROOF, elementary; a sufficient condition, not a cutoff**): with N facts sharing one driver at drift ratio ρ = q_g/q_u, nesting is cheaper whenever √N·√(2cwq_u)·[√N(√(1+ρ) − 1) − √ρ] > h. At h = 0 this is N > N\* = (√(1+ρ)+1)²/ρ. In the formal layer, the level's sensing class sets the onset exponent (B3, B9 PROVED (in-model)), and shared drift centralizes while private drift decentralizes (C1 PROVED (in-model)). **A level is a shared model of a common driver** (THEORY §6). **Fails:** with no shared drift, nesting loses by its upkeep (T3 "What fails"). The bound is also not a cutoff below N\*, and it is an approximation when the driver can be seen only through the facts.
3. **Levels can run their own clocks, but that buys little and needs separated time scales.** T3b (**KNOWN**; Roundy 1985): per-level clocks save at most 1 − 1/√2 ≈ 29% for two levels. T3c (**NUMERIC; principle KNOWN**: Simon–Ando, Tikhonov, Borkar): decoupled nesting is near-optimal only when the fast level is much faster than the slow level's memory, and it loses to flat when the ordering of time scales collapses or inverts. Higher levels track slower causes (Simon 1962, cited in THEORY §10; Kiebel et al. 2008, in litmap and the 12 Nov post). **Fails:** collapsed time scales.
4. **The architecture is self-similar ("fractal") only where the world is.** T7 (**PROOF (conditional); the unconditional claim FAILS**). Self-similarity needs scale-free drift, power-law costs and the same loss form at every level. The optimal architecture "inherits whatever scale structure the world has". Testable forms (i)–(iii) in `theory/THEOREMS.md` T7. **Flag:** T7(iii)'s level count "grows as L^{2/3}" predates THEORY v1.4. The (log-span)^{2/3} layer count is withdrawn, and the store count is now ∝ ln(span)·ln(stakes/upkeep) away from α = 2 (K12 MATH-CORRECTED; THEORY §0, "What changed in v1.4" item 3). T7(iii)'s count should carry that pointer (§6 P8). **Fails:** characteristic times (mean reversion, seasons, regime lengths) or fixed setup costs. Human calendars are not evidence, because they are astronomical in origin.
5. **The whole is a knower like its parts only under strict conditions.** MATH_ATTACK §3.4 (**PROOF + NUMERIC**): a representative agent exists only under a Gorman-type condition (c_i/(w_iσ_i²) equal across parts). Otherwise the penalty for running one aggregate clock is unbounded (51× in random draws, 500× in a constructed case). MATH_ATTACK §3.6: form invariance under coarse-graining holds iff C1–C4 hold, and **the strong form ("any population of agents obeying the objective is itself such an agent") is FALSE**. So "the same pattern at every level" is a claim about *relations measured level by level* (J6 OPEN), not about wholes copying parts. **Fails:** heterogeneous costs, non-fungible buffers, heterogeneous fix times, correlated shocks.
6. **Shared memory is a commons, and parts under-watch it.** MATH_ATTACK §3.4 (**PROOF**): if the whole loses Λ_whole when one part fails but each part counts only Λ_i, the parts' watching is too low by a factor √(Λ_i/Λ_whole). Lee, Flack & Krakauer 2024 (PRIOR ART): environmental stabilization is a public good invaded by free riders unless its benefits are monopolized.
7. **So governance appears where shared memory is a public good.** J9 (**OPEN**; THEORY §6): "No one *needs* to be in charge *when* maintaining shared memory is privately rewarded … Otherwise expect enforcement: monitors, sanctions, owners or hierarchy." J8 ("no one in charge, at every level") stays **WITHDRAWN**. Fractal vigilance is **PRIOR ART** qualitatively (J7: Beer's Viable System Model; Mintzberg). Its quantitative version is PROVED (in-model) (E6) and untested on a valid instrument (H8 OPEN). **HYPOTHESIS (THEORY §6):** the staleness of shared memory rises with users per maintainer, and enforcement structures appear where that ratio is high. **Fails:** if enforcement appears just as often where shared memory is privately rewarded, or staleness does not rise with users per maintainer.
8. **Light delay forces federation.** LAT-1 (**N27 OPEN**; the bound is elementary): with hold-last-value reads, a tracker of extent L holds a target only if q(L/c)^{α−1} is below tolerance, so L_max = c(D\*/q)^{1/(α−1)}. Big structures federate: the top level tracks only slow drift. **Fails:** if at fixed q the share of central control does not depend on latency ℓ (SCALE_ENDS §5 kill). Predictors that extrapolate (α > 2) soften the penalty.

**How this follows from the spine.** The second law forces upkeep. Upkeep makes duplicated models wasteful, so shared drivers get shared models (levels). Shared models are commons, so they need enforcement unless use rewards upkeep privately. Signal speed caps how far one model can reach, so large wholes federate. Each step is an economy of knowing under a physically forced budget. None of it adds a new physical principle.

**Where it reaches down and up.** Below the cell, the first "level" in this sense would be a compartment shared by cooperating replicators. That is listed in litmap as a major-transitions theme (Maynard Smith & Szathmáry 1995; classic, not fetched; not relied on here). Above cities, LAT-1 and the horizon cap end the ladder (SCALE_ENDS §5; Olson 2022).

## 7. Open questions

1. **The G2 mapping.** Is the reserve/income/action mapping of Theorem P onto a non-replicating dissipative structure sound, especially A1 (exogenous world) when the structure alters its own feed? This needs an independent mathematical referee.
2. **An operational line between K1 and K2.** Is there a pre-data test that says whether a variable is "exogenous" for a structure (hurricane and sea-surface temperature; a cell's internal pH)? Kolchinsky & Wolpert's scrambling intervention is the candidate. Its cost and feasibility for chemical systems are not assessed.
3. **A persistence filter as selection.** Can sequential selection or survivorship be given a resolution ε (Axiom 7) so that weak design predictions apply to planets or early chemistry? Nothing published was found here.
4. **Store density versus readability.** SCALE_ENDS's open item SCALE-ENDS-STORE-READ: can the budget plus locality derive that the densest stores are the least readable, instead of borrowing it from gravity?

This file supports "the same costs at every level" as accounting, and places the physics in the spine (flux, floors, the entropy gap).
