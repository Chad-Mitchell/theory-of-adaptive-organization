The question, fixed before the search. Does any public dataset, or a feasible experiment that needs no account, support a comparison that

- (a) can come out the other way,
- (b) has qualification decidable before the design is read, because selection strength and population size are known or set in the protocol,
- (c) has the world's statistics set or measured independently of the design,
- (d) makes a call on which this cost and the closest rival disagree?

The rivals named for (d) are Kussell–Leibler bet-hedging, efficient coding, rate–distortion, Kelly, and standard optimal foraging. The cost structure that would have to do the separating is memory against read rate against error against ruin, including a layer count. Contamination and access are scored beside (a)–(d). A row qualifies only when (a), (b), (c), and (d) are all yes. Partial is not yes.

(b) is scored against both bars. The operational bar is the one in the question: population size and selection strength set in the protocol before the design is read. The five-part bar is FRAMEWORK §9 item 1: a cost-to-fitness map σ, a population proxy N_e, a band ε = 10/(σ N_e), power against a named rival, and a redesign time T_red with at least ten such times in one world. A protocol can pass the operational bar and still fail the five-part bar. Both are recorded. No row passes both, together with (d).

## 1. Where the mathematics differs, and where it does not

These calls are already in the repository. Nothing below is a new derivation. They are the filter for (d).

**Shared with the rivals, so a deposit that only records these cannot separate the cost.**

- Kussell and Leibler (doi `10.1126/science.1114383`, Crossref 200, issued 2005) state that the optimal phenotypic switching rates mimic the statistics of environmental changes, and that stochastic switching can be favored over sensing when the environment changes infrequently. Acar, Mettetal, and van Oudenaarden (doi `10.1038/ng.110`, Crossref 200, issued 2008) compare fast and slow switchers against environmental periods. That comparison is theirs.
- Rivoire and Leibler (doi `10.1007/s10955-011-0166-2`, Crossref 200, issued 2011, title "The Value of Information for Populations in Varying Environments") price the value of a cue. Sensing when the cue is cheap and informative, and not sensing when it is not, is their call.
- Geisel (doi `10.1371/journal.pone.0027033`, Crossref 200, issued 2011) and Dekel and Alon (doi `10.1038/nature03842`, Crossref 200, issued 2005) give a growth-optimal expression level, constitutive or responsive, from a measured cost and benefit of one protein. One coordinate of expression is not a layer count.
- The square-root lifetime \(T^\star=\sqrt{6k/(w\sigma^2)}\) is Chalkley's formula, already marked KNOWN in CORE. It is not a separating prediction against the economics of a lumpy reset. CORE also says the Brownian formula is quantitatively false when the gap mean-reverts on the scale of \(T^\star\). A telegraph environment is that case. Matching a switching rate to an environmental rate is Kussell–Leibler, not this square root.
- An antitone cloud, and excess over the scalar Gauss–Markov relaxation, are not a miss of the achievable front (GF5). The joint memory-and-read corner stays open (Shannon note §3, efficiency 0.9574 on one grid point). The interiority review is the same warning on a real deposit.
- With ruin slack, "spend does not fall as the stake rises" is any optimizer of \(S+wE\), including Sims. `theory/PRICE_FREE_2026-11-21.md` already says so. Standard optimal foraging has its own reversal when a starvation threshold binds (Stephens; Caraco, Martindale, and Whittam; Dall and Johnstone, credited there). The sign of that reversal is not free to be claimed again.

**Separating, and absent from every deposit below.**

1. **Kelly against risk-neutral expected currency.** On the even-money menu in `theory/GROWTH_BRIDGE.md` §3, win probability 0.6, stake \(f\in[0,0.99]\), factor \(W=1+f\xi\) with \(\xi=\pm 1\): expected offspring is maximized at the corner \(f=0.99\); the growth rate is maximized at \(f^\star=0.2\), and \(\lambda(0.2)=0.020135513551>\lambda(0.99)\). The argmaxes differ by 0.79. Those digits are the growth-bridge check, not a new computation. CT42, cited there: with a shared shock, lineage growth picks the mixture \(q^\star=0.26\), and expected offspring picks the pure lean design. On the published Lundberg path, \(\tau_S=0.236207526722\) and \(\tau_E=0.316227766017\). Kelly is the closest rival. The two objectives disagree. Kussell–Leibler is the growth-optimal mixture for phenotypes, so a population that fixes the Kelly point agrees with them and disagrees with risk-neutral expected currency. The menu has no layers.

2. **A priced layer count against one store per mode.** When decrements are non-increasing, the from-0 count is \(K^\star_0(R)=\#\{K\ge 1: R\,d_K>1\}\) with \(R=w E_\infty/h\) (CORE; `theory/LAYER_CONVEXITY.md`). A large upkeep \(h\) leaves \(K^\star\) strictly below the world's predictive degree. Kussell–Leibler do not have this ratio: the phenotype count tracks the environmental states. Efficient coding and sequential rate–distortion, with no price per layer, give a filter or a rate, not a count that jumps when \(h\) moves. The caveat is the closest rival. If replication falls linearly with \(hK+wE\), Kelly growth and this cost pick the same \(K^\star\). The count then separates both of them from mode-matching and from an unpriced code. It does not separate the cost from Kelly.

Columns that would be required, and that were not found together. For the stake menu: the payoff table, \(N\), the mutation rate, and the evolved stake or mixture, fixed as a distribution rather than as a single fitness number. For the layer count: the spectrum, \(h\), and \(w\) in the same units as replication, \(N\), the mutation rate, and the count of stores used. A task score, a mutational neighborhood, a switching rate, or an expression level does not stand in for either column.

### Why (d) is no on the rows that otherwise look close

S1, S2, S3, and S16 record a switcher, or a switching rate, against an environmental schedule. Kussell and Leibler predict that comparison. The deposits do not record a re-forming cost varied at fixed schedule, and they do not record a layer count. S3 sets the initial population at \(10^6\) and sets the periods at or above the switching rate, which is the operational half of (b), and still has no σ in units of \(J\) and no \(T_\mathrm{red}\) fixed before the frequencies.

S4 measures how long lac proteins keep a cell from paying a lag when lactose returns. That lifetime is dilution of those proteins. It is not \(T^\star\) under a price the experimenter moved. The schedule is independent of the design. The design is the wild-type network, not a population of alternatives with a selection coefficient set in advance, so (b) is no.

S5 and S6 move one expression level. Geisel's growth calculation and Dekel and Alon's measured cost and benefit already predict that level. There is no second layer, no separate read price, and no ruin coordinate.

S7 and S8 record mutations, fitness, and variance of growth. The LTEE transfer regime is a designed bottleneck, which is why (b) is partial rather than no, and the columns are not memory, read rate, error, or a layer count. S8's abstract describes genetic and environmental variance, not a judgment design.

S9 sets the cap, the mutation rate, the period, and whether sensing exists. The on-versus-off contrast is the sensing comparison in Kussell and Leibler and in Rivoire and Leibler. The published columns are which logic functions are expressed and how ancestry changes. They are not \(K^\star(R)\) and not a refresh time against \(h\).

S10 sets the cap, the mutation rate, and the switching schedule. The recorded object is which mutants are one or two steps away, and whether that neighborhood reflects environments the population has seen. That is an evolvability question. It is not a count of memory stores against a per-store price. The cap 22,500 and the mutation rate 0.001 are from the preprint HTML of the same title. The journal PDF was not opened, so those two configuration numbers are not asserted of the journal version. The journal record was used for the title, the authors, and the existence of the digital-evolution experiment.

S11's task requires a stored bit. Any account that keeps a fact when the fact pays, including the hold rule, makes that call. The configuration table inside the deposit was not opened, so no population number is stated.

S12 selects by hand. S13 sets a large population and scores diversity of patterns. The genotype writes the Lenia rule, so the world is not exogenous, and quality-diversity is not a selection strength on \(J\).

S14 is a table of image-classification cells and their accuracy. There is no exogenous drift spectrum. The closest comparison a user of the table can make is accuracy at a compute budget. That is not a layer count against a drift spectrum, and evolution strategies that query the table are searching that accuracy. Population-based training of recurrent unroll length was not given a Crossref work in the queries run here, and no public log of unroll length against a measured gap variance was found. It is not a row.

S15's description says fluctuating temperature selects for plasticity or bet-hedging according to cue autocorrelation. That is the standard split. The deposit does not add a layer price.

## 3. The two designs that would separate, and why they are not rows

Neither has been run. Neither is a deposit. A run would be a new preregistration. Writing the configuration would not establish the hypothesis outside that configuration. T30 already says a designed fitness is an in-model or lab-condition result.

**D1. Stake menu, against Kelly.** Implement the growth-bridge menu in a public population (Avida is MIT-licensed; `https://github.com/devosoft/avida` returned HTTP 200) or in any Wright–Fisher program whose payoff table is the file. Do not put \(J\) in by hand as the fitness and then ask whether selection found it. Offspring is \(W=1+f\xi\) as in §1. Before any genome is read, fix \(N\), the mutation rate, the box on \(f\), and the win probability.

- This cost, read as risk-neutral expected currency, puts the stake at \(f=0.99\).
- Kelly puts it at \(f^\star=0.2\).
- Kussell–Leibler, on a bet menu, is the growth solution, so they sit with Kelly.
- A binding ruin cap is a third call. The published path separates \(\tau_S\) from \(\tau_E\). A preregistration would name which of the three is the prediction. This note does not.

(a) yes: the points are 0.79 apart on \(f\). (b) yes on the operational bar: \(N\) and the payoff gap are in the file, and the gap is computable before the run. The five-part σ is 1 only if someone declares fitness to be the cost, which collapses (d). (c) yes: the coin is the world. (d) yes against Kelly, the closest rival. The menu has no memory, no read rate, and no layer. It is the ruin half of the question.

Columns: the payoff file, \(N\), the mutation rate, and the distribution of \(f\) after a preregistered number of generations.

**D2. Layer count against a per-layer cycle cost.** Fix a world with a known list of timescales in the environment file. Charge \(h\) CPU cycles per extra register and \(w\) merit per unit tracking error, both in replication units. Leave the organism's replication as the native fitness.

- This cost puts \(K^\star\) at the from-0 count for \(R=w E_\infty/h\). At large \(h\), \(K^\star\) is below the number of timescales.
- One phenotype per rewarded state, and a full-degree filter with no layer price, keep \(K\) equal to that number at every \(h\).
- Kelly, if replication falls linearly with \(hK+wE\), picks the same count as this cost. Against Kelly, (d) is no. Against mode-matching and against an unpriced efficient code, (d) is yes, and Kelly is the closer rival whenever the cycle cost is inside replication.

Columns: the environment file (spectrum, \(h\), \(w\), \(N\), mutation rate) and the number of registers executed in the evolved programs. Not the task score.

D2 is the layer half of the question. It does not, by itself, answer Kelly. D1 answers Kelly and does not answer layers. No deposit in §2 is either design. A feasible no-account experiment therefore exists only as a protocol. It does not qualify as a dataset, and it is not registered here.
