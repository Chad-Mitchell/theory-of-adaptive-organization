## Answer in plain words

**Yes, in this class and under this cap.** With the memory's total exit rate capped at \(g\), no memory with up to four states, nonlinear or world-dependent or asymmetric, was found that pays less than the symmetric two-state copy at the same error, and the copy pays strictly more than CORE's floor \(kT\,r\,L(\varepsilon)\): by 0.16% (\(g/r=1000\), \(\varepsilon=0.3\)) to 66% (\(g/r=5\), \(\varepsilon=0.2\)) over the 13 grid points, about 1.6% to 6.7% at \(g/r=100\), and falling as \(1/g\). The outcome is Y of `PREREG.md` section 3: B1, B2 and B3 hit. This is one-sided: it is the best of a search over \(n\le4\) jump memories, and a search cannot prove that no better design exists. The result is a floor-side statement about what the best designs searched cost, in a toy class, not a statement about a real system.

## Search quality (reported, changes no bar)

The reproducibility flag counts designs within \(10^{-6}\) relative of the best at each \((n,\text{point})\). Arm A: \(n=2\) found the closed-form optimum 20 to 21 times of 21 at every point; \(n=3\) 7 to 143 of 151; \(n=4\) 1 to 49 of 52. **Flagged `search-limited` (fewer than 3):** \(n=4\) at \((1000,0.2)\) (2 designs) and \((1000,0.3)\) (1 design). At those two points the \(n=4\) best-found excess is 0.002171 and 0.001577, above the two-state values 0.002167 and 0.001575, so the \(n=4\) search there did not even reach the embedded two-state design; the \(n=3\) and \(n=2\) values equal the two-state excess. This is the one-sidedness of the search, not a scored miss. Arm B: \(n=4\) found its best 12 to 14 times of 52, \(n=3\) 63 to 75 of 151. The \(n=4\) start count was cut from 100 to 25 per class (`DEVIATIONS.md`, 28 Nov 2026 12:20 AM ET), so \(n=4\) evidence is weaker than the PREREG planned.

## What is known, what is new

- **Known on `main`** (not claimed here): the telegraph floor and that it is a limit, approached only as the rates diverge (`CORE.md:74`; `theory/GENERAL_FORM_PHYSICS_2026-11-22.md:66`, `:140`, `:171`). So a strict excess for the two-state copy at finite rate is a CONSISTENCY_CHECK, as the PREREG labeled it.
- **What this run adds, in the class:** (i) under a total exit-rate cap, no \(n\le4\) design found beats the symmetric copy (\(Q_{\rm frac}=0\)), so the copy's excess is the best excess found, not a feature of the two-state family alone; (ii) the excess scales as \(1/G\) over \(G=100\) to \(1000\) at all four errors; (iii) a per-edge cap does not give a speed cost at all, because redundant states multiply speed (51% to 56% of the excess removed already at \(n=4\)).
- **Not shown:** that no design with \(n\ge5\) or continuous state beats the copy; that the exit cap is the right physical speed cap (it is an assumption, see PREREG section 6); anything about a real system.

## Assumptions added (all of them; full list in `PREREG.md` section 6)

Dissipative bipartite jump memory with local detailed balance; cap on the total exit rate of every memory state, per world state (arm A) or per edge (arm B); every rate at least \(10^{-9}G\); Hamming error of the best decoder in the steady state; \(n\le4\) states; symmetric telegraph only; no cost for reading the decoder; local, one-sided search (SLSQP, fixed start counts, master seed 20261127, \(n=4\) random starts 25 per class). The stopping rule was fixed counts; the run completed with no early stop.
