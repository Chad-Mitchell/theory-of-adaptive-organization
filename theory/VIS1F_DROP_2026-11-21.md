## 2. (h, w) for dipteran photoreceptors

FRAMEWORK §3.5: upkeep h is "the existence cost per tracker per unit time, independent of reading." At cell scale, the operationalization is "expression and maintenance cost of a receptor or pathway (protein burden), from fitness cost of expressing it in a constant environment." FRAMEWORK §3.3: stakes w is "the cost per unit time per unit of loss of tracking error," estimated from "the outcome cost as a function of tracking error, from natural or designed variation in error (an error-cost curve)." A GTAO cutoff or count may be computed only from (h, w) "independently sourced or preregistered from sources that are not the measured kernels, cutoffs, or layer counts being predicted" (shortlist §5). Both prices are required, in commensurate units, because a priced design depends on the ratio or on both.

### 2.1 Metabolic costs that were opened (not w; only a partial stand-in for h)

**Laughlin, de Ruyter van Steveninck & Anderson 1998**, *Nat. Neurosci.*, doi 10.1038/236 (Crossref issued 1998-05; works record verified this session). Author PDF from `https://www.princeton.edu/~wbialek/our_papers/laughlin+al_98.pdf`, 97,243 bytes, SHA-256 `df7c2e2476daf60d5ceda07fce160c291f8be836196fb277be6852751a7359c0`. Blowfly (*Calliphora*) retina.

Opened numbers (superscripts in the PDF are written here in ordinary notation):

- Five photoreceptors stimulated at 10^6 effective photons per receptor per second: mean membrane potential E_m = −33 ± 6 mV, mean input resistance 7.1 ± 1.8 MΩ, mean total conductance 141 nS.
- From those electrical data and a membrane model: ATP consumption 7.5 × 10^9 ATP molecules per second.
- Information rate under those fully light-adapted conditions: 1,000 bits per second (the paper notes that natural signals contain correlations not present in the random stimuli, and expects lower rates under natural conditions).
- Metabolic cost: 7 × 10^6 ATP molecules per bit, stated as a lower bound.
- Chemical synapse: 2 × 10^4 to 6 × 10^4 ATP molecules per bit, at 55 bits per second; 240 vesicles released per second; about 2 × 10^5 ATP molecules per synapse per second.
- Abstract: 10^4 ATP molecules per bit at a chemical synapse, and 10^6–10^7 ATP for graded signals in an interneuron or a photoreceptor, or for spike coding.

**Niven, Anderson & Laughlin 2007**, *PLoS Biol.*, doi 10.1371/journal.pbio.0050116 (Crossref issued 2007-03-20; works record verified this session). Publisher printable PDF, 721,474 bytes, SHA-256 `d806c8d58d1397b9a3a7420ca3e404d17051bd3bc8642845e4d8be8c529a8f06`. Homologous R1–6 photoreceptors of *Drosophila melanogaster*, *D. virilis*, *Calliphora vicina*, and *Sarcophaga carnaria*, driven by the same stimuli.

Opened numbers:

- Dark (fixed) cost: the rate at which ATP is hydrolysed to maintain the resting potential in the dark; "like the rent on a factory, is paid at a fixed rate, irrespective of output." This fixed cost is about 20% of a photoreceptor's maximum consumption.
- Dark consumption: approximately 2 × 10^9 ATP molecules s^−1 in *Calliphora* and *Sarcophaga* R1–6; approximately twenty times less, 1 × 10^8 ATP molecules s^−1, in *D. melanogaster* R1–6. The paper attributes the species difference primarily to dark input resistance (more than an order of magnitude), not to the (up to 10 mV) differences in dark resting potential.
- Highest information rates at bright daylight levels: 197 ± 31 bits s^−1 (n = 26) in *D. melanogaster* to about 1,000 bits s^−1 in *S. carnaria*. At the brightest stimuli reported: *Calliphora* 955 ± 70 bits s^−1 (n = 3), *Sarcophaga* 1,130 ± 67 (n = 11), *D. virilis* 512 ± 26 (n = 21), and *D. melanogaster* 197 ± 31 bits s^−1 (n = 26).
- Signalling cost is defined as total cost minus dark cost. Unit cost (ATP per bit) falls as information rate rises, because the fixed dark cost is spread over more bits. Comparing species, fixed cost, total signalling cost, and cost per bit all increase with a photoreceptor's highest information rate (a diminishing-returns pattern in the paper's words).

What these numbers can and cannot be. Niven's dark cost is an existence cost in ATP per unit time, incurred with no light-driven signalling, and is not taken from a measured kernel, cutoff, or layer count. In that narrow sense it is a candidate metabolic stand-in for h, in ATP units, and it is species-specific (*Drosophila* versus *Calliphora*). It is **not** FRAMEWORK §3.5's operationalization: no fitness cost of expressing the pathway in a constant environment was opened. Laughlin's and Niven's *cost per bit* and light-adapted total ATP rates are computed from information rates and, in Laughlin, from signal and noise spectra of the same cells whose bandwidth and kernels would be the predicted design. Those figures are therefore not used here as an independent price for a cutoff or a count.

Laughlin 1998 and Niven 2007 do not report an error-cost curve, a mating-success or survival cost as a function of tracking error, or any other stakes w. They measure the metabolic cost of coding, which is a sensing or upkeep price, not the cost of being wrong.

### 2.2 Stakes w were not sourced

A literature search and the opened full texts did not produce a published error-cost curve for dipteran photoreceptor or LMC tracking error (fitness, survival, mating success, or foraging return as a continuous function of visual error, or of cutoff, or of kernel time constants).

One related full text was opened, and is not used as w. **Markow & Manning 1980**, *Behav. Neural Biol.* 29: 276–280, doi 10.1016/s0163-1047(80)90612-3 (Crossref issued 1980-06; works record verified this session). Author PDF from `https://labs.biology.ucsd.edu/markow/documents/Matingsuccessofphotoreceptormutants.pdf`, 277,499 bytes, SHA-256 `bf63182761fb5470bc4cd41458140f07279f10cd51b126ac6d36f195e3ac259c`. Female-choice experiments in *D. melanogaster*: Canton-S wild-type males compete with photoreceptor mutants that eliminate function in R1–6, in R7, or (norpA) the receptor potential of the entire eye. The paper reports that an intact visual system, especially R1–6, is important for male mating success, with a small but significant role for R7 when R1–6 are absent. That is presence-or-absence of receptor classes, not an error-cost curve versus tracking error or versus a filter cutoff. FRAMEWORK §3.3 lists "Loss measured only at the extremes" as a failure mode for stakes. Table 1 percentages are not converted into a w.

Matthew Cobb, "VISION COSTS," *J. Exp. Biol.* 210(15): vii (2007), doi 10.1242/jeb.001040 (Crossref issued 2007-08-01; works record verified): the publisher PDF URL returned HTTP 403 (Cloudflare HTML challenge, 5,812 bytes). The file was not opened and is not used. This is not the Niven, Anderson & Laughlin 2007 source; that remains the *PLoS Biol.* paper, doi 10.1371/journal.pbio.0050116, opened in §2.1.

No other no-account full text was found that supplies a defensible, independently sourced w in units that can be placed in a ratio with h. Inventing a mapping from ATP per bit, or from mutant mating-success extremes, onto w is not permitted.

**Therefore (h, w) cannot both be sourced independently of the design.** A metabolic stand-in for h in ATP units can be quoted from Niven's dark cost. Stakes w cannot. The (h, w) side of the drop rule fires. Pass 1 stops. This is not a test of the hypothesis, because a price-free structural claim can be fitted to the data.

## 3. L3, finite K*, and a priced cutoff

THEORY L3 / CT21 (THEORY line 81): "A continuum of time scales has no exact finite design (L3)." Lemma B2 (THEORY B.3, line 347), for a Gaussian world or the best linear tracker with white observation noise of density σ_n²: error q^{1/α} σ_n^{2(1−1/α)} / sin(π/α); memory time ∝ (σ_n²/q)^{1/α} (Wiener/Kalman bandwidth); precision effort with a linear price ∝ w^{α/(2α−1)} q^{1/(2α−1)}; "the number of stores grows with the span of time scales and with stakes over upkeep away from α = 2 (rate conjectural)." CT12's exact-reset 1/α rule is already out of scope for continuous photoreceptors and LMCs unless a discrete exact-reset read is defined and α lies strictly inside (1, 3); that arm stays dropped, as the shortlist required.

The shortlist already recorded, from the fetched van Hateren 1997 paper and without treating the phrase as a fitted pole, that the published intensity series "behave approximately as 1/f^γ … γ here close to 1," with a mild deviation below 0.1–1 Hz. No official Pass 1 fit, and no corners-versus-continuum model-selection rule applied to traces, was run in this session. Under that already-recorded description, and under L3, a finite K* is not defined and is not invented. FRAMEWORK §9 item 8 remains a placeholder; it was not finalized here because the candidate is being dropped, not locked.

A priced frequency cutoff is the fallback design quantity "only if (h, w) have been sourced independently and a cutoff is well-defined under the priced continuous-sensing problem (Lemma B2 / CT21)." That fails on both parts.

- (h, w) were not both sourced (§2).
- Lemma B2's memory time is set by observation-noise density and by the world, not by upkeep h. SN1 already recorded that in *Drosophila*, photon shot noise is exogenous. That Model S is the right noise class is an inference from that SN1 record, not a quotation from it. With σ_n exogenous, that Kalman bandwidth is not priced by w or by h. It is the SNR-determined bandwidth that efficient-coding / whitening already uses. Filter *shape* agreeing with whitening, with no priced deviation, is a collapse into efficient coding, not a pass for the hypothesis (shortlist §5 item 6).
- Lemma B2's "precision effort" is a spend quantity, not a frequency cutoff.
- A cutoff obtained by paying h per store on a continuum, and stopping when the marginal error reduction no longer covers h/w, would be a priced deviation from whitening *if* it were a proved, well-defined rule and if (h, w) existed. Lemma B2 itself marks the growth of store count with stakes over upkeep as "rate conjectural." L3 says there is no exact finite design. A previously locked in-model water-filling state-count rule (OPEN_WORK T25, WF-HSV) already failed; that is already on the record and is not re-scored here. This note does not invent a conjectural cutoff in order to keep the candidate alive.
- Van Hateren's "γ here close to 1" puts α at or outside the open interval on which the B2 error formula is stated (sin(π/α) vanishes as α → 1). The continuous-sensing problem is therefore not in the proved regime for this world, even before prices.

**Therefore L3 leaves both a finite K* and a priced cutoff undefined.** The second side of the drop rule also fires.
