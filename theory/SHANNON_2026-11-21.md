# What Shannon did, and what it means for the cost of judgment

*Saturday 21 November 2026, first written about 1:45 PM ET (America/New_York), beside `main` `2c32ade`; revised the same afternoon after the independent review (`records/reviews/shannon_2026-11-21/REVIEW.md`, verdict NOT READY). Checks: `theory/shannon_checks/shannon_checks.py` (output `shannon_checks.out`; run by `tools/test_shannon_checks.py`). Labels: **PROVED** (a proof is given here or cited, inside the stated model), **KNOWN** (published, credited), **NUMERICAL** (computed, not proved), **ANALOGY**, **OPEN**. Proved in-model says nothing by itself about nature. This revision has not yet been re-reviewed.*

## 0. Bottom line, in plain words

1. **Shannon's theory was decisive without experiments because it proved limits, not tendencies.** It had four parts: a quantity defined by the task, a converse (no scheme does better), achievability (the limit can be approached), and a formula for the limit computed from the source or channel statistics alone, whatever the hardware. Whether real systems come near the limit was a separate, later, empirical question.
2. **The cost of judgment has a clean converse for its information terms.** For any design, in a stationary world it does not control, J is at least a floor computed from the world's statistics and the prices: memory must pay for the predictive information it holds (CT1/CT4, already in the repository), and reads must pay for at least the sequential rate–distortion rate of the accuracy achieved (Tatikonda, Sahai & Mitter 2004; Tanaka et al. 2017). There is no proved layer charge: a memoryless read has K = 0 and still carries information. **PROVED** for the information floor (§2). The ingredients are KNOWN; the assembly into one floor on J is ours, and elementary.
3. **Achievability is partial.** In a scalar Gauss–Markov world the information floor is attained exactly when memory is free (the Kalman filter with tuned read noise) or when reads are free (a noisy store); with both priced, the best one-layer design has efficiency J_floor/J = 0.9574 (gap 1 − J_floor/J = 4.26%; the design costs 4.45% more than the floor) on the grid tested (layer price left out of both sides). **PROVED** at the two edges (KNOWN results); **NUMERICAL** in between (§3).
4. **There is no single number like capacity. There is a single object: the world's judgment frontier,** the set of (memory, reads, layers, accuracy) any design can reach in that world. It depends on the world and the read channel only, not on prices. The inner-product dual J\*(p) = inf ⟨p, z⟩ equals −σ_ℱ(−p), where σ is the support function of the frontier. It equals the operational least cost only when some minimizer has read cost A = c·r and linking cost L = 0. J\* is concave and positively homogeneous. Knowing J\* on the positive orthant recovers the free-disposal hull of the Pareto face of conv ℱ_W, not the convex hull itself, and not dominated points (**PROVED**, standard convex analysis, §4). Two numbers summarize a world: the loss judgment could remove at best, and the value of the first nat.
5. **The efficiency measure that needs no prices is Farrell's technical efficiency:** how far a system's resources could shrink and still deliver its accuracy. For the input cost C = κI + c·r, the part of J that scales with those inputs, cost efficiency C_min/C splits into technical × allocative (Farrell 1957). That identity is not true of J_floor/J, because wD and an unscaled h do not shrink with the radial factor. **PROVED** for this C. A design that minimizes the linear cost at strictly positive prices is undominated, so θ = 1 on that feasible set (**PROVED**, §5). With a convex feasible set the converse also holds. So **the price-free content of the hypothesis is "selected systems sit on their world's frontier."**
6. Palmer et al. 2015 test efficient coding of predictive information. Mattingly et al. 2021 test a chemotaxis bound set by information the cell acquires (within a factor of two, their abstract). Both are consistent with the technical half. Neither was preregistered here, and neither qualifies under THEORY §3.
7. **What this note adds to the causal rate–distortion program is a price per layer and the ruin cap, both still without a formula for the limit.** Lateness is already the reason the rate is sequential (Neuhoff & Gilbert 1982; Tatikonda, Sahai & Mitter 2004).

## 1. Shannon's four moves, mapped

| Shannon | What it did | GTAO counterpart | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operational quantity | Capacity C (largest reliable rate); R(D) (fewest bits for distortion D), defined by the task | J\* = min J over designs; and the frontier ℱ_W (§4) | Definition |
| Converse | No code beats C or R(D) | J(𝒟) ≥ J_floor for every design (§2) | **PROVED** for the information terms; no proved h·1[I > 0] term (K = 0 is allowed) |
| Achievability | Long random codes approach the limit | Kalman / noisy-store designs attain the information floor at the edges; efficiency J_floor/J = 0.9574 (gap 1 − J_floor/J = 4.26%; the design costs 4.45% more than the floor) in between (§3) | **PROVED** at edges (KNOWN), **NUMERICAL** in between; multi-scale **OPEN** |
| Formula from statistics | C = max I(X;Y); R(D) = min I(X; X̂) | Floor from the predictive-information curve, the sequential rate–distortion function and the prices | **PROVED** where those functions are known (Gauss–Markov closed form, in nats) |
| Universality | Same limit for any physical channel | Same information floor for any substrate with the same world, read channel and prices; Landauer floors the erasure part of κ, and Sagawa & Ueda 2009 floor the sum of measurement and erasure work, not a separate read price stacked on that floor | **PROVED** in-model for the information floor; whether real systems approach it is the empirical question |

One difference matters. Shannon's achievability uses long blocks, so delay is free. A system acting ahead cannot wait: "lateness counts as error." The right limits are therefore the **causal (sequential)** ones, not classical rate–distortion, and they are generally higher (Neuhoff & Gilbert 1982 for causal source codes; Tatikonda et al. 2004). This is where the lateness term of J enters the information side. **KNOWN.**

## 2. The converse (PROVED): information floor

**Setting.** THEORY Appendix B standing assumptions (A1) exogenous world, (A2) stationary and ergodic under the policy, plus: the design reads Y_t through a channel from X, holds memory M_t that is a causal function of Y^t (and internal randomness), and acts A_t = g(M_t) on X_{t+k} (lead k ≥ 0). Resources of a design: I_mem = I(M_t; X^t) (nats held about the world's past), r = the directed information rate from X to M per step (Massey 1990), K = number of layers, D = E ℓ(A_t, X_{t+k}). **Assumption S-A:** A ≥ c·r for a price c per nat of directed information. This is an assumption, not a sensor law. Sagawa & Ueda 2009 bound the sum of measurement work and erasure work by kT times the mutual information of that measurement (erratum, 2011). They do not give a second floor c ≥ kT on top of a Landauer floor on κ. L ≥ 0.

**Theorem S1 (information floor).** Under S-A and L ≥ 0, every design satisfies

J(𝒟) ≥ κ I_mem + c·r + w D ≥ min over (I, r) of { κI + c·r + w·max(D_IB(I), D_seq(r)) }.

*Proof.* D ≥ D_IB(I_mem) and D ≥ D_seq(r) by definition of those two functions, so D ≥ max. hK ≥ 0. Minimize over (I, r). ∎

The closed form on the scalar Gauss–Markov world is the same bound along the curve on which the two separate bounds are written in the same P: I = ½ ln(V/P), r = ½ ln(a² + Q/P), D = a^{2k}P + V(1 − a^{2k}), in nats (Tatikonda's log is base 2; the displayed formulae are the nat form). The sum is a lower bound even when no design meets both. World x_{t+1} = a·x_t + n_t, Var n = Q, Var x = V = Q/(1 − a²), squared loss. Let P = mmse(x_t | M_t). Because the future noise is independent of M_t (A1), the lead-k map is the Gauss–Markov calculation above. The cited sequential rate–distortion formula is the rate for mse P of x_t (Tatikonda, Sahai & Mitter 2004; Tanaka et al. 2017):

J ≥ min_P { (κ/2)·ln(V/P) + (c/2)·ln(a² + Q/P) + w·(a^{2k}P + V(1 − a^{2k})) }.

There is no proved h·1[I > 0] term. A memoryless rule M_t = Y_t, K = 0, has I_mem > 0 and undercuts any floor that adds h (example: a = 0.9, Q = 1, k = 2, s² = 0.2, h = 1, κ = c = 0.1, w = 1, J = 2.197 < 3.187). The corrected floor sits at or below that design (checked in `shannon_checks.py`). Directed information is the right causal read currency for this bound (Massey; Tatikonda; Tanaka 2017). It is not, by itself, a physical sensor cost.

The stock/flow split that does survive is: I_mem ≥ ½ ln(V/P) and r ≥ ½ ln(a² + Q/P) may be added, because both are lower bounds for a design that achieves P. Kalman meets the second and misses the first. That is why both prices open a gap.

Checked in `shannon_checks.py` §1–2 and §7 (the information floor is never above any design tried, including the K = 0 memoryless design; Monte Carlo confirms the design-side formulas).

**Physics floor.** Landauer floors the erasure part of κ (`theory/bridge_2026-11-21/FLOOR.md`). Sagawa & Ueda 2009 floor the sum of measurement and erasure work, not a separate read price stacked on that floor (erratum, 2011). S-A remains an assumption, not this theorem. Above the cell, thermodynamic floors are far below real prices (FRAMEWORK §3.11, β_phys ≫ 1). **KNOWN** ingredients.

**What the converse does not cover.** Worlds without a stationary law (random walks, the setting of T1 and much of the theory) are outside S1, as they are outside CT1 (CX1, open; `theory/INTERNAL_MODEL.md` §4 has the data-rate route). No information-theoretic argument forces K ≥ 1. A stronger bound, K ≥ K_min(D), the fewest states that reach accuracy D, is a definition with no formula in general. In Model S it is computable: K_min(D) = min{K : E_K ≤ D}, with E_K from the merged solver (`theory/layers_checks/stores.py`). The ruin cap only shrinks the feasible set, so it can raise the floor, never lower it; no formula for that rise is derived (`theory/bridge_2026-11-21/BRIDGE.md`, missing math). **OPEN.**

## 3. Achievability

- **Memory free (κ = 0, c > 0).** The Kalman filter with the read noise tuned attains the read term exactly: its directed information rate is ½ ln(P_prior/P) = ½ ln(a² + Q/P) (checked to 10⁻⁹, §1 of the checks). Against the information floor (no layer charge), the ratio is 1.0000 on the six simulated κ = 0, c > 0 prices; the three with c = 0 are assigned rather than simulated. **KNOWN** (Tanaka, Kim, Parrilo & Mitter 2017).
- **Reads free (exact reads).** A noisy store M = x_t + noise attains D_IB exactly (CT4; `c6d`). **KNOWN** form, **PROVED** in the repository. In the grid, the twelve c = 0 points set the design cost equal to the information floor rather than simulating that store.
- **Both priced.** The Kalman estimate holds more information about the past than it needs (I(x̂; X^t) > ½ ln(V/P); the excess is its non-predictive memory, the quantity in Still et al. 2012). The best one-layer Kalman design has efficiency J_floor/J = 0.9574 (gap 1 − J_floor/J = 4.26%; the design costs 4.45% more than the floor) at the worst grid point (κ = 1, c = 1, w = 1, layer price left out of both sides) and within 0.3% at w ≥ 10 (worst such point 0.9978). Eighteen of the 36 prices have both κ > 0 and c > 0. One world (a = 0.9, Q = 1), one lead (k = 2). **NUMERICAL.** Whether the true J\* equals the information floor here, or sits strictly between, is **OPEN**. (A layer price charged only to the design, and not to the floor, mixes an information gap with an unpriced upkeep term and is not this 4.26% figure.)
- **Several time scales.** The information terms are attained by the full-order Kalman filter (KNOWN), but that filter needs as many states as the world's predictive degree (CT21). With a price per state, achievability is the keystone question itself: N60 in Model S, conditional on convexity; no predictive rule with rhythms. **OPEN** in general.

## 4. The single quantity: the world's judgment frontier

Define ℱ_W = { (I, r, K, D) reachable by some design in world W with read channel N and lead k }. It contains no prices.

**Theorem S2.** For p = (κ, c, h, w) and J\*(p) = inf_{z ∈ ℱ_W} ⟨p, z⟩, one has J\*(p) = −σ_ℱ_W(−p), where σ is the support function σ(u) = sup_z ⟨u, z⟩. J\* is concave and positively homogeneous. Its one-sided derivative in a price, where it exists, is the optimal amount of that coordinate (envelope). Knowing J\* on the positive orthant recovers the free-disposal hull of the Pareto face of conv ℱ_W. It does not recover dominated points, and it does not recover conv ℱ_W. J\* is that infimum. The operational least cost min(κ I_mem + hK + wD + A + L) equals J\* only if some minimizer has A = c·r and L = 0. S-A gives only A ≥ c·r, so the operational minimum can sit strictly above J\*. The read-versus-loss slice is the rate–cost function of Kostina & Hassibi 2019. **PROVED** (standard convex analysis; CT33's concavity in w is the one-price case). Checked for J_floor: concavity in w, and dJ_floor/dw = D\* of the floor's minimizer (§3 of the checks).

So the analogue of capacity is not a number but a curve, as with rate–distortion or the capacity-cost function. Two numbers summarize a world for one lead k, and neither needs a price:
- **V_pred = D(no model) − D(perfect model)**, the most loss judgment can ever remove. Scalar Gauss–Markov: a^{2k}·V.
- **v₁ = −dD/dI at I = 0**, the loss saved by the first nat. Scalar Gauss–Markov: 2a^{2k}V. At the information floor (no layer charge) a model pays iff κ + c(1 − a²) < w·v₁ (checked at three price pairs, §6 of the checks). This is CT4's threshold with reads added. **PROVED** for the floor; the same threshold for real designs needs the floor to be attained near I = 0, where the numerical gap above is smallest.

## 5. The efficiency measure

For a design 𝒟 with resources z = (I, r) and loss D:
- **Technical efficiency** η_T = θ = min{θ : (θ·z, D) is reachable} (Debreu 1951; Farrell 1957). It needs the frontier, never a price.
- **Input-cost efficiency** η_C = C_min(D)/C(z) for C = κI + c·r, the part of J that scales with those inputs.
- **Allocative efficiency** η_A = η_C/θ.

Then η_C = θ·η_A for this C. It is not true of J_floor/J, because wD and an unscaled h do not shrink with θ. Worked point (`shannon_checks.py` §5): θ = 0.698 is the radial factor onto I ≥ ½ ln(V/P) and r ≥ ½ ln(a² + Q/P). Joint reachability of that corner is the open question in §3, so this θ is a lower bound on Farrell θ. J_floor/J = 0.985, and 0.698 × 0.994 = 0.694 ≠ 0.985. On input cost, C_min/C = 0.685 = θ × 0.982. The algebra C_min/C = θ·η_A, and the failure of the product for J, stand either way.

An output-oriented, one-resource version is what the retina study measured: η_IB = I(M; X_{t+k}) / IB(I(M; X^t)), the predictive information held as a share of the most any memory of that size could hold (Palmer et al. 2015). A cost-per-bit version is Laughlin, de Ruyter van Steveninck & Anderson (1998); the repository's β_phys (FRAMEWORK §3.11) is the inverse of a physics-floor efficiency.

**Theorem S3.** (i) A design that minimizes the linear cost at strictly positive prices is undominated on that feasible set, and therefore θ = 1 on that set. (ii) If the feasible set is convex, every undominated point minimizes the linear cost for some nonzero p ≥ 0. *Proof.* (i) A dominating design would have strictly smaller cost. (ii) Supporting hyperplane. ∎ **PROVED.**

The ruin cap raises the minimum and never lowers it. (i) and (ii) apply to the ruin-feasible set only. θ on the uncapped frontier can be below 1. (ii) needs convexity of that restricted set, which P(ruin) ≤ ε does not give, and ruin is not determined by the mean loss.

So the hypothesis splits cleanly:
- **Technical part (price-free):** selected systems sit near their world's frontier, θ ≈ 1. Measurable from the world's statistics and the system's own resources and accuracy.
- **Allocative part (price ratios):** they sit at the frontier point whose slopes match their prices. The directional predictions in `theory/PRICE_FREE_2026-11-21.md` (CT33 and others) are comparative statics of this part, and they need only signs.

The selection bridge (`theory/bridge_2026-11-21/SELECTION.md`) gives the expected distance: under the linear fitness map and weak mutation, the expected fractional excess of J over J\* is d/(2σN_e). In efficiency terms, 1 − E[J\*/J] ≈ d/(2σN_e) to first order. **PROVED** in-model under its assumptions; not tested on any real class.

|---|---|---|
| Shannon 1948, 1959; Berger 1971 | Capacity, rate–distortion, converse and achievability | Causality (lead k), a price per state, a ruin cap, separate prices for holding and reading |
| Sequential / causal RD: Neuhoff & Gilbert 1982; Tatikonda, Sahai & Mitter 2004; Tanaka et al. 2017; Kostina & Hassibi 2019 | The read-side floor and its Kalman achievability; rate–cost trade-offs in control. Scalar formula older: Gorbunov & Pinsker; Bucy; restated as Tatikonda's scalar result by Stavrou, Tanaka & Tatikonda 2020, doi 10.1109/TAC.2019.2941444 | Holding price κ and layer price h beside the read price; persistence under ruin |
| Bialek, Nemenman & Tishby 2001; Tishby, Pereira & Bialek 1999; Creutzig, Globerson & Tishby 2009; Still et al. 2012; Still 2020 | Predictive information, the predictive bottleneck, its thermodynamic cost | Prices and stakes that pick the operating point; layers; ruin (CT4 already credits these) |
| Sims 2003 (rational inattention) | Expected loss plus a price on information | Several resources with separate prices; structure; ruin |

## 7. What this changes, and what it does not

- It gives the theory a Shannon-shaped core: a definition, a proved information floor, partial achievability, a world-defined frontier and a price-free efficiency measure. The structure results (CT20/CT33, CT21 L1–L3, N60 conditional, CT22/CT25, CT35/CT36, the freeze limit of PR #137, the selection bridge) are then statements about the minimizer on that frontier.
- It does not make the hypothesis true, and it does not lift any NOT A TEST row. It does say what a decisive measurement looks like: the world's frontier from the world's statistics, a system's resources and accuracy, and the efficiency number, with no stakes or upkeep prices needed for the technical part.
- **The most important gap.** The part of J that is the theory's own, the price per layer, has no formula for its limit. Without "the fewest layers that can reach accuracy D in this world," computable from data, the efficiency test can touch the information part (where Bialek's programme already has results) but not the layer part. In Model S it is computable numerically; in general it is **OPEN**.
