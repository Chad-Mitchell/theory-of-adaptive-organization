# What Shannon did, and what it means for the cost of judgment

*Saturday 21 November 2026, written about 1:45 PM ET (America/New_York), beside `main` `2c32ade`. Checks: `theory/shannon_checks/shannon_checks.py` (output `shannon_checks.out`; run by `tools/test_shannon_checks.py`). Labels: **PROVED** (a proof is given here or cited, inside the stated model), **KNOWN** (published, credited), **NUMERICAL** (computed, not proved), **ANALOGY**, **OPEN**. Proved in-model says nothing by itself about nature. Not yet independently reviewed when first written.*

## 0. Bottom line, in plain words

1. **Shannon's theory was decisive without experiments because it proved limits, not tendencies.** It had four parts: a quantity defined by the task, a converse (no scheme does better), achievability (the limit can be approached), and a formula for the limit computed from the source or channel statistics alone, whatever the hardware. Whether real systems come near the limit was a separate, later, empirical question.
2. **The cost of judgment has a clean converse for its information terms.** For any design, in a stationary world it does not control, J is at least a floor computed from the world's statistics and the prices: memory must pay for the predictive information it holds (CT1/CT4, already in the repository), reads must pay for at least the sequential rate–distortion rate of the accuracy achieved (Tatikonda, Sahai & Mitter 2004; Tanaka et al. 2017), and a model needs at least one layer. **PROVED** (§2). The ingredients are KNOWN; the assembly into one floor on J is ours, and elementary.
3. **Achievability is partial.** In a scalar Gauss–Markov world the floor is attained exactly when memory is free (the Kalman filter with tuned read noise) or when reads are free (a noisy store); with both priced, the best one-layer design is within 4.2% of the floor on the grid tested. **PROVED** at the two edges (KNOWN results); **NUMERICAL** in between (§3).
4. **There is no single number like capacity. There is a single object: the world's judgment frontier,** the set of (memory, reads, layers, accuracy) any design can reach in that world. It depends on the world and the read channel only, not on prices. The least cost J\*(prices) is its support function, so frontier and least cost carry the same information (**PROVED**, standard convex duality). Two numbers summarize a world: the loss judgment could remove at best, and the value of the first nat (§4).
5. **The efficiency measure that needs no prices is Farrell's technical efficiency:** how far a system's resources could shrink and still deliver its accuracy. Cost efficiency J_floor/J splits exactly into technical × allocative (§5). **PROVED.** Minimizing J for some positive prices implies technical efficiency 1; with a convex frontier the converse also holds. So **the price-free content of the hypothesis is "selected systems sit on their world's frontier."**
6. **That price-free half already has outside evidence, but it is not ours.** Retinal ganglion cells carry predictive information close to the limit set by the stimulus (Palmer et al. 2015); E. coli climbs gradients within a factor of two of the bound set by the information it acquires (Mattingly et al. 2021). These test the efficient-coding and predictive-information programme (Barlow; Bialek), and they are consistent with the hypothesis. They were not preregistered here and do not qualify under THEORY §3, so they are context, not GTAO tests.
7. **What is ours is exactly what Shannon left out:** a price per layer (structure), lateness, and the ruin cap. For the layer term there is no formula for the limit. In Model S the frontier along the layer axis is the best K-layer error E_K, computable only numerically; with rhythms no predictive rule exists (`core/KEYSTONE_NOTE.md`). **OPEN.**

## 1. Shannon's four moves, mapped

| Shannon | What it did | GTAO counterpart | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operational quantity | Capacity C (largest reliable rate); R(D) (fewest bits for distortion D), defined by the task | J\* = min J over designs; and the frontier ℱ_W (§4) | Definition |
| Converse | No code beats C or R(D) | J(𝒟) ≥ J_floor for every design (§2) | **PROVED** for the information terms; layer term only trivially (K ≥ 1) |
| Achievability | Long random codes approach the limit | Kalman / noisy-store designs attain the floor at the edges; within 4.2% in between (§3) | **PROVED** at edges (KNOWN), **NUMERICAL** in between; multi-scale **OPEN** |
| Formula from statistics | C = max I(X;Y); R(D) = min I(X; X̂) | Floor from the predictive-information curve, the sequential rate–distortion function and the prices | **PROVED** where those functions are known (Gauss–Markov closed form) |
| Universality | Same limit for any physical channel | Same floor for any substrate with the same world, read channel and prices; physics adds kT floors on the prices | **PROVED** in-model; whether real systems approach it is the empirical question |

One difference matters. Shannon's achievability uses long blocks, so delay is free. A system acting ahead cannot wait: "lateness counts as error." The right limits are therefore the **causal (sequential)** ones, not classical rate–distortion, and they are generally higher (Neuhoff & Gilbert 1982 for causal source codes; Tatikonda et al. 2004). This is where the lateness term of J enters the information side. **KNOWN.**

## 2. The converse (PROVED)

**Setting.** THEORY Appendix B standing assumptions (A1) exogenous world, (A2) stationary and ergodic under the policy, plus: the design reads Y_t through a channel from X, holds memory M_t that is a causal function of Y^t (and internal randomness), and acts A_t = g(M_t) on X_{t+k} (lead k ≥ 0). Resources of a design: I_mem = I(M_t; X^t) (nats held about the world's past), r = the directed information rate from X to M per step (Massey 1990), K = number of layers, D = E ℓ(A_t, X_{t+k}). **Assumption S-A:** the read cost satisfies A ≥ c·r for a price c per nat delivered (for a measure-and-reset tracker, c ≥ kT by Sagawa & Ueda 2009); L ≥ 0.

**Theorem S1 (judgment floor).** For every design,

J(𝒟) ≥ h·1[I_mem > 0] + κ·I_mem + c·r + w·D ≥ J_floor := min over (I, r) of { h·1[I > 0] + κI + c·r + w·max(D_IB(I), D_seq(r)) },

where D_IB(I) = least lead-k loss for memory with I(M; X^t) ≤ I (the predictive information-bottleneck distortion of CT4) and D_seq(r) = least lead-k loss with directed information rate ≤ r (sequential rate–distortion).

*Proof.* D ≥ D_IB(I_mem) by the definition of D_IB (CT4 uses the same object, with the data-processing step of CT1). D ≥ D_seq(r) by the definition of D_seq. Any memory carrying information needs a store, so K ≥ 1 when I_mem > 0. Every other term of J is non-negative. Take the minimum over (I, r). ∎

**Closed form (scalar Gauss–Markov).** World x_{t+1} = a·x_t + n_t, Var n = Q, Var x = V = Q/(1 − a²), squared loss. Let P = mmse(x_t | M_t). Because the future noise is independent of M_t (A1), D = a^{2k}P + V(1 − a^{2k}) exactly; I(M; X^t) ≥ ½ ln(V/P) (Gaussian rate–distortion); r ≥ ½ ln(a² + Q/P) (sequential RD of a scalar Gauss–Markov source; Tatikonda et al. 2004, Tanaka et al. 2017, Kostina & Hassibi 2019). So

J ≥ min_P { h·1[P < V] + (κ/2)·ln(V/P) + (c/2)·ln(a² + Q/P) + w·(a^{2k}P + V(1 − a^{2k})) }.

Checked in `shannon_checks.py` §1–2 (the floor is never above any design tried, 36/36 price points; Monte Carlo confirms the design-side formulas).

**Physics floor.** With κ ≥ kT·ν (erasure-type updates, `core/bridge_2026-11-21/FLOOR.md`) and c ≥ kT, the only free price left in the floor is the stake w. This is the theory's version of "information has a thermodynamic floor"; above the cell the floor is far below real prices (FRAMEWORK §3.11, β_phys ≫ 1). **KNOWN** ingredients; **PROVED** assembly.

**What the converse does not cover.** Worlds without a stationary law (random walks, the setting of T1 and much of the theory) are outside S1, as they are outside CT1 (CX1, open; `theory/INTERNAL_MODEL.md` §4 has the data-rate route). The layer term is bounded only by K ≥ 1. A stronger bound, K ≥ K_min(D), the fewest states that reach accuracy D, is a definition with no formula in general. In Model S it is computable: K_min(D) = min{K : E_K ≤ D}, with E_K from the merged solver (`theory/layers_checks/stores.py`). The ruin cap only shrinks the feasible set, so it can raise the floor, never lower it; no formula for that rise is derived (`core/bridge_2026-11-21/BRIDGE.md`, missing math). **OPEN.**

## 3. Achievability

- **Memory free (κ = 0).** The Kalman filter with the read noise tuned attains D_seq exactly: its directed information rate is ½ ln(P_prior/P) = ½ ln(a² + Q/P) (checked to 10⁻⁹, §1 of the checks). The floor is attained (36/36 grid points with κ = 0 at ratio 1.0000). **KNOWN** (Tanaka et al. 2017).
- **Reads free (exact reads).** A noisy store M = x_t + noise attains D_IB exactly (CT4; `c6d`). **KNOWN** form, **PROVED** in the repository.
- **Both priced.** The Kalman estimate holds more information about the past than it needs (I(x̂; X^t) > ½ ln(V/P); the excess is its non-predictive memory, the quantity in Still et al. 2012). The best one-layer Kalman design is within 4.2% of the floor at the worst grid point (κ = 1, c = 1, w = 1) and within 0.3% at w ≥ 10. **NUMERICAL.** Whether the true J\* equals the floor here, or sits strictly between, is **OPEN**.
- **Several time scales.** The information terms are attained by the full-order Kalman filter (KNOWN), but that filter needs as many states as the world's predictive degree (CT21). With a price per state, achievability is the keystone question itself: N60 in Model S, conditional on convexity; no predictive rule with rhythms. **OPEN** in general.

## 4. The single quantity: the world's judgment frontier

Define ℱ_W = { (I, r, K, D) reachable by some design in world W with read channel N and lead k }. It contains no prices.

**Theorem S2 (duality).** J\*(p) = min over z ∈ ℱ_W of ⟨p, z⟩ with p = (κ, c, h, w) is the support function of ℱ_W. It is concave in the prices; its slope in each price is the optimal amount of that resource (envelope). Knowing J\* at all positive prices recovers the convex hull of ℱ_W, and nothing more. **PROVED** (standard convex analysis; CT33's concavity in w is the one-price case). Checked numerically for w (§3 of the checks).

So the analogue of capacity is not a number but a curve, as with rate–distortion or the capacity-cost function. Two numbers summarize a world for one lead k, and neither needs a price:
- **V_pred = D(no model) − D(perfect model)**, the most loss judgment can ever remove. Scalar Gauss–Markov: a^{2k}·V.
- **v₁ = −dD/dI at I = 0**, the loss saved by the first nat. Scalar Gauss–Markov: 2a^{2k}V. At the floor (h = 0) a model pays iff κ + c(1 − a²) < w·v₁ (checked at three price pairs, §6 of the checks). This is CT4's threshold with reads added. **PROVED** for the floor; the same threshold for real designs needs the floor to be attained near I = 0, where the numerical gap above is smallest.

## 5. The efficiency measure

For a design 𝒟 with resources z = (I, r, K) and loss D:
- **Technical efficiency** η_T = min{θ : (θ·z, D) is reachable}: how far all resources could shrink together and still give the same accuracy (Debreu 1951; Farrell 1957). It needs the frontier, never a price.
- **Cost efficiency** η_C = J_floor / J(𝒟): needs price ratios only, since J is homogeneous of degree 1 in the prices.
- **Allocative efficiency** η_A = η_C / η_T: whether the design sits at the right point of the frontier for its prices.

The identity η_C = η_T·η_A holds by construction (Farrell 1957); checked on a worked inefficient design (§5 of the checks: η_T = 0.991 by cost, radial θ = 0.70, η_A = 0.994).

An output-oriented, one-resource version is what the retina study measured: η_IB = I(M; X_{t+k}) / IB(I(M; X^t)), the predictive information held as a share of the most any memory of that size could hold (Palmer et al. 2015). A cost-per-bit version is Laughlin, de Ruyter van Steveninck & Anderson (1998); the repository's β_phys (FRAMEWORK §3.11) is the inverse of a physics-floor efficiency.

**Theorem S3 (the price-free content of minimizing J).** (i) If 𝒟 minimizes J for some strictly positive prices, no feasible design uses weakly less of every resource with weakly less loss and strictly less of one; so η_T(𝒟) = 1. (ii) If ℱ_W is convex (for example when designs can be mixed in time), every undominated point minimizes J for some non-negative prices (supporting hyperplane). *Proof.* (i) Such a dominating design would have strictly lower J. (ii) Supporting hyperplane theorem. ∎ **PROVED.** With the ruin cap, both statements hold within the feasible set.

So the hypothesis splits cleanly:
- **Technical part (price-free):** selected systems sit near their world's frontier, η_T ≈ 1. Measurable from the world's statistics and the system's own resources and accuracy.
- **Allocative part (price ratios):** they sit at the frontier point whose slopes match their prices. The directional predictions in `theory/PRICE_FREE_2026-11-21.md` (CT33 and others) are comparative statics of this part, and they need only signs.

The selection bridge (`core/bridge_2026-11-21/SELECTION.md`) gives the expected distance: under the linear fitness map and weak mutation, the expected fractional excess of J over J\* is d/(2σN_e). In efficiency terms, 1 − E[J\*/J] ≈ d/(2σN_e) to first order. **PROVED** in-model under its assumptions; not tested on any real class.

## 6. Prior art, and where J differs

| Work | What it has | What J adds or changes |
|---|---|---|
| Shannon 1948, 1959; Berger 1971 | Capacity, rate–distortion, converse and achievability | Causality (lead k), a price per state, a ruin cap, separate prices for holding and reading |
| Sequential / causal RD: Neuhoff & Gilbert 1982; Tatikonda, Sahai & Mitter 2004; Tanaka et al. 2017; Kostina & Hassibi 2019 | The read-side floor and its Kalman achievability; rate–cost trade-offs in control | Holding price κ and layer price h beside the read price; persistence under ruin |
| Bialek, Nemenman & Tishby 2001; Tishby, Pereira & Bialek 1999; Creutzig, Globerson & Tishby 2009; Still et al. 2012; Still 2020 | Predictive information, the predictive bottleneck, its thermodynamic cost | Prices and stakes that pick the operating point; layers; ruin (CT4 already credits these) |
| Sims 2003 (rational inattention) | Expected loss plus a price on information | Several resources with separate prices; structure; ruin |
| Friston 2010 (free energy) | A variational bound on surprise as the quantity organisms minimize | J is an explicit cost in the system's own currency, with free prices, a layer price and a ruin cap; no claim that surprise is the objective |
| Farrell 1957; Debreu 1951; Shoval et al. 2012 | Technical vs allocative efficiency; Pareto fronts as evidence of optimization in biology | The frontier is computed from the world's statistics rather than fitted to the data |
| Palmer et al. 2015; Mattingly et al. 2021; Laughlin et al. 1998 | Real systems measured near information limits | None: these are prior evidence for the information part, credited |

## 7. What this changes, and what it does not

- It gives the theory a Shannon-shaped core: a definition, a proved floor, partial achievability, a world-defined frontier and a price-free efficiency measure. The structure results (CT20/CT33, CT21 L1–L3, N60 conditional, CT22/CT25, CT35/CT36, the freeze limit of PR #137, the selection bridge) are then statements about the minimizer on that frontier.
- It does not make the hypothesis true, and it does not lift any NOT A TEST row. It does say what a decisive measurement looks like: the world's frontier from the world's statistics, a system's resources and accuracy, and the efficiency number, with no stakes or upkeep prices needed for the technical part.
- **The most important gap.** The part of J that is the theory's own, the price per layer, has no formula for its limit. Without "the fewest layers that can reach accuracy D in this world," computable from data, the efficiency test can touch the information part (where Bialek's programme already has results) but not the layer part. In Model S it is computable numerically; in general it is **OPEN**.
