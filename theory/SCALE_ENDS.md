## 0. Bottom line

1. **The costs are present at both ends.** What vanishes is the value of carrying a model. Erasing a bit costs at least kT ln 2 (Landauer), a measure-and-reset cycle costs at least kT × the information acquired (Sagawa & Ueda), and measurement alone can in principle be reversible [BK]. Holding information about anything that drifts needs a continual inflow and dissipation (INTERNAL_MODEL I5). A black hole stores information at an energy of 2kT_H per nat, which is the Bekenstein minimum for its size. The cosmological horizon caps the information in our causal patch at about 3×10¹²² nats and sets a floor temperature of about 2×10⁻³⁰ K. So Chad is right that the costs do not switch off. Ace's line, "nothing is keeping score up there," was wrong.
2. **At the atomic end the theory's own results give the zero-model corner as the optimum.** No argument from outside the theory is needed. Two results carry it [IR-FIX1]. Blind persistence is viable (Theorem P; non-definitional). And under the Axiom-7 definition of an action (E1) nothing selects among the atom's responses, so the value of information *to the atom* is zero (partly definitional, and said so). An atom does carry some information about its surroundings: its speed, spin and level populations correlate with temperature and fields, which is why atoms make thermometers, magnetometers and clocks. That correlation is physics, not a model. *Withdrawn:* the earlier claim that an atom's state entropy is about 10^−171 nats, so that it cannot carry information about its surroundings. That figure is only the electronic-excitation occupancy; the hyperfine spin alone carries ln 4 ≈ 1.39 nats (independent review, `reviews/night2/SCALE_ENDS_INDEP_REVIEW.md`). So atoms skip models because a model would buy them nothing, not because a model would cost them nothing. This sharpens Chad's wording ("no cost there"): the bill for a model would be nonzero, but the benefit is exactly zero.
3. **A black hole, in GTAO terms, is the extreme of the frozen class (the limit of the frozen corner), and nobody's usable memory [IR-FIX2].** It is the store with the most information per unit size and energy that physics allows. It pays no upkeep to persist (and today it takes in net energy from the CMB). Its readable state is two or three numbers (mass, spin, charge). Everything else comes back only as scrambled radiation: for a hole before its Page time (every astrophysical hole today, all colder than the CMB) no sooner than about the Page time; for an older hole after about the scrambling time, but only to a holder of the earlier radiation at a decoding cost conjectured to be exponential (Harlow–Hayden), so that case sits outside N26's PROVED scope [BK]. Counting that lateness as prediction error (THEORY §0), every tracker sits past its lookup edge: it would sooner look at the world directly than read a black hole [IR-FIX2, IR-FIX3]. Its place in the frozen *class* comes from E1, E2 and E4 (it is not selected, nothing outside threatens it, and its lifetime is set by its own leak), and its being the *limit* of that class comes from physics (Bekenstein saturation). It records the past; it predicts nothing. Its upkeep is a leak, Hawking evaporation, which sets its lifetime at about **1.2×10⁶⁷ yr × (M/M☉)³** (photons and gravitons with greybody factors, the physical case for any hole heavier than ~10⁻¹⁰ M☉; the full species range quoted in older sources is 10⁶⁶–10⁶⁷ yr) [IR-FIX4]. Ace's old H1 ("maximal storage, not organizers") survives, but it is now a *derived output*, not a scope exclusion.
4. **What GTAO does not do.** It does not derive that black holes exist, nor the area law, nor the Page curve. Those are general relativity and quantum gravity. Saying "GTAO predicts what black holes are" in that stronger sense is an imposter. The defensible form is this: *given* published black-hole physics, GTAO's results (E1, E2, E4, and Theorem 8 with the lateness term) sort the black hole to the extreme of the frozen class and past every tracker's lookup edge, and they predict that no selected system will keep drifting content in BH-like storage (dense, scrambled, costly to read). That last claim can be tested only in ordinary storage hierarchies (O5, FRAMEWORK §6.6), where the black hole is the limit point.
5. **How high it goes.** The budget applies up to the cosmological event horizon. The lateness term, though, shrinks what a large structure can track. A tracker of extent L reads with latency at least L/c, so it can hold a target only if q·(L/c)^{α−1} is below its error tolerance. This is DERIVED for hold-last-value reads (§5). Big structures must therefore federate: the top level tracks only slow drift. This generalizes H26 ("higher levels see further but update slower") and can be tested on Earth.
6. **The switch-on (origin of *selected* models) [BK].** *Selected* models (design optimization) switch on only when four conditions hold together: heredity below the Eigen error threshold, an action whose payoff depends on the world, drift inside the hold band, and a per-nat cost below the marginal value of the first nat. With Kelly-type fitness the last condition reads E_gen > β_phys·kT, where E_gen is the energy per generation. A bacterium spends about 10¹⁰ ATP per division, so for a bacterium the Landauer floor does not prevent the switch-on (E_gen ≈ 10¹¹–10¹² kT). That it *never* does anywhere, and that heredity and drift are what bind, is a HYPOTHESIS with the kill in §3.4 [IR-FIX11]. The inequality is DERIVED under stated premises (§3.4) and partly prior art (Eigen; Donaldson-Matasci et al.; Kussell & Leibler; Woelfle et al.). Under the Axiom-7 reading of "action", the first two conditions overlap, so "models need selection" is close to definitional; the non-circular content is the drift band, the energy inequality and Eigen's threshold (§3.4, IR-FIX10). Survival-relevant information and minimal (integral-feedback) models are not excluded before heredity (`theory/BOOKENDS.md` §2.1), and SE4's kill (iii) now tests the heredity clause directly [BK].

## 2. One derived result that covers both ends: when the zero-model corner is optimal (Proposition E)

Take any structure X that persists. Define the target as the world variable Y whose value affects X's ruin hazard, and the action set as the options available to X whose effect on ruin or growth depends on Y.

**E1 (no decision, no model) [DERIVED; elementary].** If X has no action whose payoff depends on Y (a single effective action), then for every loss r(u) = r_∞ and Φ = 0. By G2, holding never beats fading for any λ > 0, and Theorem 8 has W_effective = 0, so q_f → ∞ and the structure is frozen at every drift. Equivalently, the value of information is zero when no decision uses it: Blackwell's comparison of experiments (1953, used in GENERAL_THEOREM G1) orders experiments by decision value, and with one action every experiment has value 0. *Consequence: the drift spectrum α, q and N enter nothing.* The spectrum matters only where Φ > 0.

**E2 (blind-viable, no model) [DERIVED from Theorem P].** If the blind margin m − wE_L ≥ 0, so that X persists with R = 0, then a model is unnecessary. It is optimal only if κ is below the marginal value of the first bit. When E1 also holds, that marginal value is 0, so R\* = 0 for any κ > 0. Because the cost of a read is never 0 (a measure-and-reset cycle costs at least kT × the information acquired, E3), this is strict.

**E3 (no coupling, no held information about drift; corrected per [R-FIX3]) [DERIVED from KNOWN pieces].**
- (a) If a structure is uncoupled from a drifting variable Y (frozen: no inflow), its mutual information with Y_t cannot increase, and it decays as Y decorrelates (data-processing inequality). DERIVED.
- (b) Keeping it requires coupling: an information inflow of at least (1 − η)·I per step (I5).
- (c) The thermodynamic price of that inflow depends on how the coupling works. It is at least kT per nat for measure-and-erase feedback cycles (Sagawa & Ueda 2009; Landauer 1961). It is at least kT × (nonpredictive information retained) for driven systems (Still et al. 2012; they formalize this for isothermal Markov systems with a single bath and no back-action on the drive, and elsewhere it is an analogy [BK]). It **can approach 0** for quasi-static passive coupling to slow drift. The review's counterexample shows this: a two-state receptor tracking a drifting energy keeps I ≈ 0.18 nats while its dissipation per cycle falls ∝ ω, to 0.026 kT at ω/γ = 0.01 (`reviews/night2/SCALE_ENDS_REVIEW_checks/e3_counterexample.out`). Mehta & Schwab (2012) give the cellular case for *learning* (time-averaging), which does require dissipation.
- (d) In every case, I(X; Y) ≤ H(X): a structure cannot carry more information about anything than its own state entropy. DERIVED.

*The first version of E3 said "zero entropy production ⇒ no held information about drift". That is false, and it is withdrawn.* What survives is narrower. Holding **memory** (information beyond the present instantaneous correlation) about a drifting variable costs dissipation. *[IR-FIX1] The self-review's atomic application of (d), "a ground-state atom has near-zero state entropy, so it can carry no information about its surroundings", is also withdrawn.* Only ground-state hydrogen's electronic-excitation entropy is tiny (occupancy 10^−170.7, entropy ≈ 10^−168 nats at 300 K). Its hyperfine spin entropy is ln 4 ≈ 1.39 nats, its translational entropy about 13 nats (300 K, 1 bar), and one atom's speed carries 0.08 nats about whether the bath is at 300 K or 600 K (`reviews/night2/SCALE_ENDS_INDEP_checks/physics_indep.out`). Atoms are passive sensors of the (c) kind.

**E4 (frozen lifetime) [KNOWN physics, mapped].** A frozen store pays no upkeep, but its lifetime H_l is finite, and its protection sets that lifetime:
- a barrier E_a: H_l ≈ τ₀ e^{E_a/kT} (Arrhenius/Kramers; crystal defects, isotopic zoning, metastable nuclei);
- a conservation law: the proton, τ > 1.6×10³⁴ yr for p → e⁺π⁰ at 90% CL (Super-Kamiokande, Abe et al. 2017);
- a horizon: a black hole, H_l ∝ M³ (§4.2).
In Theorem 8 terms, H_l is the "lifetime" input, and the frozen keeping cost is 0. Physics thus confirms C6's kill clause ("a frozen layer's keeping cost rises enough that it needs fading") as **not met**: frozen stores do not need fading, because they leak on their own.

**The ladder these give [DERIVED classification].** There are three distinct persistence classes. The theory's three costs take different values in each, and each class needs its own conditions.

| Class | Upkeep h | Model R | Needs | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|

**Four notions of "knowing" [BK; `theory/BOOKENDS.md` §2.0.2].** (a) correlation (nearly universal); (b) survival-relevant information (scrambling it at fixed fluxes lowers viability; Kolchinsky & Wolpert 2018); (c) a structural model (internal model principle, integral feedback minimal; it passes BK-IMP: perfect adaptation after a step, robust to parameter changes, with the integrated quantity the error) of a variable bearing on the entity's own ruin; (d) a selected model. Class 2 vs 3 is split by (c); design predictions need (d). Excluding feedback on one's own state is a definitional choice (Rosen 1985 counts a model of itself).

H8/H9's "frozen vs held" split becomes three classes, with class 3s inside class 3. Stars are *held* (they dissipate to persist) but carry no model, so "held" and "has a model" are different things. GTAO's design predictions (onset, vigilance, band edges) apply only to selected models, class 3s (FRAMEWORK §1.2 item 4). The *cost accounting* applies to all three.

**Prior art for the three classes [IR-FIX8].** The split is not new in substance. GTAO restates it in its own cost terms. Prigogine (1978) separates equilibrium structures (crystals) from dissipative structures. Pross (2011) separates thermodynamic stability from the dynamic kinetic stability of replicators. Kolchinsky & Wolpert (2018) define the value of a system's information about its environment by whether it is causally necessary for the system's continued existence, explicitly for "a rock, a hurricane, a bacterium": close prior art for E1/E2's viability-based value of information and for these three classes. Krakauer et al. (2020) derive organismal, colonial and driven forms of individuality from information propagated from past to future. Friston et al. (2023) sort particles into conservative vs dissipative, inert vs active, and ordinary vs strange. That framework is a **rival** at the atomic end: it grants inference-like dynamics to broad classes of particles, where E1/E2 give R\* = 0 wherever nothing is selected. As stated, the disagreement is about wording, not a testable difference.

**What the atom stores.** Its quantum numbers (Z, A, charge, spin) are frozen, conserved records. A crystal can also keep a frozen record of its formation environment: zoning, defects, isotope ratios. That is memory in GTAO's sense, a record whose keeping cost is 0. It is not a model. The atom does nothing with it, so by E1 it is worth nothing *to the atom*. It is worth something to a later tracker that reads it: a geologist reading a zircon is doing a GTAO "freeze" read of a frozen store with a long H_l.

### 3.2 Why atoms do not carry added model complexity (the answer to Chad's question)

What it lacks is any selected use for that information (item 3).
3. **E1 (the Axiom-7 reading) [R-FIX4]:** an "action" in GTAO is an alternative over which a selection process operates. Atoms have physical responses but no selection over them, so Φ = 0 by that definition. This is the band's frozen corner where there is no selection, which is the scope Chad set at t1927. It is partly definitional. Since item 2 is withdrawn [IR-FIX1], it is load-bearing together with item 1, and the atomic case should be read that way: E2 says nothing threatens the atom, and E1 (Axiom-7 reading) says nothing it does is selected. The costs are not zero. A model would cost at least kT ln 2 per bit per refresh, and its benefit is exactly zero. **The bottom of the scale is the frozen corner of GTAO's own band, reached at W → 0 and q → 0 together.**

### 3.3 Where the switch-on happens

The first structures that are *held* rather than frozen persist by a flux, with no model: prebiotic dissipative chemistry, autocatalytic cycles and "dynamic kinetic stability."
- Prior art: Prigogine 1978 on dissipative structures; Kauffman 1986 on autocatalytic sets; England 2013 on the thermodynamic lower bound for self-replication.
- They need D > 0. They are class 2 unless they hold a structural model; whether they carry survival-relevant information is tested by BOOKENDS BK-SEM [BK].

**A *selected* model (class 3s) needs selection over designs**, which means heredity (or, above the cell, learning or design) [BK]. Below the Eigen error threshold, a replicator of length L with per-site copying error ε and selective superiority σ keeps its information only if εL ≲ ln σ (Eigen 1971, KNOWN). Vasas, Szathmáry & Santos (2010) showed that compositional (metabolism-first) autocatalytic networks lack evolvability, so a GTAO-type design optimum cannot be approached without template heredity. That is prior art, and it supports putting the switch-on at heredity.

### 3.4 Is there a derivable threshold? (DERIVED under stated premises; partly PRIOR ART)

A model is selected only if all four of the following hold:
- **(S1) Heredity:** εL < ln σ. KNOWN (Eigen).
- **(S2) An action whose payoff depends on the world** (E1 fails).
- **(S3) Drift inside the hold band:** q_f < q < q_c (Theorem 8). In evolutionary terms, the environment must change on time scales between "slow enough to be frozen into the genome" and "too fast or unpredictable to track." Below q_f, constitutive (frozen) wins; above q_c, lookup or bet-hedging wins. Kussell & Leibler (2005) is the KNOWN phenotype-switching version.
- **(S4) The first nat pays (Theorem P).** For proportional-betting (Kelly-type) fitness, the growth value of a cue carrying I nats is at most I nats per generation, with equality in many cases (Donaldson-Matasci, Bergstrom & Lachmann 2010). Rivoire & Leibler (2011) treat general fitness structures.

  Suppose the cost of acquiring and using I nats is at least β_phys·kT·I per generation, and a cell needs energy E_gen per division. Spending ΔE then slows growth by about ΔE/E_gen nats per generation. Including the fixed cost κ₀ of building the sensing machinery (protein burden) [R-FIX7], net gain ≥ I·(1 − β_phys·kT/E_gen) − κ₀/E_gen. The Landauer part alone is positive iff

  **E_gen > β_phys · kT,**

  and the binding term is then κ₀/E_gen (machinery), not the floor. By E3(c), passive coupling can cost less than the per-cycle kT per nat, which only strengthens this.

  A 1 µm³ bacterium needs about 2.7×10¹⁰ ATP per division (Lynch & Marinov 2015; other estimates are about 3× lower), roughly 10¹¹–10¹² kT. So S4 is met unless β_phys exceeds about 10¹¹.

**Consequence (HYPOTHESIS, falsifiable).** The Landauer floor never prevents a model from switching on. The binding conditions are S1 and S3. The floor shapes the *design* of the models that switch on (precision and speed: §2.5's θ; Lan et al. 2012), not whether they exist. This partly cuts against a strong reading of "the floor binds tightly at the cell": it binds the exponents, not the existence.

**Kill.** A primitive sensing or regulatory system shown to be energy-limited at its existence margin, i.e. one that would pay in information terms but is absent because β_phys·kT·I is comparable to E_gen. Premises: Kelly-type fitness (outside it, the value per nat can be lower; Rivoire & Leibler); a growth penalty linear in ΔE/E_gen; erasure-type cost.

**Evidence that fits S3 (PRIOR ART, not GTAO-specific).** In Woelfle et al. 2004, cyanobacteria with a working circadian clock beat clock-disrupted strains in light–dark cycles, but the advantage disappears in constant light (the arrhythmic strain even gained slightly), and a strain does best when its period matches the cycle. A held model pays only when the world drifts in a way it can track. This is H2's "anticipating chemistry," with real data, in a cell. It is not a GTAO magnitude test: it does not measure where q_f and q_c sit.

## 4. The top end: stars, galaxies, black holes, the universe

### 4.1 Stars and galaxies (class 2: held structure, no structural model)

A star persists by dissipating a free-energy throughput L, paid from nuclear free energy (L is throughput, not a maintenance cost: a star that stops fusing contracts and heats, and white dwarfs persist with no flux) [BK]. It regulates on its *own* state: negative heat capacity makes hydrostatic and thermal balance a proportional restoring effect (if the core runs hot it expands, cools and burns slower). That is self-regulation, not H80's thermostat rung, which predicts an exogenous target [BK]. For an isolated star its ruin (fuel exhaustion, then collapse) is fixed by its mass and composition, not by tracking anything external. Most massive stars interact with a companion (Sana et al. 2012), which changes their fate, but the star has no response it can vary to track that [BK]. The interstellar medium's drift does not enter its main-sequence persistence, so E1 holds and R\* = 0. This is DERIVED as a classification. Mapping: throughput ↔ L (DERIVED, measurable); no target; no selection (stars do not inherit designs, except in Smolin's universe-level reading, §4.4). Under FRAMEWORK it is **a known group for "held but not modeling"**: any instrument that reports a model in a star fails MV7. **Galaxies [BK]:** in the gas-regulator model star formation is regulated by a gas reservoir, and the sSFR is set to the specific accretion rate if SFR efficiency and wind loading are constant (Lilly et al. 2013): a leaky integrator tracking inflow, so K1 per that model. An observational test is not defined, so galaxies are **not** a known group for MV7.

**GTAO reading (DERIVED, given the physics above):**
1. **Memory.** The black hole has the most memory any region of its size can hold (holographic bound; Bousso 2002 review). It stores at the Bekenstein-minimum energy per nat. So it sits at the extreme of "cheapest storage per unit size and energy." That is a property of the frozen corner: keeping cost 0, capacity at the physical maximum.
2. **Upkeep.** Persisting needs none (h = 0: nothing is paid to persist). Today there is also a net energy inflow from the CMB, which is not "negative upkeep" [R-FIX6]. Its only loss is a *leak* (Hawking emission ∝ M⁻²), which fixes its frozen lifetime H_l ∝ M³ (E4). The leak is not upkeep paid to hold information; it is the store's spontaneous decay.
3. **Reads.** For an outside tracker, the readable interface is 2–3 numbers out of about 10⁷⁷ bits. Everything else returns only as thermal-looking radiation. It returns after the Page time for early infall. For an old hole it returns after about t\* (Hayden–Preskill's "mirror"), but only to someone who holds essentially all earlier radiation and can decode it, which is conjectured to take time exponential in S (Harlow–Hayden). In GTAO terms the binding cost is **lateness**, not energy [IR-FIX3]. Priced as energy (FRAMEWORK §3.11's convention), a Hawking quantum is the cheapest read in nature, about kT_H ≈ 10⁻³⁰ J per nat for 1 M☉, and with energy pricing alone Theorem 8's band would be wide open (`reviews/night2/SCALE_ENDS_INDEP_checks/band_indep.out`, B). What closes it is time. A read of infallen content arrives no earlier than the Page time for a young hole (Page 1993). For an old hole it arrives only to a collector that has gathered the radiation since before the Page time and can decode it (conjectured exponentially hard, Harlow–Hayden). The emitted entropy rate is only about 3×10² nats/s for 1 M☉ (photons and gravitons). Lateness ℓ adds W·q·ℓ^{α−1} to every read's error and cannot be bought down (§5). So lookup beats any read of the hole once q > (μu − h)/(Wℓ^{α−1}), which at ℓ ~ 10⁶⁶ yr is every drift anyone tracks (`band_indep.out`, C).
   - Theorem 8's own limit c → ∞ (energy or effort per read) gives the same outcome by a different route [IR-FIX2]. q_f = 2c/(WH_l²) → ∞, so freezing beats holding, but freezing then costs c/H_l > μu. The optimum is **lookup**, not freeze, at every drift (`band_latency.out`; `band_indep.out`, A). The band condition μuH_l > 2c + hH_l fails: no hold band at any drift.
   - So the band sorts a black hole, *as somebody else's memory*, beyond the lookup edge: unused. Its membership in the frozen *class*, as a persisting structure, comes from E1, E2 and E4 (items 2 and 4), not from Theorem 8.

   **A tracker never uses a black hole as its memory; it looks at the world instead.**
4. **Prediction.** The black hole has no action whose payoff depends on its surroundings, and its persistence is blind-viable, so E1 and E2 give R\* = 0. What it holds is a scrambled record of its past: what fell in. That is not information about the future of anything it faces. Fast scrambling is, if anything, the *opposite* of holding a model. It turns locally readable correlations into global ones that no local read can recover. **In GTAO's terms a black hole has maximal memory, maximal mixing and minimal readability. It is the frozen class's extreme, and past every tracker's lookup edge [IR-FIX2].**

**Does the theory *predict* this endpoint?** Here is where it succeeds and where it fails, stated plainly.
- *Succeeds (DERIVED, conditional on two mappings [R-FIX5, IR-FIX3]: the Axiom-7 reading of "action" (E1), or SE-E2 + SE-E4 under the response reading [BK]; and lateness counted as prediction error with the hole's retrieval latency ≥ the Page time ∝ M³. That latency holds for pre-Page-time holes, which is every astrophysical hole today. For post-Page-time holes the conclusion rests on the decoding cost, a conjecture (Harlow–Hayden), and is outside N26's PROVED scope [BK]):* given the physical facts (capacity S, readable state (M, J, Q), retrieval latency ~t_Page, leak ∝ M⁻²), E1, E2 and E4 put the black hole in the frozen class, at its extreme, and Theorem 8 with the lateness term puts it beyond every tracker's lookup edge [IR-FIX2]. H1 ("maximal storage, not organizers") is now an output of E1, E2 and Theorem 8, not a scope rule.
- *Fails (stated):* GTAO does not explain *why* maximal density and maximal unreadability coincide. That comes from gravity: horizons are what saturate the bound and what block signals. GTAO also does not derive the area law, the Page curve (recent derivations: Penington 2020; Almheiri, Engelhardt, Marolf & Maxfield 2019) or the scrambling bound. Claiming any of these as GTAO predictions is an **imposter**.
- *Falsifiable prediction left over (HYPOTHESIS BH-1).* Across selected memory systems, the denser and costlier-to-read a storage tier is, the slower the drift of the items kept there. The freeze edge q_f = 2c/(WH_l²) grows linearly with read cost. The black hole is the limit where read latency or cost diverges, and nothing drifting is kept there (in Theorem 8 terms: past the lookup edge) [IR-FIX2]. "Denser" is not an input of Theorem 8; it enters only because, physically, the densest stores are the hardest to read. **Kill:** FRAMEWORK §6.6's O5 kill (closed-form mode classification no better than item type, ΔAUC < 0.02) on a gated sample of storage tiers (cold, archival or tape vs hot caches; DNA vs mRNA vs protein modification). This is the existing O5 test (CLAIM_MAP F13). The black hole adds a limit point, not a new test. It cannot be tested on black holes themselves: MV1 fails, since there is no tracker, target or pool.
- *Physics-side check that the frozen reading is right:* "no readable memory beyond (M, J, Q)" is exactly what black-hole spectroscopy tests (Isi et al. 2019: consistent with Kerr). Echoes or hair would put readable memory on a black hole. That is GR's falsifier, which GTAO borrows; it adds a label, not content.

**Compared with H1.** H1's kill ("any black hole showing anticipation") is now concrete: a black hole whose response to infall depends on its history beyond (M, J, Q) in a way that tracks its surroundings ahead of time. Under known physics this is excluded at the ringdown-test level. Status: H1 is **upheld and re-derived**, and its source changes from "scope condition" to "E1 + E2 + Theorem 8 with published physics."

### 4.3 Holography and ER = EPR (published claims only)

- **Holography** ('t Hooft 1993; Susskind 1995; Bousso 2002). The maximum information in a region scales with its boundary area. For GTAO this is a hard **capacity cap on memory at every scale**: no tracker of radius R can hold more than about A/(4ℓ_p²) nats. Every real tracker sits astronomically below it, so it never binds a design. This is PRIOR ART used as a bound.
- **ER = EPR** (Maldacena & Susskind 2013). This is the published conjecture that entangled black holes, and perhaps all entangled systems, are joined by non-traversable Einstein–Rosen bridges. By the authors' own premise these bridges carry no signals, so they provide **no read channel**. GTAO can draw nothing from it: no read, no Φ. Any reading of "black holes as a connected memory network" is an **imposter**.

### 4.4 Smolin's cosmological natural selection (nearest prior art for an adaptive reading at cosmic scale; credited)

Smolin (1992; 2004 review, hep-th/0407213, fetched) proposes the following. Universes reproduce through black holes. Each child inherits slightly mutated parameters. Over many generations the parameters come to maximize black-hole production. That gives Axiom 7 at cosmic scale: heredity plus selection. It is falsifiable. Smolin's §6.3 argues that if the strange-quark mass sits below a critical value (the Bethe–Brown kaon-condensate case), the neutron-star upper mass limit is about 1.5 M☉. He then says that a sufficiently heavy neutron star would refute the theory: "Sufficiently high is certainly 2.5M☉, although if one is completely confident of Bethe and Brown's upper limit of 1.5 solar masses, any value higher than this would be troubling." Measured masses since then include PSR J0740+6620 at 2.08 ± 0.07 M☉ (Fonseca et al. 2021) and PSR J0952−0607 at 2.35 ± 0.17 M☉ (Romani et al. 2022). Both are "troubling" by his own wording, and neither reaches the "certainly" refuting 2.5 M☉. Status: **PRIOR ART, under observational pressure** (that is our reading of the published masses against Smolin's stated criterion, not a published verdict).

**Where selection exists at cosmic scale (under the t1927 scope).** CNS is the one published proposal in which Axiom 7 (heredity plus selection over designs) operates above the level of stars. If it holds, GTAO's design layer applies to universes as units, and MV8-ii (selection) is met *in that hypothesis*. MV1 still needs a named target, pool and channel. The "target" would be the fitness landscape (black-hole yield as a function of parameters). It has no drift, so there is nothing to track and the design optimum is the frozen corner.

**GTAO's reading of CNS (HYPOTHESIS, labelled; not a GTAO prediction).** If CNS holds, the universe-level "organism" has a stationary fitness function (black-hole yield as a function of parameters, with no drift, q = 0). With q = 0, GTAO's optimum is the frozen corner: no sensing, no plasticity, inheritance by copying frozen parameters. GTAO therefore predicts that the constants do not drift, which matches the Rosenband bound. **This does not discriminate:** any physics with constant constants predicts the same, so GTAO adds no test beyond Smolin's own. In the CNS reading the black hole is the *reproductive* step. Its scrambled archive is not passed on; only the frozen parameters are. That fits §4.2: a black hole is not a memory anyone uses.

### 4.5 The universe as a whole

- **Is the universe a GTAO tracker?** No. It has no exterior target, no stakes conditioned on error, and no action set (E1). It is an **IMPOSTER** to call the universe an adaptive organization, a model-carrier or "a universe choosing" (already a retired flag in HYPOTHESES_CORE §4). That covers the whole. Smolin's population of universes is the only published version with selection, and §4.4 covers it.
- **Costs at the horizon (DERIVED from published bounds; numbers in `physics_numbers.out`).** With H₀ = 67.4 km/s/Mpc and Ω_Λ = 0.685, 1/H_Λ ≈ 17.5 Gyr.
  - The de Sitter horizon entropy S_dS = πc²/(H_Λ²ℓ_p²) ≈ 3.3×10¹²² nats. This is the asymptotic value. Egan & Lineweaver's (2010) *current* cosmic-event-horizon value is 2.6 ± 0.3 × 10¹²² k. Theirs is smaller because today's event horizon is smaller than the asymptotic de Sitter horizon and grows toward it [R-FIX2]. This is the memory cap for anything in our causal patch.
  - The de Sitter temperature T_dS = ħH_Λ/(2πk) ≈ 2.2×10⁻³⁰ K (Gibbons & Hawking 1977). That puts a permanent floor on the price of a bit, kT_dS ln 2 ≈ 2×10⁻⁵³ J. No tracker anywhere in our future can pay less.
  - The present entropy of the observable universe, S_obs ≈ 3×10¹⁰⁴ k, is dominated by supermassive black holes (Egan & Lineweaver 2010). It lies far below the cap. That gap is the disequilibrium D that funds every held structure (Egan & Lineweaver say the same in words: the increase "has not yet been capped… is the reason dissipative processes are ongoing and that life can exist").
- **Heat death (DERIVED + PRIOR ART).** By the growth inequality, held structures need D > 0. By E3, held information about drift needs a dissipative flux. As stars burn out (Adams & Laughlin 1997) and D → 0, class-2 and class-3 structures cannot persist; only frozen stores remain (black holes until they evaporate; protons if they are stable). Krauss & Starkman (2000) already argue that in a Λ-dominated universe the total recoverable energy and information are finite, that T_dS sets a minimum operating temperature, and that hibernation fails because "all alarm clocks eventually fail" and run at a minimum power. **In GTAO's terms:** Dyson's (1979) hibernation strategy is the move to the freeze corner. Krauss–Starkman's alarm clock is the read that a frozen store still needs (G2: freezing still needs one read per lifetime). Its failure is E4's finite H_l plus a read price that cannot drop below kT_dS. This is PRIOR ART relabelled. GTAO adds the labels, not a new result.

## 5. How high does it go? The lateness bound on a tracker's extent (DERIVED; testable)

THEORY §0 counts lateness inside prediction error. A tracker whose sensor and decision sit a distance L apart, or whose parts must agree across L, reads with latency at least ℓ = L/c. Take a target with structure function E(Δ_τ x)² = qτ^{α−1} (FRAMEWORK §3.1; 1 < α < 3) and **hold-last-value reads** every T (THEORY's reset-read premise). The mean squared error is q·avg_{s∈[0,T]}(ℓ + s)^{α−1}. At α = 2 it is q(T/2 + ℓ) exactly (Monte Carlo over 4,000 paths: 1.2107 vs 1.2000 + dt/2 grid bias; `band_latency.out`). The latency part, Wqℓ^{α−1}, **cannot be bought down by reading more often**.

- **Bound.** With an error tolerance D\*, or a bounded fallback F (local autonomy at its own cost): the tracker can hold the target only if qℓ^{α−1} < D\*, so **L_max = c·(D\*/q)^{1/(α−1)}**. At α = 2, L_max ∝ 1/q.
- **Consequence (HYPOTHESIS LAT-1).** A large organization federates. A target with drift q is held centrally only if q(L/c)^{α−1} is below tolerance. Faster targets are delegated to local levels. The top level of anything galaxy-sized could centrally hold only targets whose change over one light-crossing time (~10⁵ yr) stays within tolerance [R-FIX8]. This is H26 with a physical latency floor. For predictors that extrapolate (α > 2, an optimal linear tracker), the latency penalty is smaller. The bound is stated for hold-last-value reads only.
- **The ceiling.** Nothing beyond the cosmological event horizon can ever be read, so the horizon is the hard top of any tracker. Inside it, the lateness bound and the horizon's memory and price caps (§4.5) are where "how high" ends.
- **Kill (testable on Earth and in space, MV1–MV8 satisfiable).**
  - Systems: spacecraft operations with measured one-way light time; geo-distributed databases or firms with measured latency ℓ, per-target drift q from §3.1, and a recorded mode (central vs delegated).
  - Prediction: P(central) falls with q·ℓ^{α−1}.
  - Kill: at fixed q, no dependence on ℓ (CI includes 0), or the fitted exponent on ℓ excludes α − 1 while including 0, in a gated sample.
  - Rival: mode set by size or norms alone.
- **Prior art (swept by the independent review, IR-FIX8).** Simon (1962): near-decomposability, with fast local and slow global dynamics in hierarchies. Flack (2017): slow, coarse-grained variables at higher levels. Abadi (2012, PACELC): the latency-vs-consistency trade in geo-replicated stores. Bailis et al. (2012, PBS; already cited in THEORY §3, rule 5). Tatikonda & Mitter (2004): control under communication constraints. Lloyd (2000): communication time vs flip time, and fully serial computation at the black-hole limit. Olson (2022, arXiv 2208.07871): horizon limits on signalling within a cosmological-scale civilization. No published statement of L_max = c(D\*/q)^{1/(α−1)} in this exact form was found. The form is elementary, and novelty is **not claimed**.

## 10. Limits

- E1 is partly definitional (review FIX 4); the atomic case rests on E2 and E1's Axiom-7 reading, because E3(d)'s atomic application is withdrawn (IR-FIX1). E1's force depends on defining "action" before data. For borderline chemistry (a catalyst whose rate responds to conditions), whether that is a world-dependent *action* or just physics is a modelling choice. The switch-on is a band, not a line.
- §3.4's inequality is an order-of-magnitude argument resting on three premises (Kelly-type fitness, a linear growth penalty, erasure-type cost). Prebiotic systems with no reproduction fall outside it entirely.
- The black-hole read cost (exponential decoding) rests on Harlow–Hayden's complexity *argument*, which is conjectural. The Page-time latency bound alone (∝ M³), entering through the lateness term, already puts every tracker past its lookup edge for any drift faster than once per ~10⁶⁶ yr. With read cost priced as energy only, the band would stay open (IR-FIX3).
- The evaporation times depend on which species are emitted. The physical value for stellar and larger holes is the photon + graviton one, ≈ 1.16×10⁶⁷ yr × (M/M☉)³; the naive no-greybody (2.1×10⁶⁷) and massless-neutrino (2.2×10⁶⁶) figures are kept only as labelled comparisons (IR-FIX4).
- LAT-1 is for hold-last-value reads; extrapolating trackers do better at α > 2 (independent check: 20% lower penalty at α = 2.5). Its prior art has now been swept (§5, IR-FIX8).
- The first referee pass was by the same executor (disclosed). The independent review is `reviews/night2/SCALE_ENDS_INDEP_REVIEW.md` (PASS-WITH-FIXES; fixes marked [IR-FIXn]). Prior art for LAT-1 and for the three-class ladder has been swept there.
