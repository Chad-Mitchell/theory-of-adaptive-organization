# Placements on the face figure (`figures/face.png`): notes and sources

**Every position is a hypothesis.** No system has valid measured stakes (GAPS G3), so none has a measured R, and the regions are those of one made-up model world (`figures/face_model.py`): 14 non-oscillating time scales from 1 to about 3×10⁶ fast units (two per decade, equal variance, so a spectrum of about 1/f), sensing noise chosen optimally at a price C0/N with C0 = 0.003h, a federation overhead of 20h and K layers each costing h. A circle says "if this system's dimensionless coordinates were about here, this model world would call its design X". The circles are drawn about ±0.4–0.8 decades wide on purpose; they are probably too narrow.

## What is derived and what is illustrative

| Part of the figure | Status |
|---|---|
| Region boundaries (0, 1, 2, … layers; federate) | **Computed from J** in the model world by exhaustive minimization over designs (`figures/face_regions.py`), with read effort optimized for every design; rechecked independently by `tools/test_face_regions.py` (random points recomputed by brute force; closed-form boundaries R = h/(E_K − E_{K+1}) at fixed sensing). Not hand-drawn. |
| The *order* "no model → layers → federation as latency rises" and that regions go on to the right with no ceiling | Follows from J in this model; that layers per unit never fall as stakes rise is **not** a theorem once reads are chosen (the reviewer's counterexample, CT33, and the figure's own federation boundary, where layers per unit fall). What is proved is that total judgment spend (upkeep plus sensing) never falls with stakes. |
| The numbers (where 1 → 2 happens, how fast layers accrue: about one more layer per half decade to a decade of R at the high end) | **Illustrative**: they depend on the made-up spectrum (14 modes, 1/f), the sensing price C0/h = 0.003, the federation overhead 20h. Another spectrum shifts every boundary. |
| Layer counts for rhythms | The model has no rhythms. A resonant mode is a degree-2 object: counted by degree it needs two layers; counted as real first-order stores it needs ever more (`scope_checks.py` §4). |
| Circles | **Hypotheses**: order-of-magnitude guesses. Time-scale sources where they exist, R a guess in every row. |

Why this replaces the earlier figure: it had two time scales, so at most two layers, and its "not viable" region came from holding income fixed while stakes rose. Neither is a property of J. **There is no "not viable" region here**: with no ruin constraint binding and no fixed income, every stake level has a best design. A real system can still be non-viable (a shock faster than any response it can afford, CT22/CT25), but that is a statement about its buffer and income, not a region of this map.

**Axes.** x = R = w·Var/h (stakes over upkeep, any currency; **not** size): size enters through R (a bigger system has more at stake and more to pay for upkeep) and through latency, not directly. y = ℓ/τ_fast (how late a reading arrives, over the fastest time scale the system tracks). Colour and the number above the map: layers per unit in the best design. Hatching: federate, meaning split into semi-independent units, each reading locally at lead 0 with its own layers, at a fixed overhead; it is a latency-driven alternative to one central tracker, not a dead end.

Where there is a source it is for a **time scale**, not for R. R is a guess in every row.

What the figure does and does not say:

- It says where one model world's optimal-design regions lie in dimensionless coordinates. That much is a result in a model.
- It does not say any real system sits where its circle is. The placements are hypotheses to be replaced by measurements (the first valid ones would be R from a measured stake, upkeep and variance, and ℓ/τ from a measured delay and fast time scale; GAPS G3).
- Real systems are not this model world: other spectra, noises, prices and overheads would move the boundaries, and a system's units may differ in kind (a circle for the biosphere is a hypothesis about a hypothesis).
- Possibly still misleading: (1) one 1/f spectrum is imposed on every system, though cells, firms and nations have different spectra; (2) the federation overhead is a single number, not derived for any system; (3) a single y axis hides that a system has many fast scales; (4) the image invites reading each circle as a prediction, but only the placement of the *regions* is a computed result; (5) the model counts first-order stores only.
- Regenerate: `nice -n 19 python3 figures/face_model.py` (about 4 minutes on 6 cores, writes `face_tables.json`), then `nice -n 19 python3 figures/face.py` (about 30 seconds, writes `face.png` and `face_regions.json`).
