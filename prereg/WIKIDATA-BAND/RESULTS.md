## 4. Interpretation (separate from the verdicts; THEORY.md v1.4.3a §2.2, §2.5; CLAIM_MAP N16, N17)
1. **Which optimum the test compares against.** The reset-read (exact periodic refresh) cost optimum of formal T8: a local copy refreshed every T costs c/T + WqT/2, versus one shared copy consumed by N pages; only the **signs** of the drift and N effects were predicted, and these hold for every H in T8's power class and for the Poisson (replacement) analogue (Cho & Garcia-Molina 2003). This is neither "the best linear tracker" nor "the Bayes tracker" of noisy sensing: no noisy-sensing quantity (memory time, error, precision effort) enters. Under v1.4.3a §2.2/§2.5 the premise "Gaussian world, or the best linear tracker" governs the noisy-sensing rows; exact reset reads are unaffected (N16; TELEGRAPH-LINEAR-PREMISE P4: telegraph = OU for exact reset reads; the TLP audit marked WIKIDATA-BAND "OK"). So the jump-world single-ln τ Bayes adaptation (N16) does **not** change what this test should have found. Stated in the form HEADLINE-PREMISE-SCOPE asks future preregs to lock: **optimum compared = reset-read cost optimum, premise-invariant (same for linear and Bayes trackers); signs only.**
2. **Switching or event-like?** Event-like, by construction and in the data. A Wikidata fact changes by discrete replacement (item-valued: head of state, CEO, coach) or by discrete dated updates (population, revenue, software version), and the drift proxy is a probability of any change in a window. Two kinds of events show up: (i) **scheduled series** (revenue, software version) with H well below ½ (0.24, 0.12): more regular than Poisson (annual reports, release cadence), with stable per-item rates; (ii) **leadership replacements** (P6 head of government H = 0.58; P6 and P169 item-UNSTABLE, ρ ≤ 0) whose per-item rates do not persist across halves: clustered, rate-modulated switching. (ii) matches N17 (edit gaps are not renewal; rates modulate) on a third data source; (i) is the opposite, periodic case. Neither is a Gaussian world, but because the predictions are reset-read signs, the premise does not bite. Caution: the H estimates use dated statement qualifiers (when a value was recorded as valid), not the change process itself, and 18 of 26 properties had too little dated history.
3. **What the result is about.** The gradient direction appears (β_q > 0 in the pooled fit and in all 4 eligible wikis), but it does not beat item type out of sample by the locked margin, the N effect is null, and the hold-vs-freeze effect is unmeasurable at n = 83. The instrument failed its own known-groups check, for two reasons that are design faults, not theory faults: KG(a) could not pass at n = 40 (the bound is ≥ 0.031/yr even with zero changes), and the drift proxy counts store corrections. KG(b) failed in French (P569 mostly read from Wikidata there), consistent with the lock's note that mode can be policy/template-driven (whether frwiki's infobox templates fetch Wikidata by default was not checked here).
4. **Version note.** Predictions were locked under THEORY v1.2 and the formal layer's T8 (Regime A, one drift rate). v1.4.3a keeps T8 as PROVED (in-model) (CLAIM_MAP F13) and its §3 freeze-or-hold rule; nothing in v1.4.x changes the sign predictions (§1 above). The N² widening and closed-form edges were never tested here.
