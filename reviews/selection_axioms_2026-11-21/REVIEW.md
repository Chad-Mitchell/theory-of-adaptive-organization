The haploid Moran argument is the right theorem, and its algebra is right. Equipartition on a quadratic well is right once the measure is the continuous Boltzmann measure in those coordinates. Chalkley's introduction says Dixit's second-order approximation is exact at zero discount. The constant \(2\sqrt{kw\sigma^2/6}\) is the renewal value for driftless Brownian motion, flow cost \(w x^2\), and a reset to zero; this review re-derived that constant. It was not read off the equations in Dixit or Chalkley. The SA3 algebra is right for the ODE the note writes down. Linearity of expectation does add two mean excesses once they are excesses of one process. The Part 2 hypothesis sentence is byte-identical in `THEORY.md`, `CORE.md`, and the note:

**The law of adaptive organization (hypothesis): strongly selected systems minimize the cost of judgment.**

## Blockers

1. `theory/SELECTION_AXIOMS.md` §7, the Sella & Hirsh bullet (the sentence that calls \(d/(2\beta)\) their load). Sella & Hirsh 2005, equations 11–13, compute a different object. On a landscape with fitness uniform on \((0,1)\), mean fitness is \((\nu+1)/(\nu+2)\) and the fixed-drift load is about \(1/\nu\). They describe that landscape as a one-dimensional Fisher's geometric model and defer the multidimensional model. Replace that sentence with: `Sella and Hirsh derive the stationary weight of fixed genotypes. In their equations 11–13 the fixed-drift load is for fitness uniform on (0,1): mean fitness \((\nu+1)/(\nu+2)\), load about \(1/\nu\). They defer Fisher's geometric model in more than one dimension. SA1b's excess \(d/(2\beta)\) is equipartition on a quadratic cost well under their stationary weight.`

That is the Moran exponent. The diploid exponent is \(2N-1\). A headcount \(N\) in the diploid line moves the fractional excess by about 2, and a factor-of-three kill does not absorb an undefined factor of 2. Replace the diploid sentence and the following sentence with: `For diploid Wright–Fisher with multiplicative fitness within a locus, Sella and Hirsh's approximation gives \(\beta=\alpha(2N-1)\) (their weight \(f^{2N-1}\)). 4. `theory/SELECTION_AXIOMS.md` §4, Assumptions SA2-A–C and Theorem SA2. The linearity step is correct: if the instantaneous excess is \(w x^2+\tfrac12 y^\top H y\), the expectations add with no independence. The assumptions do not yet describe one process whose mean is the displayed sum. SA1 is a substitution chain on a static connected graph. SA2-C is an optimal barrier policy for a moving optimum. Between resets the lumpy coordinate has to be off that chain, the optimum has to be still in the \(y\) coordinates, and a reset of \(x\) has to leave \(y\) where it is. The value \(2\sqrt{kw\sigma^2/6}\) is the zero-discount value (Chalkley 1997; CL19 says so). A positive discount rate changes the band. Weak mutation (\(N\mu\ll 1\)) makes mixing slow, and SA2-B needs the \(y\)-chain to mix inside a redesign cycle; both can fail together, and the note already withdraws the sum when mixing fails, but it never writes the pair of inequalities. The \(d\) in the sum is \(\dim y\), not the SA1b dimension of the whole design. Add to SA2-C, and require them in the theorem: `Discount rate zero. Between redesigns the lumpy coordinate is not a site of the origination–fixation chain. The optimum moves only in that coordinate. A reset changes that coordinate only. The pair \(N\mu\ll 1\) and \(t_{\mathrm{mix}}(y)\ll b^{\star 2}/\sigma^2\) is part of the hypothesis; if no mutation rate satisfies both, SA2 is not claimed. Here \(d=\dim y\).` Until those sentences are in the theorem, row R2's status **PROVED (in-model)** overclaims the process. After they are in, the linearity proof can keep that status.

6. `theory/SELECTION_AXIOMS.md` §4, the sentence "The Dixit occupation density on a cycle is derived in the checks file," and `tools/selection_axioms_checks.py` `triangular_occupation_sample`. The density \(\rho(x)=(b-|x|)/b^2\) on \([-b,b]\) is the right one: the Green function of driftless Brownian motion of variance rate \(\sigma^2\), killed at \(\pm b\), is \((b-|y|)/\sigma^2\), the mean exit time is \(b^2/\sigma^2\), and the regenerative occupation is the ratio. Then \(\mathbb{E}[x^2]=b^2/6\). The checks file does not derive that. It draws from the inverse CDF of the density it assumes, so the Monte Carlo cannot fail if the occupation law is wrong. The CL19 identities that follow from \(b^\star\) are algebraic and do hold (20/20 on rerun, and the renewal arithmetic is right). Replace the sentence with this text: The occupation \(\rho(x)=(b-|x|)/b^2\) is the normalized Green function of driftless Brownian motion killed at \(\pm b\). In the sampler's docstring, replace any claim of a derivation with: `Inverse-CDF sample of the assumed density. Not a derivation and not a path simulation.`
