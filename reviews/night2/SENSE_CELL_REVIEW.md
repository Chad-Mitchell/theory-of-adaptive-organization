## 1. Central question: what do the anchor and the measurement actually refer to?

**Lan et al. 2012** (quoted from the PMC free text and figure legends):
- Model: "The case for Tar is shown (dashed line) with the available energies in SAM and ATP (both at 20% efficiency) marked." (Fig 4 legend). The microscopic model is one receptor dimer with Tar's MWC parameters: "The parameters in ΔE(s, m) are from [9] for E. coli chemoreceptor Tar: K_I = 18.2 µM, K_A = 3000 µM ... only N = 1 is studied here".
- The anchor: "given the high energy release (ΔG0 ∼ 29 kT) from methylation by SAM [26], a modest 20% efficiency (ΔW/ΔG0) leads to a maximum adaptation accuracy of ∼99%, consistent with the high adaptation accuracy observed in E. coli chemotaxis [27]." Ref 27 is Alon et al. 1999 (behavioural). So **99% is a model output for Tar at an assumed 20% efficiency; the efficiency is not measured and 99% is not a FRET measurement.**
- The floor: "All the error-energy points are bounded by a 'best performance' (BP) line, which agrees exactly with (Eq. 8)", over "over 10,000 different models ... with random choices of parameters ... log10(s/K_I) is randomly picked from [−10, 10]" (Fig 4 legend). **The BP line is the lower envelope over parameter sets and over background ligand s.** For large s the model's own error saturates: "ε_c is the saturation error at ΔW → ∞" (Fig 3c; the dashed high-s curves sit at ε/ε0 of order 0.5, by eye). "The deviation from this BP line is caused by the finite saturation error ε_c" (Fig 4 legend).
- ε0: "ε0 ≡ ε(γ = 1)" (Fig 3b legend), the error of the equilibrium case; Fig 4a plots ε/ε0, not ε. **Lan never states a numerical ε0.**
- Lan's own cell measurement: "tryptone broth-grown E. coli K-12 strain LJ110 Δ(cheY cheZ) expressing the CheY-YFP/CheZ-CFP FRET pair ... stimulated ... with 50 µM α-methyl-DL-aspartate (MeAsp)" (Methods); "The relative adaptation accuracy remains unchanged (∼95%)" in starving cells (Fig 5c). That is a **wild-type-receptor (Tar + Tsr + minor) whole-cell reading at a moderate step, by the same group**, ~95%.

**Keegstra et al. 2017** (Europe PMC full text):
- "Step-response experiment on wildtype cells (CheRB+; VS115) ... addition and removal of 500 µM MeAsp" (Fig 1 legend). Strain table: VS115 = ΔYZ ΔFliC with the FRET pair plasmids; receptors are the native mix. "Variability in the recovery time is thus likely to reflect cell-to-cell variability in the ratio between the expression level of CheR and that of the chemoreceptor species responding to ligand (Tar for the experiment in Figure 1)." Fig 2e legend: mean Tar/Tsr ratios 0.4, 0.9, 1.2 across harvest densities, with CV 1.1, 0.5, 0.4.
- "The adaptation precision is defined as its post-adaptational activity level divided by the pre-stimulus level (Π = a_adapted,i / a0,i)"; "the average precision (0.79) agrees well with population measurements (Neumann et al., 2014)". Activity is "normalized to the total response amplitude (Max-Min for each time series)" (Fig 1b legend), so a0 is a fraction of response amplitude, not absolute kinase activity.

**Neumann et al. 2014** (the population measurement Keegstra cites, co-authored by Lan's experimentalists; wild-type LJ110, same strain as Lan Fig 5): "the chemotaxis pathway in Escherichia coli shows increasingly imprecise adaptation to higher concentrations of attractants ... this imprecision results from a gradual saturation of receptor methylation sites at high levels of stimulation, which prevents full recovery of the pathway activity" (abstract). Fig 1B (by eye): MeAsp precision is near 1 at low µM, falls through ~0.85-0.9 near 10-30 µM, and is **~0.80 at ~0.5 mM**, i.e. Keegstra's 0.79 is consistent with the high-stimulus end of this curve. **Meir et al. 2010, abstract, Tar-only cells:** "Quantifiable loss of precision of adaptation was observed at levels of the attractant MeAsp as low [as] 10 µM ... loss of precise adaptation is due to a slowing of receptor methylation as available modification sites become scarce."

**Finding.** Keegstra's Π compares (a) a whole mixed Tar/Tsr array, at (b) 500 µM MeAsp (well above the ~10 µM at which even Tar-only cells lose precision), against (c) a floor that is a Tar-model envelope over s and whose calibration point (99%) is for Tar at a modest step. The cell-level imprecision at 500 µM is attributed by its own authors to **methylation-site saturation, which is not an energy limit**. beta_phys = DG0/dW_BP(eps) asks how far the cell's fuel burn is above the minimum needed *for the accuracy it achieves*; if the accuracy is capped by a non-energetic mechanism, a large ratio says nothing about whether physics binds the design. The Tar-vs-array issue is real (Lan: Tsr "less accurate ... smaller |ΔE(m0)|"; Keegstra: spread from the Tar/Tsr ratio) but **the stimulus-regime mismatch is the larger one and the PREREG missed it** (L7 in RESULTS names only Tar-vs-array).

A diagnostic that needs no further assumption: Lan's Fig 4b (by eye) has the equilibrium (ΔW = 0) model adapt to ~0.80 of a ~0.98 pre-step level, i.e. ε0 ≈ 0.2 for that step; Keegstra's ε is 0.20-0.21. **The scored cell error is as large as the model's no-dissipation error** (in 12% of my Monte Carlo draws below, eps ≥ eps0, so beta is undefined/infinite). It is not on the BP line at all.

## 3. Premise audit (every premise as an interval, nothing hidden)

1. **Bridge premise** (Lan's setpoint error ε on the same footing as the theory's read-noise precision). Lan's ε is a bias of the mean adapted activity, a ratio, not an inverse variance. P = 1/ε (amplitude) or 1/ε² (variance) both lose the noise structure. For H1 this changes θ_eff by a factor 2 either way (the author reported the conservative one). **Unverified, stated, carried.**
2. **Floor definition.** FRAMEWORK §3.11 defines the floor as "kT ln 2 per bit erased, or the relevant nonequilibrium bound". Lan's BP line is a minimum dissipation for a minimal 3-node feedback circuit with Tar-type MWC energetics, an empirical envelope of random parameter draws (agreeing with Eq. 8), and it depends on α = (kR+kB)/2kB, a design parameter. It is **not** a second-law floor on any implementation (that would be lower, so beta against it is larger still). So "beta_phys" here is "fuel ÷ best-circuit minimum for the achieved accuracy". It is a fair reading of "physics binds *this architecture*", not of "physics binds the design" in general. Label it so.
3. **Actual cost.** ΔG0 is the free energy released per SAM transfer; Lan's own efficiency ΔW/ΔG0 = 20% is "modest", assumed, and not measured. Using ΔG0 as cost is an upper bound on the dissipation actually spent per cycle.
4. **ε0** 0.18-0.70 (above). **α** 0.70-0.75. **ε_obs** depends on the reading (§4).
5. **The "beta = 1" edge** corresponds to ε ~ 1e-10 (Monte Carlo 2.5-97.5%: 0.9e-10 to 7.8e-10), unreachable. **beta ≤ 10 is equivalent to a dissipation-limited ε of about 4% (2.3-8.3%).** So H2 is a statement about whether the *dissipation-limited* error of the cell is below roughly 2-8%, which is what Lan's 95-99% figures claim and what 80% at 500 µM cannot speak to.

## 5. Other errors of the same kind found

1. **Stimulus-regime mismatch (new, larger than Tar-vs-array).** §1.
2. **Calibration at the edge of its range.** Method A (ε0 = 0.64) is the high end; Method F's 0.25-0.5 omits the 0.67 reading (if "99%" is 98%). Neither alone spans the figure-supported range (0.18-0.7). Effect: H2 FAIL under every reading of ε0 *for R3*, but R3 is invalid; for R1 the label flips with ε0 (ε0 0.5 → 9.2, 0.18 → 16.5 at ε = 0.05).
3. **Mean of ratios vs ratio of means** (eps 0.203 vs 0.210; the paper's 0.79 is the latter). Small.
4. **Keegstra a0 → α mapping** unnecessary and unverified (amplitude-normalised a0). Take α from Lan's figure.
5. **RESULTS §3.2 / §4 over-reach:** "the published cell data say real cells spend roughly 15-40 times the minimum free energy needed for the accuracy they show" is a statement about R3 only; withdrawn. "Borderline supported" is also too strong in the other direction; the supported statement is §6.
6. **H1 breadth:** with ε0 0.18-0.70, θ_eff over ε in [0.01, 0.05] spans 0.24-0.78 (author: 0.24-0.53), all below the rival θ = 1 but the 0.5 edge is not cleared, so INCONCLUSIVE stands. H1 tests the model line only, not cell data of effort.
7. **H3:** the neuron figure 10^5 (Laughlin et al. 1998) is "not re-verified" by the author and I did not re-verify it either; H3 remains non-discriminating.
8. **Rival never reached:** H2's rival (beta ≥ 100) is not approached by any reading (R3 median 38), so even the author's FAIL does not favour "non-binding physics".
9. **SI not read:** the Lan SI PDF returned a captcha; Eq. 8 derivation details (α as a design parameter, BP envelope construction) are taken from the main text only.

## 6. What the cell result is, calibrated

- **Supported:** Lan's model gives cost rising with the log of accuracy (θ_eff < 1 on that channel, premise: bridge). For this model and architecture, beta_phys ≤ 10 is equivalent to a dissipation-limited error of about 2-8%.
- **Not established:** whether real cells sit in [1, 10]. The closest like-for-like numbers give beta about 5-9 (Tar anchor, circular) and 7-21 (Lan's WT 95%), straddling the 10 edge. The number that looked like a decisive miss (18 [12, 26]) comes from a regime where accuracy is capped by methylation-site saturation, not energy.
- **Open:** H4 (data gap), effort vs stakes at the cell.

## 8. Plain English (public-safe, two sentences)
Bacteria such as E. coli appear to pay for sensing accuracy in a way that physics sets, with energy cost rising slowly (logarithmically) as accuracy improves. Whether real cells run close to the minimum energy a standard model says that accuracy needs is not yet settled: the best like-for-like numbers put them within roughly 5 to 20 times that model minimum, at the edge of the 1-to-10 range we set in advance, and the one measurement that looked like a clear miss was taken where the cell's accuracy is limited by something other than energy.
