## Rulings on PR #64's four questions
1. **Population selection on a common training path as the primary learner: acceptable, with honest labelling.** It is non-circular in the sense the test needs: no cost formula and no theory anywhere in the learner. It is, however, an exhaustive argmin of realized cost on a long path, which makes it close to a noisy numerical evaluation of the discrete-world optimum. Check 1 shows that optimum's Δ is +0.002, so this learner mainly tests whether discreteness, finite paths and long memory spoil the algebra. Sharing one training path across N makes T_g(N) monotone and smooth, which reduces variance; it is not a bias. The ES learner, with independent noisy evaluations, fresh segments and independent runs per N, gives the same Δ (+0.012). The sentence now names the learner and the ES replication.
2. **Margin 0.267: fair as the named rival but generous.** It is the smallest α-movement among the α-dependent §2.5 exponents over this pair (a = 1/α, and θ + 1 − 1/α), so it is a principled rival. A PASS at ±0.267 only says the onset exponent does not move the way 1/α does, though. The informative statement is the CI: |Δ| ≲ 0.12–0.14 at 95% in every PASS run, and ≲ 0.07 in the 48-seed pool. The sentence now says so. **The α = 2 pilot is clean.** It was committed in the lock (`2921bb2`, 6:34 PM ET), lies outside the contrast, ran at a different C, and was used only to set σ. The realized SE was 0.051 (author) and 0.065–0.079 (reviewer, n = 16), so the gate's 1.5× inflation to σ = 0.075 was needed. The pilot could not show that noise is heteroscedastic in α (α = 1.5 is 2–3× noisier than α = 2.5 in every run). Future cross-α gates should pilot at both ends, and this is recorded as a note, not a fix.
3. **Per-α read cost C: no bias; it is the right choice.** In continuous time C cancels exactly. In discrete time, check 1 shows the window exponent stays within ±0.004 of −0.962 once the lone member's T ≳ 25. When T ≲ 15 it is biased: −1.000 at α 2.5 with C 84.3 (T 7), and −0.753 at C 5 (T 3). Equalizing T near 40 therefore removes discreteness bias, with Δ_discrete +0.002 at the author's C. A common C would be worse. At C 2000, α 1.5 needs T ≈ 330, so few reads per path make it very noisy (SE 0.12, INCONCLUSIVE); C 84.3 would put α 2.5 at T ≈ 7, which is biased. Both C values were locked in the prereg.
4. **Replicates with independent code: yes.** ES passes at n = 16, and the grid learner passes at 48 seeds (post hoc). The single INCONCLUSIVE grid replicate at n = 16 is reported, not hidden.

**Post hoc level gap (author: 0.34 vs predicted 0.31).** Reviewer runs give 0.20 (grid, n = 16), 0.27 (ES) and 0.34 (grid, 48 seeds); over ρ ≥ 0.04 only, they give 0.30, 0.30 and 0.33 against 0.31. The discrete exact gap is 0.32. "The level moves with α as the constants predict" holds, but the 0.34 figure is noise-dominated at small ρ, and the sentence now gives the range.
