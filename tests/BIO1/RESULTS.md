## Bottom line
- **The core claim, that memory and adaptation timescales track how fast the environment changes, holds at
  every biological scale checked: cells, immune systems, ants and neurons.**
  - **But it is almost entirely already established.** Kussell & Leibler (2005), Kussell et al. (2005),
    Lan et al. (2012), Mayer et al. (2019), Schnaack & Nourmohammad (2021), Fairhall et al. (2001),
    Wark et al. (2009), Dussutour et al. (2009) and Czaczkes et al. (2015) each state their piece
    explicitly. At these scales the theory's contribution is the unification, not new results. Those
    papers must be credited, and none of this counts as new confirmation of the theory.
- **One clear contradiction (P3a).** Within humans, how long immune memory lasts does not track
  pathogen drift.
  - It tracks the kind of antigen. Non-drifting tetanus and diphtheria toxoids last 11 and 19 years;
    viruses last 50 to more than 200 years.
  - Memory of the fast-drifting 1918 influenza is still present about 90 years later.
  - Rule 2 survives only in its "usefulness" form (P3b): drift erodes how well old memory protects. It
    fails as "the host keeps memory for a shorter time when the pathogen drifts faster."
- **Quantitative form.** The only clean slope found is the neural one: adaptation time is proportional
  to the switching period, a log-log slope of about 1 (Fairhall 2001; Wark 2009).
  - That fits T1's prediction for jump-switching worlds, not its √ (−½) form for diffusive drift.
  - A scale-free adaptation mechanism with no tuning would also give this slope, so it does not tell
    tuning apart from scale-free dynamics.
- **T4 (√N trail fade) is untested.** No data were found that link evaporation rate to colony size with
  independent depositors.
- **Global kill criterion** (primary prediction CONTRADICTS at 2 or more of cells, immune and ants):
  **not triggered.** Only the immune naive form (P3a) contradicts; the immune usefulness form (P3b)
  supports.

## What the theory can and cannot claim at biological scales
1. **Can claim:** its drift-matching rule (T1/T1b, rule 2) is the same principle that biology papers have
   derived or measured separately: switching rates (Kussell & Leibler), immune forgetting (Mayer et al.),
   immune memory cutoffs (Schnaack & Nourmohammad), neural adaptation (Fairhall; Wark) and pheromone
   lifetimes (Dussutour; Czaczkes). Finding the same rule at four scales supports the unification bet.
   Under the theory's own wording, shared math is consistent with a common cause but does not prove one.
2. **Can claim (physics):** in cells, adapting costs free energy, and energy limits speed (Lan 2012) and
   information limits performance (Mattingly 2021). Both are prior art.
3. **Cannot claim:** that host immune memory lifetime is set by drift (P3a contradicts). The stronger
   reading, "every system tunes its memory lifetime to its environment's drift", is false in at least
   this case. Mechanism (long-lived plasma cells, kind of antigen) dominates.
4. **Cannot claim:** the √ exponent (T1b, diffusive drift) at any biological scale. No data matched that
   setting. The one measured slope (about 1) fits the jump form and also fits scale-free dynamics.
5. **Cannot claim:** T4 √N for ants (no data), or a cross-species pheromone law (only anecdotes).
