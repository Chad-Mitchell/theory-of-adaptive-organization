## What was checked and holds

**Clustering.** The identity \(|1-H|^2 S+H^2 N=(S+N)(H-W)^2+SN/(S+N)\) with \(W=S/(S+N)\) expands correctly. For an even spectrum the two-sided integral against \(d\omega/2\pi\) is the integral on \([0,\infty)\) against \(d\mu=(S+N)\,d\omega/\pi\). A design \(H=\sum_{k\le K} g_k\mathbf 1[|\omega|<b_k]\) with free real \(g_k\) is a frequency staircase with at most \(K\) steps, zero above the highest cutoff, and an arbitrary real level on each step. The optimal level on a free step is the \(\mu\)-mean of \(W\), and the cost of that step is the weighted within-step variance. Contiguity is the low-pass constraint itself: the model does not offer non-interval level sets. When \(W\) is non-increasing, those intervals are the contiguous clusters of one-dimensional \(k\)-means on the values of \(W\), except that the last band is held at 0 rather than at its mean. Empty bands make "at most \(K\)" and "exactly \(K\)" the same cost. The reformulation is exact for 𝓑.

**Monge.** The merge formula \(w(X\cup Y)=w(X)+w(Y)+\frac{\mu_X\mu_Y}{\mu_X+\mu_Y}(m_X-m_Y)^2\) is the usual sum-of-squares identity. For \(i\le j\le k\le l\), write \(A=[i,j)\), \(B=[j,k)\), \(C=[k,l)\). Non-increasing \(W\) gives \(m_A\ge m_B\ge m_{BC}\) and \(\mu_{BC}\ge\mu_B\). The factor \(\nu/(\mu_A+\nu)\) increases in \(\nu\), and \((m_A-m)^2\) is larger at the smaller mean, so \(w(i,l)-w(j,l)\ge w(i,k)-w(j,k)\). That is Lemma M. It is Wu's quadrangle for squared error, read on a monotone observation instead of on the point coordinate. Two thousand fresh monotone quadruples had 0 violations. Of 500 non-monotone draws, 265 violated the same inequality, so monotonicity is load-bearing.

The direction is the concave Monge condition in Aggarwal, Schieber, and Tokuyama 1994: \(w(i,j)+w(i+1,j+1)\le w(i,j+1)+w(i+1,j)\). For a gap cost \(f(j-i)\) this is the inequality convex \(f\) satisfies. Concave \(f\) satisfies the reverse, and the note's concave-of-gap trial was non-convex in 599 of 600 draws. Their Corollary 7 is \(2P(\ell)<P(\ell-1)+P(\ell+1)\), proved by the path swap under that condition. Citing the exchange to that paper, and marking Lemma Z as the new piece, matches what the paper contains. The sentence that the mathematics of Lemmas M and X is not new is fair.

**Terminal band and the exchange.** Keeping \(B\)'s mean on \(A\cup B\) costs \(w(j,k)+\int_A(W-m_B)^2\,d\mu\), which bounds \(w(i,k)\). On \(A\), \(W\ge m_B\ge 0\) gives \((W-m_B)^2\le W^2=\text{the slice of }z\). Lemma Z follows.

The paths are indexed correctly. A path with \(K-1\) free bands and one terminal is \((p_0,\ldots,p_K)\); a path with \(K+1\) free bands is \((q_0,\ldots,q_{K+2})\). The crossing time \(t=\min\{t\in\{1,\ldots,K\}: q_{t+1}\le p_t\}\) exists because \(t=K\) has \(q_{K+1}\le\infty\), and minimality gives \(p_{t-1}\le q_t\le q_{t+1}\le p_t\). Each swapped path has \(K+2\) breakpoints, so \(K\) free bands and one terminal. The symmetric difference of the costs is exactly \([c(p_{t-1},q_{t+1})+c(q_t,p_t)]-[c(p_{t-1},p_t)+c(q_t,q_{t+1})]\). For \(t<K\) all four bands are free, and Lemma M applies at \((i,j,k,l)=(p_{t-1},q_t,q_{t+1},p_t)\). For \(t=K\) the terminal band is the one that moves, and the same display is Lemma Z. Passing to near-optimal paths gives \(2E_K\le E_{K-1}+E_{K+1}\) for \(K\ge 1\). That is convexity of \(E^B_K\) on \(K\ge 0\), and of the excess, since \(E^B_\infty\) does not depend on \(K\). Non-increasing \(S/N\) makes \(W=1/(1+N/S)\) non-increasing, including where \(S=0\). The sentence that lists "every Model S spectrum" is a list of spectra for which this band cost is convex. It is not a claim about Model S's causal \(E_K\). Section 6 says so again.

**Model S, \(J\le 2\), every \(K\).** The root count for \(N+\sum_j 2a_j v_j/(a_j^2+x)\) is one root in each interval \((-a_{k+1}^2,-a_k^2)\) and one root in \((-\infty,-a_J^2)\), because the sum is strictly decreasing from \(+\infty\) to \(-\infty\) on each of those intervals and is positive on \((-a_1^2,\infty)\). Ordering the positive square roots gives \(a_1<c_1<a_2<\cdots<a_J<c_J\). The causal factor \(H(s)=1-\prod(s+a_j)/\prod(s+c_k)\) is the standard white-noise form, strictly proper, and its residue at \(-c_k\) has sign \(-(-1)^k/(-1)^{k-1}=+1\). Forty fresh two-mode draws, and the note's 1000, kept the interlacing and the sign.

The covariance in `theory/layers_checks/stores.py` is termwise positive for positive rates, variances, and noise: each OU term is \(v_j b_k b_l/(b_k+b_l)\cdot(1/(a_j+b_k)+1/(a_j+b_l))\), and the white term is \(N b_k b_l/(b_k+b_l)\). Positive residues carry that sign onto the Wiener components, so \(C=\mathrm{Cov}(u_1,u_2)>0\). Orthogonality of the causal error gives \(E_0-E_\infty=\mathrm{Var}\,\hat s=V_1+V_2+2C\). The best scalar readout of store \(k\) gains \((V_k+C)^2/V_k\ge V_k+2C\). Those two lower bounds sum to \(V_1+V_2+4C>\mathrm{Var}\,\hat s\), and the larger of two numbers is at least half their sum, so the better Kalman-rate store gains more than half. The optimal one-store gain is at least that large. Hence \(E_1<(E_0+E_\infty)/2\), that is \(d_1>d_2\).

The same filter is a sum of \(J\) exponential stores, so \(E_2=E_\infty\) for \(J=2\). Any \(K\)-store rule is a causal linear filter, so \(E_K\ge E_\infty\), and a zero readout on an extra store gives \(E_K\le E_2\). Thus \(E_K=E_\infty\) and \(\varepsilon_K=0\) for every \(K\ge 2\), \(d_K=0\) for \(K\ge 3\), and \(d_2=\varepsilon_1\ge 0\). The decrements satisfy \(d_1>d_2\ge 0=d_3=d_4=\cdots\). That is convexity on all of \(K\ge 0\). The step from 0 to 2 is the only nontrivial comparison; the tail is identically zero, not an unproved induction. For general \(J\) the same comparison is Cauchy–Schwarz and yields only a \(1/J\) share. The rerun's worst one-store excess share is 0.3044, and the smallest one-store share is 0.6727, as the note's 0.30 and 0.67.
