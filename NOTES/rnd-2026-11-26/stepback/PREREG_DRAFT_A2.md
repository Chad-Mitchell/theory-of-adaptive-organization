## §1 What the redesign found on paper (A1, A2)
1. **KNOWN (Barrett 2007, PRA 75:032304, 10.1103/PhysRevA.75.032304, Crossref OK this session; Hardy 2001, arXiv quant-ph/0101012, no DOI claimed):** for any GPT composite containing the product states, the product states span a space of dimension K_A·K_B, so **K_AB ≥ K_A·K_B**, with **equality iff the composite is locally tomographic**. Real QM rebit²: K = 10 > 9. Complex QM qubit²: K = 16 = 4·4.
2. **PIM (one line, given 1):** under a per-parameter (dimension) price on composites **of fixed parts**, which the chain already uses as P6 for single systems (qm-selection), local tomography is exactly the set of cheapest composites. So, *if P6 is allowed to price composites*, LT is not an independent premise: it **merges into P6**. That is the useful content of C2, and it needs no test. What the gate read should check: whether extending P6 from parts to composites is legitimate or a new premise in disguise.
3. **The catch (KNOWN):** for rebit (disk, d = 2) parts, an LT composite with a non-trivial continuous reversible interaction does not exist (Masanes, Müller, Pérez-García & Augusiak 2014, JMP 55:122203, 10.1063/1.4903510, and de la Torre et al. 2012, PRL 109:090403, 10.1103/PhysRevLett.109.090403; both Crossref-checked in rnd/entangle/crossref_entangle.txt; under their hypotheses). So at fixed rebit parts, "LT" and "an interaction exists" cannot both hold: the dimension price picks the LT composite, and that composite has no continuous reversible interaction. **A2's question at fixed parts is not separable from the interaction premise I.** Under chain α (which assumes I), the dimension price plus LT selects d = 3 parts, which is the MMPA route the chain already cites.
4. **Reading of the gate read's two readings:** reading 1 (local-only tracker) is LT by definition (agreed); reading 2 (equal-price joint reads) is decided by dimension (agreed), and item 1 shows the dimension ordering *favours* LT at fixed parts, so the gate read's worry that real QM wins on dimension applies only across different parts (rebits versus qubits), not at fixed parts.

## §2 Residual test (only if wanted; small)
**Question.** Does CORE's own heat floor (Q̇ ≥ kT × learning rate), with drift generated by each composite's own reversible group, reproduce the dimension ordering of §1 at fixed parts, or does it add a premium or discount beyond dimension?
- **Worlds (fixed):** qubit parts: complex QM qubit² (LT, K = 16) against two non-LT composites of the same qubit parts if any exist in the BGW 2020 category (Barnum, Graydon & Wilce, Quantum 4:359, 10.22331/q-2020-11-08-359, Crossref OK this session); **A6 decision now:** quaternionic composites are **excluded** unless their K_AB and product-state structure are read from BGW 2020 before the lock and satisfy K_AB ≥ K_A·K_B with product states present; if not, they are outside this GPT setting and are reported as excluded with that reason. Rebit parts are used only as the §1.3 illustration (no interacting LT comparator exists).
- **Stakes (A3):** w × squared error of the tracker's estimate of the full composite state in the group-invariant metric (no conditioning on any holistic direction).
- **Drift and normalisation (fair comparator, A2):** Brownian motion on the composite's reversible group with generators normalised so that each **local marginal** has the same drift rate in every composite (same parts, same local speed). By Schur's lemma the invariant metric on an irreducible module is unique up to scale, so after this normalisation the only free quantity is the composite's dimension and module structure.
- **Prediction (HYP, numeric):** the minimal learning rate at fixed error ε grows with (K_AB − 1) and, per affine dimension, agrees across composites within ±5% at every point of the grid w ∈ {1, 10, 100}, ε ∈ {0.1, 0.01}. A "premium beyond dimension" is a per-dimension ratio outside ±5%.
- **Gates (A5):** (G1) an estimator/Fisher gate: the learning rate for a single qubit under SU(2) drift must match the analytic value for the Gaussian-channel tracker to 1e−6; (G2) optimiser reach: the tracker optimisation must recover Helstrom success (1 + 1/√2)/2 for |0⟩ vs |+⟩; (G3) LT composites must show per-dimension ratio 1 ± 1e−6 against each other (same group, two normalisations); (G4) positive control that is not the hit restated: a hand-built non-LT toy (a classical bit² with one added holistic parity coordinate that drifts at a declared extra rate) must show the declared premium within 1e−6.
- **Prior (A7):** a premium beyond dimension: 0.15.
- **What a hit (premium) would and would not mean:** the heat cost penalises holistic parameters beyond their count; it would not derive LT without the drift normalisation, which is CHOSEN.

## Corrections after gate read 2 (GATE_READ_STEPBACK2.md §§1, 4; appended; earlier text stands, this section governs)
- **1a.** §1.1's source for K_AB ≥ K_A·K_B is **Hardy 2001 §6.15** (arXiv quant-ph/0101012v4, read this session), not Barrett 2007. Barrett 2007 is cited only for LT as an assumption (his Assumption 5) and real QM violating it.
- **1b.** Proof of "equality iff LT", hypotheses stated (finite dimension; product states with factorising probabilities; product effects valid): product effects are K_A·K_B linearly independent functionals; LT ⇔ they separate states ⇔ they span the effect space ⇔ K_AB = K_A·K_B. Quaternionic QM fails the factorising hypothesis and is outside the statement.
